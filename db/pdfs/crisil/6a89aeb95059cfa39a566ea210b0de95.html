Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAAmfs’ rating on BHARAT Bond ETF - April 2033 based on monthly monitoring of portfolios of scheme. The credit score of the scheme is within the benchmark limit.

 

The rating indicates Crisil Ratings opinion on the credit quality of the portfolio of debt securities that BHARAT Bond ETF – April 2033 holds. The rating does not indicate the willingness or ability of the asset management company (AMC) to make timely payments to the funds’ investors. The rating is also not an opinion on the net asset value (NAV) of the funds. The NAV may vary as per market developments. Schemes with this rating are considered to have the highest degree of safety regarding timely receipt of payments from their investments.

 

BHARAT Bond ETF – April 2033, managed by Edelweiss Asset Management Limited (EAML), is an exchange-traded fund launched on December 12, 2022, and initiated by government of India, to cater the borrowing requirements of central public sector entities (CPSE), by pooling investments from retail investors, high-networth individuals and institutional investors. BHARAT Bond ETF - April 2033 has defined maturity date of April 18, 2033. The ETF has a dedicated index, the index for BHARAT Bond ETF – April 2032 is Nifty BHARAT Bond Index – 2033. The index comprises highly rated bonds issued by CPSEs. The weight of the bonds in the indices is based on total outstanding amount of each issuer. The single issuer weight is capped at 15%. The index is rebalanced/reconstituted at the end of each quarter.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings’ assessment of a rated fund’s credit quality is based on the creditworthiness of the fund’s portfolio. For arriving at the rating, Crisil Ratings has evaluated the portfolio of the scheme on fortnightly basis to derive the credit score.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strong credit quality of underlying securities

BHARAT Bond ETF – April 2033, managed by Edelweiss Asset Management Limited (EAML) is an open-ended target maturity exchange-traded bond fund. The investment objective of the scheme is to replicate the Nifty BHARAT Bond Index – April 2033 by investing in bonds of CPSEs/CPSUs/CPFIs and other government organizations,d subject to tracking error. For selection purposes, the scheme takes exposure only in entities that have credit rating of ‘AAA’, which makes the scheme safe. Single issuer weight is capped at 15%, in compliance with the Norms for Debt Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs)/index funds announced by the Securities and Exchange Board of India. In case of excess weight (above 15%) in a single issuer, the excess weight is redistributed among rest of the issuers, proportional to their original weights.

 

For arriving at the credit score of the scheme, Crisil Ratings has analysed the credit quality of the underlying investments, which is a replication of the index constituents. The fund scheme has maintained credit scores consistent with its assigned rating since inception.

 

Risk linked to churn in portfolio

Due to the target maturity date structure, the fund scheme invests in bonds maturing not beyond April 2033. The fund scheme follows a buy and hold strategy where existing bonds are held till maturity unless sold for meeting redemptions. Since the scheme is a passively managed open-ended index scheme, change in investment pattern is normally not foreseen. This results in relatively lower credit quality risk linked to portfolio churn compared to other open-ended active debt fund schemes. Nevertheless, the risk of rating downgrade of underlying investments will remain a rating sensitivity factor. As on September 30, 2026, along with government of India securities, the scheme has invested in 9 CPSEs, of which 8 CPSEs are rated by Crisil Ratings at ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’.

Any other information:
A bond fund credit quality rating indicates Crisil’s opinion on the credit quality of the portfolio of debt securities that the fund holds; the rating does not indicate the willingness or ability of the AMC to make timely payments to the funds investors. The rating is also not an opinion on the fund’s NAVs; the NAVs can vary with developments in the market.

Crisil Ratings has developed a credit quality matrix to assess the aggregate credit quality of a fund’s underlying portfolio. The matrix is a set of credit factors and scores, derived scientifically from the default and transition rates of Crisil’s long-term ratings. The credit factors reflect the expected default behaviour of the securities in the portfolio and the likely weakening in their credit quality. The lower the credit factor for each security, the higher will be its credit quality. The credit factors are applied to the proportion of securities held in each rating category to arrive at the credit score for the portfolio. The rating on the fund is assigned on the basis of the fund’s total credit score. A rated fund needs to maintain, at all times, a credit scores consistent with its assigned rating. If the portfolio’s credit score meets the benchmark score for the existing rating, the rating is retained. If the portfolio credit score has been breached from the benchmark credit score for the existing rating, Crisil Ratings communicates the same to the fund manager of the concerned AMC and may provide time to bring the portfolio credit score within the benchmark credit score for maintaining the existing rating level. If the portfolio composition of the fund is rebalanced to bring the portfolio credit score within the benchmark credit score, the rating is retained. However, if the portfolio continues to breach the benchmark credit score for the existing rating level, the rating is revised to reflect the change in the portfolio’s credit quality.
