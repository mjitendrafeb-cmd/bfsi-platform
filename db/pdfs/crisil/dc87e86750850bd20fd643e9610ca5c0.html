Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Provisional Crisil AA (SO)’ rating to Series A pass-through certificates (PTCs) and ‘Provisional Crisil A (SO)’ rating to Series B PTCs issued by Cedric 07 2026 under a securitisation transaction originated by EarlySalary Services Private Limited (ESPL; ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’), backed by a pool of personal loan receivables.

 

The ratings reflect the credit support available to the PTCs, the credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, the origination and servicing capabilities of ESPL as well as the soundness of the legal structure governing the transaction.

Payment structure

The transaction has a Par with Turbo amortisation structure. The trust settled by the transaction’s trustee, Catalyst Trusteeship Limited, will issue Series A and Series B PTCs to investors in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 82.0% and 6.0%, respectively, of the pool principal at the time of securitisation.

  • The PTCs are issued under a replenishment structure, with a door-to-door tenure of 36 months, implying a legal final maturity of PTCs in October 2029. The initial six months serve as a replenishment period followed by an amortisation period. Maximum maturity of the loans that can be added during the replenishment period must have a scheduled repayment date or maturity date of no later than one month prior to the Series A1 PTCs legal final maturity date.

 

During the replenishment period, Series A PTC holders and Series B PTC holders are entitled to receive timely interest on a monthly basis. Residual collection, including but not limited to, excess interest spread (EIS), prepayments, receipt of overdue principal, receipt of overdue interest and any other amounts received as part of monthly collection or otherwise, will then be used to replenish the purchase of the replenishment pool into eligible receivables, defined as per the selection criteria.

 

During the amortisation period, Series A and Series B PTC holders are entitled to receive timely interest on a monthly basis. Principal repayment to Series A PTCs is expected on monthly basis but promised on ultimate basis — on legal final maturity. Post redemption of the Series A PTCs, principal payment to Series B PTCs is expected on monthly basis but promised on ultimate basis — on legal final maturity. 100% of the residual cashflow will be used for redemption of Series A PTCs. Post redemption of Series A PTCs, 100% of the residual cashflow will be used for redemption of Series B PTCs.

 

The Series B PTCs principal repayments are fully subordinated to the Series A PTCs. Series A and Series B PTCs have a timely interest and ultimate principal structure (TIUP).

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts are supported by external cash collateral, over collateral and EIS. During the replenishment and amortisation periods, on a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to pay the senior expenses and promised interest payments to Series A PTCs and Series B PTCs. On legal maturity date, the cash collateral can be used to pay promised principal to Series A PTCs and Series B PTCs, respectively.

Credit enhancement available in the transaction are as below:

Series A PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of rated Series B PTCs principal amounting to Rs 3.68 crore (6.0% of the initial pool principal), subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 12.00 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).

 

For Series B PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 11.98 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).

 

The external credit enhancement will be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank for and on behalf of the trust.

 

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description  

Strengths:

  • Credit enhancement available in the structure:
  •                  Series A PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of rated Series B PTCs principal amounting to Rs 3.68 crore (6.0% of the initial pool principal), subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 12.00 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).
  •                  For Series B PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 11.98 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).
  • Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
  •                  The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning (number of instalments paid) of 7.4 and consequently, the pool had amortised by 19.1% as on the cut-off date of September 30, 2026.

 

Weakness:

  • High risk profile of underlying asset class
  •                  Unsecured nature of loans and risk profile of underlying borrowers: The pool comprises of personal loans extended to salaried and self-employed borrowers. In a stressed environment, such loans are vulnerable to higher delinquencies. Given the unsecured nature of these loans, recovery prospects from delinquent borrowers are limited. The pool’s collection performance will remain susceptible to risks inherent in this asset class.
  • Geographical concentration
    •                  The top three states (Maharashtra: 20.0%, Karnataka: 15.8% and Telangana: 11.7%) account for 47.5 % of the pool principal.
  • Replenished pool composition
  •                  Potential changes in the pool during the replenishment period; however, these changes are controlled within certain boundary conditions established through well-defined eligibility criteria for new loans added to the pool and replenishment termination events to prevent excessive build-up of risks in the pool.

These aspects have been adequately factored in the rating analysis of Crisil Ratings.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 3.0 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collection from the pool
  • For Series B PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 2.1 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collection from the pool.
  • A sharp upgrade in rating of the servicer or originator

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 2.7 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool
  • For Series B PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 1.9 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool.
  • A sharp downgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator

 

Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating.

Liquidity Strong

Liquidity is strong for Series A PTCs and Series B PTCs given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the currently-estimated base shortfall.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cashflows

The transaction is backed by personal loan receivables originated by ESPL. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (September 30, 2026) are outlined below:

  • The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning of 7.4 monthly instalments paid, which has led to principal amortisation of 19.1% as of the pool cut-off date.
  • The pool is concentrated in terms of geography, with the top three states accounting for 47.5%.
  • The average ticket size for contracts in the pool is Rs 2.24 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 22.7%.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTC investors are taking a direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool-specific characteristics.
  • To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the static pool performance (with information on  90+ delinquency) of personal loans originated by ESPL during the period Q1FY2024 to Q1FY2027 (with performance till September 2026).
  • Crisil Ratings has also analysed the dynamic portfolio delinquencies of ESPL’s portfolio. As of September 2026, the 90+ delinquency for ESPL’s portfolio was 1.4%. Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which includes seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate, loan-to-value, etc. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored risk arising from borrower & geographic concentration in the pool.

Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of excess interest spread in the transaction. Prepayment risk has been assessed based on historically observed levels of prepayments for similar pools.

.

Assumptions:

  • After making the adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfalls in the pool by maturity of the transaction is in the range of 7.0% to 9.0% of pool cashflows.

Monthly prepayment rate of 2.0% to 4.0% has also been applied to the pool cashflows

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

As per indicative transaction terms, the key points on the role of the servicer to be covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

  • The trustee acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint, the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the collection and payment account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.

The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.
