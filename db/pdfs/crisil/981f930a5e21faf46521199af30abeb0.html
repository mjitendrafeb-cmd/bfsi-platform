Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AA+/Stable’ rating to the Rs 350 crore non-convertible debentures and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA+/Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the existing debt instruments of Hinduja Housing Finance Ltd (HHFL).

 

The ratings continue to factor in the expected strong financial, operational and management support to HHFL from the parent, Hinduja Leyland Finance Ltd (HLF; ‘Crisil AA+/Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), both on an ongoing basis and in the event of any distress. This is on account of the strategic importance of HHFL to HLF, and the strong moral obligation of the parent to support the former. HHFL is the housing finance arm of HLF, which holds 100% stake in the company. HHFL is strategically important to HLF as it is the vehicle for growing the home loan business, which is a focus area for the parent.

 

The ratings are supported by HHFL’s stable performance, backed by a growing loan book as well as comfortable capitalisation. These strengths are partially offset by moderate seasoning of the overall loan book, with a majority of it built in recent years. Therefore, ability to continuously scale up the business while maintaining comfortable asset quality and improving earnings remains monitorable.

 

The rating on the perpetual debt instruments reflects the subordinated nature of the instruments and factors in the extent of comfortable buffer consistently maintained by HHFL over the regulatory capital adequacy requirements and high financial flexibility due to majority ownership by HLF. HHFL has maintained cushion of more than 5% on average over the regulatory minimum capital ratio over the past five fiscals. Crisil Ratings believes HHFL will maintain comfortable cushion going forward.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profiles of HHFL and has factored in support from its parent, HLF, because of the strategic importance of the former to the latter. The ratings on HLF’s debt instruments factor in the expectation of strong support to HLF from Ashok Leyland Ltd (ALL) and the Hinduja group given the majority ownership and strategic importance of HLF to ALL and the group.

 

The rating on the perpetual debt instruments reflects their subordinated nature of instruments and factors in the extent of comfortable buffer consistently maintained by HHFL over the regulatory capital adequacy requirements and the company’s high financial flexibility due to majority ownership by HLF. The rating on the perpetual bonds additionally takes into account the restriction on HHFL from servicing these instruments if it breaches the minimum regulatory capital requirement or if the regulator denies permission to the company to make payments of interest and principal if it reports losses.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Expectation of strong support from HLF

HHFL is the housing finance arm of HLF, which holds 100% stake in the former. HHFL is likely to receive strong support from HLF given its strategic importance to the parent, as well as the strong moral obligation of the parent to support the subsidiary.

 

HHFL is strategically important to HLF, as it is the vehicle for growing the home loan business, which is a focus area for the parent. Also, conducting the home loan business through a housing finance company allows for more efficient use of capital. Capitalisation has been supported by regular capital infusion by the parent with HLF infusing Rs 493 crore since inception. The parent plans to hold the single largest majority stake in HHFL over the medium term and is willing to infuse additional capital to support growth requirement over the medium term. HHFL plans to follow a conservative gearing policy and maintain capital adequacy well above the regulatory norms. Both the companies have operational synergies, with HHFL operating through HLF’s branch network, besides its own standalone branches. Furthermore, HHFL also gets to leverage the banking relationships of its parent. The shared name also enhances HLF’s moral obligation to support HHFL.

 

Adequate earnings profile

HHFL has been profitable since inception, supported by controlled credit costs and low operational expenses in the past, as it benefits from operating out of HLF’s branches in a shared model, though the company has its own standalone branches in regions where HLF is not present. Of the 367 branches for HHFL as of March 2026, 134 are shared with HLF.

 

During fiscal 2026, the company maintained a comfortable return on managed assets (RoMA) of 2.3%, albeit lower than 2.8% in fiscal 2025 and 3.3% in fiscal 2024. The moderation was driven by a marginal increase in operating expenses, which rose to 2.7% of average managed assets in fiscal 2026 from 2.5% in fiscal 2025 and 2.3% in fiscal 2024, along with higher credit costs, which increased to 1.1% from 0.9% and 0.7%, respectively, over the same period.

 

Furthermore, during the first quarter of fiscal 2027, RoMA moderated to 1.5% (annualised) on account of reduction in other income (as a percentage of average managed assets) to 2.6% (annualised) in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 from 3.3% in fiscal 2026.

 

The ability to manage asset quality, credit cost and operating expenses and maintain comfortable earnings profile will remain monitorable.

 

Comfortable capitalisation

Networth of HHFL stood at Rs 2,310 crore as on June 30, 2026, against from Rs 2,346 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 1,897 crore as on March 31, 2025. The company’s capitalisation continues to be supported by regular capital infusion by the parent, with HLF infusing Rs 493 crore since inception. The capitalisation is expected to remain comfortable for growth plans over the medium to long term, supported by regular capital infusion and internal accrual. Furthermore, gearing was moderate at 4.4 times as on June 30, 2026, and is expected to remain under 6.0 times on a steady state basis.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses 

Moderate seasoning profile

HHFL started full-fledged operations in July 2016 and operates in the affordable housing segment with products such as housing loans and loans against property (LAP). The company has also been doing portfolio buyouts, which form a small share of its assets under management (AUM). As on June 30, 2026, organic housing loans comprised 59% of the AUM, LAP 38% and portfolio buyouts the balance 3%. Within housing finance, starting fiscal 2024 the company has also begun focusing on the low-income housing segment to target customers in remote locations. 

 

During fiscal 2026, HHFL reported 15% growth in its AUM to Rs 15,937 crore from Rs 13,820 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 10,550 crore as on March 31, 2024). The AUM further rose to Rs 16,157 crore as on June 30, 2026, marking an annualised growth rate of ~6%.

 

While HHFL has adequate vintage, there has been healthy growth over the last 2-3 years, and this new book has lower seasoning. Hence, ability to ensure adequate asset quality while growing the loan book remains monitorable.

 

Asset quality remains a monitorable

Driven by dedicated collection efforts, the 90+ days past due (dpd) remained controlled at 2.3% (on AUM basis) as on June 30, 2026, against 2.2% as on March 31, 2026, and 2.5% as on March 31, 2025. Within segments, the 90+dpd stood at 1.9%, 1.8% and 19.1%, for housing loans, LAP and portfolio buyouts, respectively, as on June 30, 2026. On a two-year lagged basis, given the healthy rise in AUM in the past few fiscals, overall 90+ dpd was modest at 3.3% as on June 30, 2026 (3.4% as on March 31, 2026). Furthermore, gross non-performing assets (GNPAs), on AUM basis, stood at 2.3% as on June 30, 2026, as against 2.2% as on March 31, 2026.

 

Collection efficiency averaged around 98.5% in the 12 months ended June 2026. Also, HHFL has put in place adequate systems and processes to manage risks, which it keeps on tightening and refining regularly based on the feedback from the collections team. It operates on a cluster-based model, wherein each cluster consists of at most eight locations, and up to eight clusters form a region. Each application is run through several checks, including know-your-customer norms, risk assessment, personal discussion and verification of the business, and bank statements and references from existing customers. The income assessment is done by multiple methods depending on whether the income is based on salary or self-employment. The technical and the legal teams verify the quality of the asset that is given as collateral. The final approval for sanctioning the loan is from the credit team. However, given that the portfolio is moderately seasoned so far, ability to manage asset quality over the long term, while scaling up the portfolio, will be monitorable.

Liquidity Strong

HHFL has an adequate asset liability management (ALM) profile with positive cumulative mismatches up to the one-year bucket and negative cumulative mismatches thereafter as on June 30, 2026. As on September 30, 2026, the company had unencumbered liquidity with cash and equivalents of ~Rs 76 crore and unutilised cash credit/working capital demand loan of ~Rs 295 crore, against repayments obligation of ~Rs 296 crore for the next month.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believes that HHFL will continue to benefit from the strong support from HLF.

 

In addition, the rating on the perpetual debt instruments remains sensitive to the capital buffer maintained by HHFL over the regulatory capital requirements, and the rating transition for these instruments could potentially be sharper than those for other debt instruments and bank facilities.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factor

  • Upward revision in the Crisil Ratings’ view on HLF's credit risk profile by one notch or higher

 

Downward factors

  • Decline in support from HLF or material change in HLF's shareholding in HHFL or downward revision in Crisil Ratings’ view on the credit profile of HLF by one notch or more
  • Deterioration in asset quality metrics translating into pressure on profitability and capitalisation

About the Company

HHFL, the housing finance arm of HLF, was incorporated as a wholly owned subsidiary of HLF on April 15, 2015. On September 30, 2015, HHFL received a certificate of registration from the National Housing Bank. The company operates in the affordable housing segment, offering retail products such as home loans and LAP.

 

As on June 30, 2026, the company had presence in 19 states with an AUM of Rs 16,157 crore, up from Rs 15,937 crore as on March 31, 2026.

 

About the parent

HLF, incorporated in 2008, commenced operations in 2010. It was promoted as a captive financier by the Hinduja group’s flagship automobile manufacturing company, ALL. Gradually, HLF ventured into financing of non-ALL vehicles, and forayed into the LAP segment in fiscal 2015. Apart from commercial vehicles (CVs), the company also funds purchase of two- and three-wheelers, tractors, construction equipment and used CVs. The company has also been buying portfolios over the past 4-5 years to diversify its product profile.
