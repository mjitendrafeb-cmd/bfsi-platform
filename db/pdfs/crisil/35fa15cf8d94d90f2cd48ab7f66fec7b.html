Detailed rationale

Crisil Ratings has converted its provisional rating assigned to Series A1 Pass-Through Certificates (PTCs) issued by Ignite 15 to final rating of 'Crisil AAA (SO)' under a securitisation transaction originated by Truhome Finance Limited (Truhome; rated ‘Crisil AA/Stable’), backed by a pool of home loan receivables.

 

The ratings are based on credit support available to PTCs, credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, originator’s origination and servicing capabilities, and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

 

Crisil Ratings has now received executed transaction documents. Deed of assignment of receivables in the process of securitisation was executed in place of the registered deed of assignment. Accounts agreement, servicing agreement and collateral agreement were subsumed into the deed of assignment of receivables in the process of securitisation. However, these changes are not material in nature and do not have any material impact on the credit profile of the PTCs. The other terms of the documents received are in line with the transaction terms envisaged at the time of provisional rating. Hence, provisional rating is converted into final rating.

Payment structure

The transaction has a ‘par with excess interest spread (EIS)’ structure. Transaction has a TIUP structure from default recognition. However, CC? in the structure is being used for monthly expected (to the extent of 87.0%) principal payments.

 

Crisil Ratings has estimated peak shortfalls during the transaction tenure of 3.0-5.0% of pool principal in its base case. These base case shortfalls are further stressed to arrive at the rating of PTCs.

 

The total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of equity tranche and EIS; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, equity tranche and subordination of EIS. The cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest and expected principal payments to Series A1 PTCs, in case of shortfalls in collections from the pool.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure is as below:

  • Internal credit enhancement from scheduled cash flow subordination (assuming zero prepayments) amounting to Rs 311.57 crore, comprising Rs 50.70 crore of equity tranche (13.0% of pool principal) and Rs 260.86 crore of EIS (66.9% of pool principal), provide credit support to Series A1 PTCs.
  • External cash collateral in the structure amounting to Rs 11.70 crore (3.0% of pool principal), which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank and lien-marked in favour of the trustee.

 

Based on Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS, reserve accounts and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) to provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, is commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit support available in the structure
  • External cash collateral in the structure amounting to Rs 11.70 crore (3.0% of pool principal) and internal credit enhancement from scheduled cash flow subordination (assuming zero prepayments) amounting to Rs 311.57 crore, comprising Rs 50.70 crore of equity tranche (13.0% of pool principal) and Rs 260.86 crore of EIS (66.9% of pool principal), provide credit support to Series A1 PTCs.
  • Well-diversified nature of the pool
  • The pool is well-diversified with the loan receivables from 1127 contracts, and the top 10 borrowers contributing to only 2.5% of the pool principal.
  • All the contracts in the underlying loan pool have been current as of the cut-off date (May 31, 2026) with a weighted average seasoning of 12.4 months.
  • Legal soundness of the transaction structure
  • The legal structure envisaged for the transaction entails bankruptcy remoteness of the pool of receivables and credit enhancement from the originator, and adherence to prevailing regulations on securitisations.
  • These are certified through an independent legal opinion from an external legal counsel.

 

Weakness:

  • Interest rate risks
  • The pool has floating rate contracts and more than 80.0% of pool principal are hybrid contracts that have fixed interest rate initially and shall reset to floating rate in the coming months.
  • Underlying contracts are pegged to Truhome’s PLR, while the coupon on the Series A1 PTCs is pegged to 1-month T-bill rate, leading to basis risk in the transaction.

 

These aspects have been adequately factored in its rating analysis by Crisil Ratings.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors:

  • None as pool is rated AAA (SO)

 

Downward factors:

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) failing to cover losses of 3.5 times the estimated base case shortfalls on the residual cash flows of the pool due to weaker-than-expected pool collections or interest rate risks
  • A downgrade in the rating of the servicer/originator
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity: Strong

Liquidity is adequate given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover losses exceeding 1.5 times the base case shortfalls in the pool.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cash flows

The contracts in the pool pertain to HL loans originated by Truhome. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (31-May-26) are outlined below:

  • Pool loans have weighted average seasoning (instalment paid) of 12.4 months during which it has amortised by 3.2%.
  • The average disbursement amount for pool loans was ~Rs 35.7 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 11.8%, and weighted average LTV of 64.4%

 

None of the pool loans has had any overdue as of the cut-off date

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTC investors are taking a direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool-specific characteristics. To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the portfolio delinquency movement, 90+ delinquencies for HL portfolio as of March 2026 is 1.5%.
  • Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which include seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate, and ticket size. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in the risk arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.
  • Prepayment is a form of market risk that will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. Prepayment risk has been assessed based on historically observed levels of prepayments for similar pools.
  • Another form of market risk is basis risk in the transaction, the liability side interest rate (linked to external benchmark) and asset side interest rate (linked to originator’s PLR) are floating. Crisil Ratings has factored adverse interest rate movement resulting in reduction of EIS in its analysis. 

 

Assumptions:

Crisil Ratings has also factored the following assumptions, basis the typical industry characteristics of the asset class and its criteria for rating mortgage-backed securitisations:

  • After making the adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfalls in the pool by maturity of the transaction is 3.0-5.0% of pool principal.
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 1.5-2.5% has also been applied to the pool principal originator’s portfolio analysis

About the company- originator/servicer profile

Incorporated in 2010 under the Companies Act 1956, Truhome Finance Limited (erstwhile Shriram Housing Finance Limited) started its operations in 2011 after obtaining certificate of registration from National Housing Bank. The board of Truhome’s earlier parent company, SFL, had approved the complete stake sale in Truhome to Mango Crest Investments Ltd (an affiliate of Warburg Pincus LLC). Post-consummation of the transaction, the company is owned by Mango Crest Investment Ltd, with 98.55% shareholding as on March 31, 2026.

 

The company provides loans for construction or purchase of residential property and loans against property. It predominantly caters to self-employed borrowers and informed salaried customers in Tier 2 and 3 cities and the focus has been on the lower income segment. It has 224 branches across 16 states in India as on March 31, 2026.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer to be covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

  • The trustee, acting for, and on behalf of, the investors shall appoint the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the collection and payment account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees that shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the waterfall mechanism as per the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner, as per the terms of the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a servicer event of default

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors), as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of servicer’s event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.
