Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the bank loan facilities and debt instruments of Bajaj Finance Limited (BFL).

 

BFL and its subsidiaries, Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL) and Bajaj Financial Securities Ltd are together referred herein as the Bajaj group.

 

The ratings continue to reflect the Bajaj group’s strong business risk profile, given its position as one of India’s largest retail asset financing non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), and expectation of sustenance of strong capitalisation metrics. Further, the healthy earnings profile also underpins the ratings. Besides, the ratings also factor in BFL’s strategic importance to its ultimate holding company, Bajaj Holdings and Investments Ltd (BHIL), and parent, Bajaj Finserv Ltd (Bajaj Finserv). These strengths are partially offset by the company’s sizeable exposure to risky asset classes. Nevertheless, Crisil Ratings believes the group’s continued focus on risk management and data analytics will mitigate inherent asset quality challenges in risky asset segments.

 

As per the draft guidelines released by the Reserve Bank of India dated August 6, 2026 on Non-Banking Financial Companies – (Credit Facilities), which restrict non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) from offering revolving credit facilities and offer only term loans. Crisil Ratings understands some of BFL’s product segments, in their current form, could be impacted if the guidelines were to be implemented as is. Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor the developments in this regard.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the consolidated business and financial risk profiles of BFL and its subsidiaries, BHFL and Bajaj Financial Securities Limited, together referred to as the Bajaj group. Further, Crisil Ratings has factored in its strategic importance of BFL to its ultimate holding company Bajaj Holdings and Investments Ltd (BHIL), and parent, Bajaj Finserv Ltd (Bajaj Finserv).

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Large, well-diversified NBFC

The Bajaj group has emerged as one of the largest retail-focused NBFCs in India. with its two-pronged strategy of building scale and maximising profit. Segments such as mortgage, small business loans and commercial lending are focused on building scale, while consumer durable loans, personal loans, and two and three-wheeler financing are aimed at driving profitability.

 

On a consolidated basis, the assets under management (AUM) of BFL have grown at a CAGR of 27.2% over the last five years to ~Rs 5,09,975 crore as on March 31, 2026. In fiscal 2026, the company’s AUM grew at 22.4% on consolidated and 20.8% on standalone basis.  As on September

30, 2026, the consolidated and standalone AUM stood at Rs 5,84,624 crore and Rs 4,26,879 crore, respectively.

 

The company has recently entered into segments such as new car loans, commercial vehicle (CV) loans, tractor financing, gold loans and loans to microfinance institutions (MFIs). These form a minuscule portion of the overall book on a consolidated basis. As on September 30, 2026, on a consolidated basis, mortgages (including loans against property [LAP]) and home loans accounted for 32% of AUM (of this, lease rentadl discounting accounted for 19%), personal and consumer durables loans (36%), loans to micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) (9%), two and three-wheeler financing (2%), loan against securities (7%) and others (14%), including gold loans, MFI lending and CV and tractor finance, amongst others. Further, the secured book (excluding consumer durables) accounted for more than 60% of the AUM as on March 31, 2025 and March 31, 2026 (61% as on March 31, 2024).

 

BFL is also enhancing its presence through an expanded branch network which will further support growth.

 

Strong capitalisation levels

Capitalisation is strong, with sizeable, consolidated net worth of Rs 1,23,180 crore as on September 30, 2026 (Rs. 113,999 crore as on March 31, 2026). Standalone networth stood at Rs 110,909 crore as on the same date (Rs 103,122 crore as on March 31, 2026).

 

On December 2, 2025, to meet regulatory requirements, BFL divested ~2% of its stake in BHFL through the open market mechanism and gained Rs 1,416 crore (net of selling expenses). Post this transaction, BFL's holding in BHFL has declined to 86.71% from 88.70%. BFL is expected to further reduce its stake to or below 75% to comply with Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) norms. Healthy capitalisation enhances the ability to absorb potential losses on the portfolio; adjusted networth to net non-performing assets (NPAs) ratio was healthy at 62 times as on June 30, 2026 (59 times as on March 31, 2025), on a consolidated basis and at similar level on a standalone basis.  

 

As on September 30, 2026, gearing stood at 3.3 times and 4.1 times on a standalone and consolidated basis, respectively. The group follows a conservative gearing policy. Despite sustenance of healthy growth, adjusted gearing has remained below 6 times for the past five fiscals. At every instance where the gearing inched closer to 6 times, capital raising plans have been initiated and concluded. This is supported by timely and regular equity raise and healthy cash accrual. Hence, the Bajaj group is likely to maintain gearing at less than 6 times on a steady-state basis. Furthermore, on October 1, 2026, the company’s board approved a capital raise of up to Rs. 11,700 crore through a qualified institutional placement and up to Rs. 5,800 crore through preferential warrants to Bajaj Finserv Limited. The proposed transactions remain subject to shareholders and other applicable approvals.

 

Crisil Ratings expects the capital profile to remain comfortable over the medium term supported by regular capital infusion, demonstrated ability to raise capital, and healthy internal cash accrual, providing cushion against asset-side risk.

 

Healthy earnings profile

The earnings profile of the Bajaj group is supported by a large proportion of high-yield businesses and competitive borrowing cost. Additionally, the company has increased efforts to diversify earnings by focusing on various fee-based income avenues, such as existing member identification cards, co-branded credit cards and distribution of third-party products. Nonetheless, the earnings profile continues to be driven by healthy yields, supported by controlled credit cost and operating expenses.

 

On a consolidated basis, portfolio yield moderated to 15.7% in fiscal 2026 from 16.1% in fiscal 2025. further it stood at 15.6% in the first half of fiscal 2027. Additionally, credit cost increased marginally to 1.8% in fiscal 2026 from 1.7% in fiscal 2025, primarily because of accelerated expected credit loss provisioning. However, it improved to 1.4% (annualised) in the first half of fiscal 2027, supported by healthy asset quality.

 

Consequently, return on average managed assets (RoMA) moderated to 3.7% in fiscal 2026 from 3.9% in fiscal 2025, compared with 4.4% in fiscal 2024. However, RoMA improved to 4.1% in the first half of fiscal 2027, supported by lower credit cost and a marginal reduction in the cost of funds

 

Standalone RoMA was also healthy and stable at ~4.7% (annualised) for the first half of fiscal 2027, against 4.4% in fiscal 2026.

 

BFL’s earnings remain susceptible to volatility in credit cost, especially during macroeconomic stress, despite the conservative provisioning policy. While the company maintains a healthy earnings profile, its ability to manage credit cost and cost of borrowings will be key monitorable.

 

Strategic importance to the ultimate holding company BHIL, and parent Bajaj Finserv

BFL is strategically important to the Bajaj group as the company gets significant financial, managerial and operational support from its parent, Bajaj Finserv. BFL is one of the crucial entities of the group's financial services business and its established track record of profitable growth enhances its strategic importance. Crisil Ratings believes BFL will continue to benefit from synergies with the Bajaj group.

 

The financial flexibility of Bajaj Finserv has improved, supported by performance of its operating companies, including insurance ventures. In the unlikely event of BFL requiring group support in an extraordinary situation, BHIL has sufficient liquidity in the form of cash and bank balance and portfolio of quoted investments to address the requirement. In addition, Crisil Ratings believes there is sufficient flexibility inherent in the market standing of various listed and unlisted financial services entities in the group. Crisil Ratings also believes that financial flexibility will be sufficient to support any material requirements of BFL even if the group were to step up its stake in the insurance ventures.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Sizeable exposure on risky asset classes

On a consolidated basis, gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) were comfortable at 1.06% as on September 30, 2026, as against 1.01% as on March 31, 2026, 0.96% as on March 31, 2025, and 0.85% as on March 31, 2024.

 

GNPAs, including write-offs during the first half of fiscal 2027, remained steady at 2.54% as against 2.73% for fiscal 2026. At a standalone level, GNPA and NNPA were comfortable at 1.33% and 0.53%, respectively, as on September 30, 2026, as against 1.27% and 0.52%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Healthy provisions have enabled the company to sustain NNPAs at the current level.

 

However, the company has a large exposure to asset segments such as personal loans and consumer durable loans (including lifestyle and digital loans). These segments, which accounted for ~36% of the consolidated loan portfolio as on September 30, 2026, are vulnerable to economic cycles. Furthermore, BFL offers flexi-loans with a moratorium on repayment of principal across segments, including consumer B2C, SME and mortgages.

 

While the company’s asset quality remains healthy currently, its ability to sustain healthier asset quality metrics going forward while continuing to scale up operations remains a key monitorable

Liquidity Superior

The liquidity profile of BFL on consolidated basis is comfortable with positive cumulative mismatches across all buckets in the asset liability statement as on September 30, 2026. As on September 30, 2026, BFL had unencumbered liquidity (in the form of cash, equivalents and unutilised working capital lines) of Rs 24,951 crores and have static liquidity cover of 1.01 times for subsequent 2 months. The company’s liquidity coverage ratio was 163% as on September 30, 2026.

Environment, Social, and Governance (ESG) profile

Crisil Ratings believes that BFL’s ESG profile supports its already strong credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base, and can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector has no direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment.

 

BFL has an evolving focus on strengthening various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

Key ESG highlights:

  • BFL’s environmental profile is supported by its expanding sustainable finance portfolio. In FY2026, it financed over 14,500 solar equipment installations with disbursements exceeding ~Rs.150 crore, financed more than 3 lakh electric vehicles (EVs) with disbursements of over ~Rs.2,300 crore, and funded over 13.5 lakh eco-friendly devices with disbursements exceeding ~Rs.4,000 crore, contributing to renewable energy adoption, energy-efficient consumption and green mobility
  • Additionally, its subsidiary, Bajaj Housing Finance Limited (BHFL), financed 49 green building projects with total sanctions exceeding ~Rs.6,800 crore during FY2026, supporting the development of sustainable infrastructure. The company has also identified sustainable lending as a strategic priority and linked ESG-related objectives to senior management performance metrics.
  • BFL has set a target to achieve carbon neutrality for Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 2032, demonstrating its commitment to reducing operational emissions and advancing its long-term climate-transition strategy.
  • From a social perspective, it reported a lower-than-peer-average employee attrition rate (~19% versus peer average of ~20%), lower-than-peer-average Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) complaint intensity, and no data-breach incidents in FY2026, reflecting strong workforce and operational risk-management practices.
  • Social performance was further supported by lower-than-peer customer complaint intensity, low stakeholder complaints, extensive customer reach through a network of over 4,000 branches, and high employee training coverage, with ~90% of employees receiving skill-development training and ~99% trained on safety-related aspects.

 

The governance framework is characterized by a 9-member Board with a high proportion of independent directors (~78%), fully independent audit committee, majority-independent Board committees, separation of the Chairman and Managing Director roles, high independent director attendance (~97%), ~100% investor complaint redressal.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believes BFL will remain strategically important to, and continue to receive support from BHIL and Bajaj Finserv. The company will also sustain its healthy financial risk profile.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Downward factors:

  • With the current AUM mix, any significant deterioration in asset quality translating into pressure on earnings profile thereby leading return on managed assets to drop below 2.0% on a sustained basis. This excludes any significant change in AUM mix due to an increase in the secured portfolio that will inherently impact return on assets negatively.
  • Significant increase in consolidated leverage metrics.

About the Company

Set up in 1987, BFL is a subsidiary of Bajaj Finserv (51.30% ownership), the financial services arm of the Bajaj group. BFL has a diversified product suite comprising vehicle loans (two and three-wheelers manufactured by Bajaj Auto), consumer durable loans, personal loans, mortgage loans, small business loans, loans against securities, commercial finance and rural finance.The company has recently started providing loans for new cars and CVs, tractor financing and gold loans.

About the parent

Set up in 2007, Bajaj Finserv Limited is a financial services company, primarily engaged in various sectors such as lending, insurance and wealth management. Bajaj Finserv operates through its subsidiaries, including Bajaj Finance Limited and Bajaj General Insurance offering range of lending and insurance products. Bajaj Holdings & Investment Ltd currently holds 38.41% in Bajaj Finserv Limited.
