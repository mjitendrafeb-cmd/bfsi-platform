Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Provisional Crisil AAA (SO)’ rating to Series A RMBS, issued by ‘Affordable Housing  RMBS Trust II’ under a securitisation transaction originated by Grihum Housing Finance Limited (Grihum; rated ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), backed by a pool comprising  home loans (HL)receivables.

 

The ratings are based on credit quality of the pool backing the transaction, the origination and servicing capabilities of Grihum, credit support available to the PTCs, payment mechanism for the transaction, and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

Payment structure

The transaction has a defined quarterly amortisation schedule with timely interest and timely principal structure. Any additional receivables in the collection and payout account (after servicing the interest and principal obligation for that quarter) can be used to purchase new loans in the pool. %. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulates an average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be atleast 11%. The replenishment is allowed for the first 78 months or until the occurrence of trigger event. During the replenishment period, if the asset cover1 > 1.4, additional receivables can flow back to the originator subject to investor consent.

 

The transaction structure provides for certain trigger events, defined at the time of issuance of PTCs. Should any of the pre-defined trigger events occur during the replenishment period, the replenishment would stop, and the revised amortisation schedule of defined monthly payouts will come into effect. During the monthly amortization period all collected receivables can be used for only redeeming the PTCs and excess cashflows, at the discretion of the investor, would be either used to redeem the PTCs or gets trapped in collection and payout account. There will be no flowback to the originator. The trust settled by the transaction’s Trustee, i.e. Catalyst Trusteeship Services Limited will issue Series A1 to investors in exchange for a purchase consideration equal to 90.0% of the pool principal at par.

 

Investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, overcollateralisation and excess interest spread (EIS). Grihum will continue to service loans in the pool as the servicing agent.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, reserve accounts, overcollateralisation and subordination of excess interest spread (EIS). The cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest and principal payments in case of shortfalls in reserve accounts and collections from the pool to Series A RMBS.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure is as below:

  • Internal credit enhancement from overcollateralisation amounting to Rs 31.14 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of excess cashflow.
  • Internal credit enhancement from excess interest spread; current yield on the PTC is 7.37% and average yield on the initial pool is 13.1%
  • %. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulates an average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be atleast 11%. This aspect has been adequately factored in its analysis by Crisil Ratings.
  • External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 31.14 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal) which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank and lien-marked in favour of the Trustee

 

Based on Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS, reserve accounts and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) to provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, is commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description  

Strengths

  • Structural credit enhancement for Series A RMBS payouts available at this time of issuance:
  • Internal credit enhancement from subordination of principal amounting to Rs 31.14 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of excess cashflow
  • Internal credit enhancement from excess interest spread; current yield on the PTC is 7.37% and average yield on the initial pool is 13.1%. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulates an average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be atleast 11%. This aspect has been adequately factored in its analysis by Crisil Ratings.
  • External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 31.14 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal) which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank and lien-marked in favour of the Trustee.

 

  • Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
  • The weighted average no. of EMI paid in the pool is 30.7 leading to a presale amortization of 10.6%
  • The underlying borrowers have a weighted average bureau score of 758
  • All contracts in the pool are current as of the pool cut-off date. Additionally, since disbursement, 100% of the pool has been ever current. 

 

Weaknesses

  • Prepayment risk
  • The PTC maturity is non-coterminous with the pool tenure and hence for timely repayment to investors on a quarterly basis, there is certain degree of dependency on prepayments in the transaction.
  • Basis risk
  • There is basis risk in the transaction as both pool yield (linked to originator's floating reference rate) and the PTC yield (linked to the MOIS) are floating. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulates an average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be atleast 11%.
  • Borrowers in the underlying pool could come under pressure due to a challenging macroeconomic environment. Headwinds such as increasing interest rate scenario and increasing costs on account of inflation and geo-political uncertainties. These factors may impact the free cashflows of the borrowers and eventually hamper pool collection ratios.

 

These aspects have been adequately factored in its rating analysis by Crisil Ratings.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factors:

  • None as pool is rated AAA (SO)

 

Downward factors:

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) failing to cover losses of 3.5 times the estimated base case shortfalls on the residual cashflows of the pool due to weaker than expected pool collections or interest rate risks.
  • A downgrade in the rating of the servicer/originator
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity Strong

Liquidity is strong given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover losses exceeding 1.5 times the base case shortfalls in the pool

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cashflows

The contracts in the pool pertain to home loans originated by Grihum. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (31-July-26) are outlined below:

  • Pool loans have weighted average seasoning (instalment paid) of 30.7 months during which it has amortised by 10.6%.
  • The average disbursement amount for pool loans was ~ Rs 13.9 lakhs, with a weighted average interest rate of 13.1%, weighted average LTV of 65.3% and a weighted average original tenure of ~15.9 years.
  • None of the pool loans have had any overdues as of the cut-off date.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTC investors are taking a direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool specific characteristics.
  • To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analyzed the static pool performance (with information on  90+ delinquency) of loans originated by Grihum Housing finance Limited during the period FY2021 to Q4FY2026 (with performance till March 2026).
  • Crisil Ratings has also analysed the dynamic portfolio delinquencies of GHFL’s portfolio. As of March 2026, the 90+ delinquency for Grihum’s Home loan portfolio was 1.9%. Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which includes seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate, loan amt, etc. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored risk arising from borrower & geographic concentration in the pool.
  • Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of excess interest spread in the transaction. Prepayment risk has been assessed based on historically observed levels of prepayments for similar pools and prepayment in the portfolio.

 

Assumptions:

  • After making the adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfalls in the pool by maturity of the transaction is in the range of 3.5% to 4.5% of pool principal.
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5% to 1.5% along with additional sensitivities have also been applied to the pool principal.

About the company- Originator/Servicer profile

Grihum is an affordable housing finance company with more than 200 branches across 18 states or union territories, catering to more than 84,000 live borrowers as on March 31, 2026.

 

Grihum’s AUM of Rs 9,178 crore as on March 31, 2026, is geographically diversified with the northern region contributing to ~41%, East ~4%, South ~22% and West ~33%.

 

The share of affordable home loans in AUM was 77% as on March 31, 2026. The average ticket size of the book was ~Rs 11 lakh as on March 31, 2026.

 

In February 2013, erstwhile Magma Fincorp acquired GE Money Housing Finance. Post acquisition, the company was renamed Magma Housing Finance Ltd. Magma Housing Finance Ltd was rebranded as Poonawalla Housing Finance Ltd, post the acquisition by Rising Sun Holdings Pvt Ltd (via Poonawalla Fincorp Ltd) in February 2021.

 

Thereafter, in December 2022, Poonawalla Fincorp Ltd announced its decision to sell-off its entire stake in Poonawalla Housing to Perseus SG Pte Ltd (TPG group). The transaction was completed in July 2023, after receiving the requisite approvals from all the necessary regulatory bodies. Post the acquisition of 98.33% stake by TPG group in the housing finance entity, the latter was renamed Grihum as part of the rebranding exercise in December 2023.

 

Grihum has acquired ~5% holding in RMBS development company limited (RDCL) which is established by National Housing Bank (NHB) with an objective to promote growth in Residential Mortgage Backed Securities market (RMBS) in India.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

As per indicative transaction terms, the key points on the role of the servicer to be covered as part of the transaction documents are as follows:

 

  • The trustee acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint the servicer for collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the collection and payment account, holding the underlying security and carrying out other roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees, which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the waterfall mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer will collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amount in the collection and payment account in a timely manner.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • Occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer:

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of Default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.
