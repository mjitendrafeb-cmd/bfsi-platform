Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA+/Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities and debt instruments TVS Credit Services Limited (TVS Credit; part of the Chennai-based TVS group).

 

Crisil Ratings has also withdrawn its rating of Rs. 225 crore of non-convertible debentures and Rs 300 crore of tier II bond in line with its withdrawal policy (See Annexure 'Details of rating withdrawn' for details). Crisil Ratings has received independent confirmation that these instruments are fully redeemed.

 

On October 29, 2026, TVS Motor Company Limited (TVS Motor) announced a proposed demerger and reorganisation of the TVS group's financial services businesses. Under the scheme, TVS Motor's majority shareholding in TVS Credit is proposed to be transferred to Harita ARC Private Limited (Harita ARC, an unlisted non-operating company) that will be ultimately held by TVS Holdings Limited. (rated 'Crisil AA+/Stable').

Currently, TVS Credit is held by TVS Motor (~85% on a fully diluted basis), with TVS Holdings Ltd. as the ultimate parent. Upon completion of the proposed scheme, TVS Credit will be directly held by Harita ARC, while TVS Holdings will continue as the ultimate parent company with majority stake. Thereafter, board control, oversight, brand-name and other aspects of parentage are expected to be led by TVS Holdings and the same will be suitably reflected in Crisil Ratings’ analytical approach.

This scheme remains subject to regulatory approvals and is expected to come into effect in July 2027. That said, till the transaction is consummated, Crisil Ratings expects that TVS Credit will continue to receive support from its current majority shareholder, TVS Motor and will continue to monitor the developments pertaining to this scheme.

 

The ratings continue to factor in the high strategic importance of TVS Credit to TVS Motor as a key financing arm supporting the latter's vehicle sales.

 

TVS Motors’ volume growth outpaced the domestic two-wheeler sector growth in first half of fiscal 2027 and has also helped it improve its market share in both motorcycle and scooter segments. Volume growth in domestic markets was largely buoyed by the revision in goods and service tax (GST) rates on two-wheelers (2Ws), while exports also witnessed strong demand from African, Latin-American and other markets such as Sri Lanka and Nepal.

 

The ratings on TVS Credit also factor in the steady growth in the company’s scale of operations and strong process orientation that has enabled improvement in asset quality over the past few years. These strengths are partially offset by average, but improving, earnings profile and inherent exposure to risks related to the modest credit risk profile of borrowers.

 

TVS Credit has a diversified presence across asset classes, with gross loans of Rs 34,618 crore as on September 30, 2026, growing by 10.9% year-to date, from Rs 31,216 crore as on March 31, 2026 with 14.9% on year growth. Major asset classes include two-wheeler (27%), consumer durables (24%), personal loans (14%), tractor (12%), used commercial vehicle (CV;9%), used car (8%) and three-wheeler and business loans (4%) as on September 30, 2026.

 

The rating on the perpetual bonds reflects the adequate buffer maintained by TVS Credit over the regulatory capital adequacy requirements, and high financial flexibility enjoyed on account of being a subsidiary of TVS Motor. TVS Credit has maintained a cushion of 2-4% over the regulatory minimum capital ratio in the past few years and Crisil Ratings believes that it will continue to maintain adequate cushion (refer to Crisil Ratings publication 'Criteria for rating hybrid instruments of NBFCs and HFCs' for details on the Crisil Ratings approach for rating such instruments).

Analytical Approach

The rating continues to centrally factor in the strategic importance of TVS Credit to TVS Motor and the parent’s strong moral obligation to support the lending subsidiary, both on an ongoing basis and during distress. The expectation of strong support is based on TVS Credit being a direct subsidiary of TVS Motor, along with a common brand and strong linkages with the TVS Group.

 

In light of the proposed scheme of arrangement, TVS Holdings will become the majority shareholder (with ownership held directly as well as through Harita ARC Pvt Ltd) of TVS Credit. Other aspects such as oversight, board control and source of support – are also expected to get aligned according to the prospective shareholding structure under the scheme.

 

Once the transaction is consummated, the rating will be based on the standalone credit risk profile of TVS Credit and thereafter, its strategic importance to, and expectation of financial and managerial support from its ultimate parent TVS Holding Ltd.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Strategic importance to the parent and expectation of strong support from it

As a captive financing arm, TVS Credit remains integral to TVS Motor plan to increase its market share. TVS Credit finances 17-19% of the parent's domestic sales by volume. It operates through  around ~1,200 strong dealers of TVS Motor Company and more than 3,700 sub-dealers for sourcing clientele. The synchronised planning and sales efforts highlight the strategic importance of TVS Credit to TVS Motor.

 

TVS Motor revenues is estimated to grow by healthy double-digit in fiscal 2027, driven by good demand in both domestic and overseas markets, guarded price hikes and continued premiumisation, also benefiting realisations. Earlier during the last fiscal, the company’s 2W sales registered volume growth of ~23% reaching ~5.7 million units, from 4.6 million units in fiscal 2025, with motorcycle volume growing ~24% on-year, scooters ~27% and mopeds ~7%. Domestic sales witnessed recovery and posted strong growth driven by improved rural demand and supported by GST rationalization benefits leading to reduction in product prices. The scooterisation trend and market position of TVS Motor also benefitted from better demand for ICE (internal combustion engine) vehicles as well as electric scooters. Export volume grew 31% during fiscal 2026 driven by expanding presence of TVS Motor in Nepal, Vietnam and France, and improving economic conditions in key overseas markets such as Africa, Sri Lanka and Latin America. Three-wheeler sales volume also improved by 63% on-year reaching 2.19 lakh units in fiscal 2026 supported by model launches and better volumes in both domestic and overseas markets.

Consolidated operating profitability (excluding TVS Credit) is expected to improve gradually in fiscal 2027 (10.8% in fiscal 2026 compared to 10.1% in fiscal 2025 and ~8.5% in fiscal 2021) due to better operating leverage and product mix as well as gradually declining loss at overseas subsidiaries, which will help offset steep rise in commodity costs. TVS Motor is expected to maintain double-digit revenue growth over the medium term, with its operating profitability sustaining at 11-12%, driven by steady volume growth, calibrated price hikes and improved profits from its overseas businesses. 

 

TVS Motor’s financial risk profile continues to improve, driven by healthy cash generation and prudent capital spend. Debt is expected to rise materially in fiscal 2027, due to refinancing NCRPS redemption proceeds, ongoing sizeable capital expenditure and investments in subsidiaries. Yet due to a healthy increase in operating profits, debt protection metrics will continue to be healthy. This trend is expected to continue over the medium term.

 

TVS Credit continues to receive strong financial, operational and management support from TVS Motor. The total shareholding (direct and indirect) of TVS Motor in TVS Credit stood at ~85% on a fully diluted basis as on September 30, 2026. Over the last two fiscals (2025 and 2026), the parent infused around Rs 455 crore of capital into the company. Of this, Rs 283 crore was infused during fiscal 2025 and Rs 172 crore in fiscal 2026. In fiscal 2026, apart from the capital contributed by the parent – TVS Motor, TVS Credit also received Rs 22 crore by Premji Investment, Rs 4 crore by HDFC Bank and Rs 3 crore by PHI Research Private Limited.

 

Regular capital support like this, in addition to internal accrual, has resulted in adequate capitalisation, with TVS Credit having networth of Rs 6,518 crore as on September 30, 2026.

 

TVS Motor also provides managerial support to TVS Credit. There are four directors from TVS Motor on the board of TVS Credit and many senior management personnel have been with the TVS group for several years. These factors and the shared brand name reflect robust linkages between TVS Motor and TVS Credit; further in Crisil Ratings view, this implies a strong moral obligation on the part of TVS Motor to support TVS Credit.

 

Once the proposed scheme of arrangement is consummated, TVS Holdings – the prospective majority stakeholder for TVS Credit, is expected to provide financial and managerial support including strategic oversight to the company. The nature and extent of support is expected to remain unchanged and will be a key monitorable.

 

Improving scale of operations

The scale of operations has improved significantly over the past few fiscals. Loan book grew ~10.9% year to date to Rs 34,618 crore as on September 30, 2026, against Rs 31,216 crore as on March 31, 2026 (14.9% growth on year). For the three fiscals till 2026, AUM grew at a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of ~14%. Disbursements for first half of fiscal 2027 were 19,612 crore whereas for full fiscals 2026 and 2025, disbursements were Rs 33,017 crore and Rs 26,297 crore. Of the disbursements made in the first half of fiscal 2027, consumer durables formed ~49%, two-wheelers were another ~18% followed by personal loans and tractors at 9% and 5%, respectively.

 

The company had ventured into products such as consumer durables, used CVs, personal loans (mainly cross selling to existing customers), gold loans and business loans to enhance product diversity. As a result, the contribution of two-wheeler loans gradually declined to 27% of the total loans as on September 30, 2026, compared to 42% as on March 31, 2020, even as this segment continues to grow. The company has also expanded its presence to 29 states with no single state having more than 15% AUM concentration.

 

Strong process orientation has enabled improvement in asset quality in the recent past

The company makes significant investments in people, processes and systems to ensure strong origination, underwriting and collection processes. Borrowers are categorised into multiple risk brackets based on their origination characteristics and repayment patterns to focus collection efforts on accounts that show higher propensity for delays. Furthermore, senior management have worked with TVS Motor's dealers closely, establishing relationships and enabling better co-ordination in terms of origination and collections for two-wheeler loans. Strong systems and processes are expected to enable TVS Credit to maintain sound asset quality.

 

On account of a conservative write-off policy, the gross stage 3 assets (GS3) stood at 2.1% as on September 30, 2026 and 2.4% as on March 31, 2026. Including write-offs, GS3 assets stood at 3.0% and 4.6% on the respective dates. The reduction in fiscal 2026 was on account of reduction in write off in the two-wheeler and consumer durable segments and lower incremental slippages at an overall level. However, the tractor portfolio still shows higher delinquencies with the GS3 at 6.6% as on September 30, 2026 compared to 7.3% in fiscal 2026 on account of adverse monsoon in certain geographies. As a result, the management has cautiously curtailed disbursement in the new tractor portfolio resulting in slower growth in overall disbursements.

 

Going ahead, the ability to manage asset quality via efficient processes and controls as the company scales up and diversifies into other product segments, will remain monitorable.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Average, but improving, earnings profile

Overall profitability improved in fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025 compared to earlier years due to reduction in credit costs and yields remaining range bound. Yields on the lending portfolio (AUM) stood at 21.3% for fiscal 2026 and 21.6% for fiscal 2025 (21.4% for fiscal 2024), backed by steady growth and presence in high-yielding segments. Cost of borrowings also remained stable, supporting stability in net interest margins. However, given the small ticket size and large distribution and collection infrastructure, operating costs, while improving, remained high at 8.6% of average total assets for fiscal 2026 and 8.3% for fiscal 2025 (8.9% for fiscal 2024). Credit cost, though moderately high, has corrected to 3.5% during fiscal 2026 from 4.4% in fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2024. Provision coverage ratio for GS3 assets stood at 54% as at the close of fiscal 2026.

 

The company reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 913 crore and return on assets (RoA) of 2.8% for fiscal 2026 against Rs 767 crore and 2.6%, respectively, in fiscal 2025. For the first half of fiscal 2027, PAT was Rs 452 Cr and RoA was at 2.4% (annualized).

 

Going forward, the ability of the company to improve its operating efficiency and manage credit costs will be monitorable.

 

Exposure to risks related to the inherently modest credit risk profiles of borrowers

The borrowers in most of the operational segments have inherently modest credit risk profiles and limited access to bank finance. Industry delinquency levels in the two-wheeler finance business, the company's dominant product, have historically remained high on account of the modest risk profile of the borrower and low resale value of the used asset. Having said that, for TVS Credit, GS3 in the two-wheeler portfolio has historically remained better than peers. Furthermore, the tractor book, which accounts for 12% of the portfolio as on September 30, 2026, is linked to the performance of the agriculture segment and the rural economy.

 

Thus, because of the inherently moderate credit risk profile of this borrower segment as well as higher disbursements across these segments, the ability to maintain asset quality in the near to medium term will remain a key monitorable.

Liquidity Strong

TVS Credit's liquidity profile remains strong. The company's asset liability maturity profile as on September 30, 2026, remains well matched with cumulative positive gap in all maturity buckets. The company had liquidity of Rs 8,035 crore comprising ~Rs 2,985 crore in cash and balance being undrawn bank lines as on October 31, 2026. This is against the debt repayment of Rs 7,241 crore coming due till January 2027. Liquidity profile is also supported by the demonstrated track record and expectation of timely financial support from the parent and access to capital given the company is a part of TVS Venu Srinivasan group.

Outlook Stable

TVS Credit will remain strategically important to the parent and continue to scale up operations steadily over the medium term.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward Factors

  • Upward change in Crisil Ratings credit view on TVS Motor
  • Significant scale up of operations while improving asset quality with GS3 assets stabilising at around 2%
  • Significant and sustainable improvement in profitability, with RoA at around 3%

 

Downward Factors

  • Downward change in Crisil Ratings credit view on TVS Motor
  • Any material changes in the shareholding (below 50%) or support from TVS Motor
  • Gearing deteriorating beyond 7.5 times on continuous basis.

About the Company

TVS Credit, based in Chennai, is a captive finance company and subsidiary of TVS Motor. The company was incorporated in 2008, and was held ~85% by TVS Motor on a fully diluted basis as on September 30, 2026, while the remaining was held by, HDFC Ltd (2%; ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), Phi Research Pvt Ltd (1%) and PI Opportunities (11%).

 

TVS Credit commenced operations as a non-deposit-taking NBFC in May 2010. It has scaled up its business and had a loan book of Rs 34,618 crore as on September 30, 2026. The company currently finances two-wheelers (of TVS Motor), new tractors, used tractors, used cars, consumer durables, used CVs, personal loans (mainly cross selling to existing customers), business loans, gold loans and 3W loans. It caters largely to customers who have limited access to bank financing.

 

Post consummation of the transactions TVS Holdings Limited is expected to become the majority shareholder of TVS Credit, with ownership held directly as well as through Harita ARC (instead of TVS Motor). Upon the scheme becoming effective, Harita ARC will issue and allot its fully paid equity shares to eligible shareholders of TVS Motor in proportion to their respective shareholding in TVS Motor as on the record date such that shareholding pattern of Harita ARC will mirror that of TVS Motor. Harita ARC will be listed on stock exchanges pursuant to the scheme subject to the receipt of requisite approvals from statutory and regulatory authorities. The group will undertake a rebranding exercise incorporating the ‘TVS’ brand name in Harita ARC

About the TVS Motor Company Ltd

TVS Motor Company Ltd  was incorporated in 1983 as Indian Motorcycles Pvt Ltd, a JV between the TVS Group and Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) of Japan; the company came out with its IPO in 1984 and the name was changed to TVS-Suzuki Ltd. TVS-Suzuki was the first Indo-Japanese motorcycle venture in India. TVS was earlier manufacturing mopeds in the early 1980's and was the first player to commence manufacturing of 100cc motorcycles in 1984.

 

At present, TVS Motor is the third largest manufacturer of 2w in the world and has global presence operating in Asia, Africa, Latin America and Europe. TVS Motor has four manufacturing units, three in India and one in Indonesia, multiple distribution centres, and offices serve 80+ markets across the world. TVS Motor is strategically expanding its international presence by fortifying its EV offerings and strengthening its distribution network across the world. TVS Motor has footprints globally, including geographies like Middle East, Africa, SE Asia, Indian subcontinent, Latin & Central America.

 

It currently manufactures a wide range of two-wheelers and three-wheelers at its manufacturing facilities at Hosur, Tamil Nadu; Mysuru, Karnataka; and Nalagarh, Himachal Pradesh, with a total installed manufacturing capacity of 74 lakh two-wheelers and 2.8 lakh three-wheelers per annum as on March 31, 2026.
