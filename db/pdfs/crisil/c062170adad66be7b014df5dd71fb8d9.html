Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has converted its provisional ratings assigned to Series A1 Pass-Through Certificates (PTCs) and Equity Tranche PTCs issued by ‘Nico 01 2026’ to final ratings of 'Crisil AA- (SO)' and ‘Crisil A- (SO)’ respectively. The PTCs were issued under a securitisation transaction originated by KrazyBee Services Ltd (KrazyBee; rated ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), backed by a pool of personal loan receivables. 

 

The ratings are based on the credit enhancement available to support the promised PTC payouts, the expected credit quality of the underlying pool, Krazybee’s origination and servicing capabilities, and the expected soundness of the transaction’s legal structure and payment mechanism. 

 

Crisil Ratings has now received the final legal/executed documents for this transaction. Initial Assignment Agreement was executed in place of the Deed of Assignment. Amended and restated trust deed was executed in place of the Trust Deed. Servicing Agreement and Accounts Agreement were additionally executed. However, these are not material changes and do not have any impact on the credit profile of the PTCs. The other terms of the executed documents received are in line with the transaction terms envisaged at the time of the provisional rating. Hence, Crisil Ratings has converted the provisional ratings to final ratings.

Payment structure

The transaction has a ‘par with excess interest spread (EIS)’ structure. However, upon the occurrence of any pre-defined trigger event, residual cash flow will be redirected towards the repayment of Series A1 PTC principal.

 

The trust settled by the transaction’s trustee — Catalyst Trusteeship Ltd — has issued Series A1 PTCs and Equity Tranche PTCs to investors in exchange for a purchase consideration equal to 86.5% and 6.5%, respectively, of the pool principal at the time of securitisation.

 

The PTCs are issued under a replenishment structure, with a total door-to-door tenure of 67 months, implying a legal final maturity of PTCs in March 2032. The initial 12 months serve as a replenishment period followed by an amortisation period. Maximum maturity of the loans that can be added during the replenishment period is capped at 30 days prior to the final maturity date, ensuring alignment with the overall transaction tenure.

 

During the replenishment period, Series A1 PTC holders are entitled to receive timely interest on a monthly basis. Residual interest collections will be utilised to repay Series A1 PTC principal; principal collection will be reinvested into eligible receivables defined as per selection criteria. During the replenishment period, no payments are expected to Equity Tranche PTCs.

 

During the amortisation period, Series A1 PTC holders are entitled to receive timely interest on a monthly basis. Principal payment to Series A1 PTCs is expected on a monthly basis but promised on an ultimate basis — on legal final maturity. Residual cash flow, if any, will be used in the following manner:

  1. If EIS Trigger event has occurred, then 100% will be used to prepay Series A1 PTCs, else
  2. To be paid to the residual beneficiary

 

Equity Tranche PTCs are fully subordinated to the Series A1 PTCs. Once Series A1 PTCs are completely paid, all cash flow will be paid to Equity Tranche PTCs as principal payouts, which is promised on ultimate basis — on legal final maturity.

 

Series A1 PTCs have a timely interest and ultimate principal structure (TIUP) and Equity Tranche PTCs have an ultimate principal structure (UP). There is no interest payable to Equity Tranche PTCs.

 

EIS Trigger event

  • Collection efficiency[1] falling below 90% of billings for two consecutive months
  • Cumulative default rate[2] exceeding 7%
  • Series A1 PTC exceeding 89.0% of performing balance[3]
  • Utilisation of cash collateral
  • Downgrade in the rating of Series A1 PTCs
  • Downgrade in rating by two notches by any rating agency from the existing standalone rating of the originator or seller as of the initial settlement date.

 

[1] Collection efficiency is total collections including advance collections and prepayments

[2] Cumulative default rate is principal outstanding of contracts in 30+ bucket to the aggregate of the principal balance on the assignment date of all the loans added to pool till date

[3] Performing balance is principal outstanding of all loans which are current — not overdue

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts are supported by external cash collateral, over collateral and EIS. During the replenishment and the amortisation period, on a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to pay the senior expenses and promised interest payments to Series A1 PTCs. On legal maturity date, the cash collateral can be used to pay promised principal to Series A1 PTCs and Equity Tranche PTCs, respectively.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction are as below:

  • Series A1 PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of rated Equity Tranche PTC principal amounting to Rs 15.90 crore (6.5% of the initial pool principal), subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 17.12 crore (7.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 28.08 crore (11.5% of the initial pool principal). External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 11.01 crore (4.5% of the initial pool principal).
  • For Equity Tranche PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 17.12 crore (7.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 28.08 crore (11.5% of the initial pool principal). External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 11.01 crore (4.5% of the initial pool principal).
  • External credit enhancement will be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank.
  • Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provides loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTC.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit enhancement available in the structure:
  • Series A1 PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of rated Equity Tranche PTC principal amounting to Rs 15.90 crore (6.5% of the initial pool principal), subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 17.12 crore (7.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 28.08 crore (11.5% of the initial pool principal). External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 11.01 crore (4.5% of the initial pool principal).
  • For Equity Tranche PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 17.12 crore (7.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 28.08 crore (11.5% of the initial pool principal). External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 11.01 crore (4.5% of the initial pool principal).
  •                   Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
  • The contracts in the pool have a weighted average seasoning (number of instalments paid) of 7.8, and consequently, the pool has amortised by 25.1% as of the cut-off date of July 31, 2026.
  • All contracts in the pool are current on the pool cut-off date of July 31, 2026.

 

Weaknesses:

  • High risk profile of underlying asset class
  • Unsecured nature of loans and risk profile of underlying borrowers: The pool comprises personal loans extended to salaried and self-employed borrowers. In a stressed environment, such loans are vulnerable to higher delinquencies. Given the unsecured nature of these loans, recovery prospects from delinquent borrowers are limited. The pool’s collection performance will remain susceptible to risks inherent in this asset class.
  • Geographical concentration
  • Top three states (Karnataka: 16.1%, Tamil Nadu: 13.8%, Telangana: 13.4%) account for 43.2 % of the pool principal.
  • Replenished pool composition
  • Potential changes in the pool during replenishment period; however, these changes are controlled within certain boundary conditions established through well-defined eligibility criteria for new loans added to the pool and replenishment termination events to prevent excessive build-up of risks in the pool.

 

These aspects have been adequately factored in its rating analysis by Crisil Ratings.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 2.7 times the estimated adjusted base shortfalls on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collections from the pool.
  • For Equity Tranche PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 1.9 times the estimated adjusted base shortfalls on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collections from the pool.
  • A sharp upgrade in rating of the servicer or originator.

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 2.4 times the estimated adjusted base shortfalls due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool.
  • For Equity Tranche PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 1.7 times the estimated adjusted base shortfalls due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool.
  • A sharp downgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator.

 

Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating.

Liquidity: Strong

Liquidity is strong in Series A1 and Equity Tranche PTCs given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the currently-estimated base shortfalls.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cashflows

The transaction is backed by receivables for personal loans originated by KrazyBee. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (July 31, 2026) are outlined below:

  • The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning of 7.8 monthly instalments paid, which has led to principal amortisation of 25.1% as of the pool cut-off date.
  • The pool is concentrated in terms of geography with top three states and top 10 cities accounting for 43.2% and 32.3% of the pool principal, respectively.
  • The average ticket size for contracts in the pool is Rs 3.18 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 20.6%.

Rating assumptions

Background:

PTC investors are taking a direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool specific characteristics.

 

To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the static pool performance (with information on 90+ delinquency) of unsecured personal loans originated by KrazyBee Services Limited during the period from fiscal 2021 to the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (with performance till June 2026).

 

Crisil Ratings has also evaluated the disbursements and write-offs on Krazybee’s portfolio. As of June 2026, the 90+ delinquency (gross of write-offs) for Krazybee’s portfolio was 1.5%. Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which includes seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate, loan-to-value, etc. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored risk arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.

 

Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. Prepayment risk has been assessed based on historically observed levels of prepayments for similar pools.

 

Assumptions:

  • After making the adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfalls in the pool by maturity of the transaction is in the range of 7.0% to 9.0% of pool cash flows.

 

Monthly prepayment rate of 2.0% to 4.0% has also been applied to the pool cash flows.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

  • The trustee, acting for and on behalf of the investors, shall appoint the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the CPA for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security, and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction document.
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees, which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the CPA in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default..

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of servicer’s event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.
