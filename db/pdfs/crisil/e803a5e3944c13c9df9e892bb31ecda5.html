Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has converted the provisional rating assigned to Series A residential mortgage-backed securities (RMBS) amounting to Rs 280 crore issued by Affordable Housing RMBS Trust II to a final rating of 'Crisil AAA (SO)' under a securitisation transaction originated by Grihum Housing Finance Limited (Grihum; ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), backed by a pool comprising home loan (HL) receivables and has simultaneously withdrawn the ‘Provisional Crisil AAA (SO)’ rating on Series A RMBS amounting to Rs 0.27 crore based on a request from Grihum and in line with the Crisil Ratings policy for withdrawal of ratings.

 

Compared to the terms of the initial rating, the overall pool principal remains the same at Rs 311.41 crore. However, the total amount of Series A RMBS has reduced from Rs 280.27 crore to Rs 280.00 crore, increasing the internal support available for Series A RMBS. The credit enhancement amount has been revised from Rs 31.14 crore to Rs 31.11 crore. The total support available to Series A RMBS (both internal and external) is higher in the transaction, compared with the terms highlighted at the time of initial rating. The ratings are based on credit support available to Series A RMBS’s credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, origination and servicing capabilities of Grihum, and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

 

Crisil Ratings has now received the final legal/executed documents for this transaction. The changes are supported by requisite documentation and request from the originator and are credit-positive for Series A RMBS as overall support has increased. All other provisions of the executed documents are as per the terms envisaged at the time of the provisional rating.

Hence, Crisil Ratings has converted the provisional rating to final rating.

Payment structure

The transaction has a defined quarterly amortisation schedule with timely interest payment and principal repayment structure. Any additional receivables in the collection and payout account (after servicing the interest and principal obligation for that quarter) can be used to purchase new loans in the pool. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulate average interest rate of 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool, with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be atleast 11%. Replenishment is allowed for the first 78 months or until the occurrence of trigger event. During the replenishment period, if the asset cover1 is more than 1.4, additional receivables can flow back to the originator subject to investor consent.

 

The transaction structure provides for certain trigger events defined at the time of issuance of PTCs. Should any of the pre-defined trigger events occur during the replenishment period, the replenishment will stop, and the revised amortisation schedule of defined monthly payout will come into effect. During the monthly amortisation period, all collected receivables can be used for only redeeming the PTCs and excess cash flow, at the discretion of the investor, will be either used to redeem the PTCs or gets trapped in the collection and payout account. There will be no flowback to the originator. The trust settled by the transaction’s trustee, Catalyst Trusteeship Services Limited, has issued Series A RMBS to investors in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 90.0% of the pool principal at par.

 

Investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, overcollateralisation and excess interest spread (EIS). Grihum will continue to service loans in the pool as the servicing agent.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, reserve accounts, overcollateralisation and subordination of EIS. The cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest payment and principal repayments in case of shortfall in reserve accounts and collections from the pool to Series A RMBS.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure:

  • Internal credit enhancement from overcollateralisation amounting to Rs 31.38 crore (10.08% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of excess cash flow.
  • Internal credit enhancement from EIS; current yield on the PTC is 7.89% and average yield on the initial pool is 13.1%.
  • As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulate average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool, with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be 11%. This aspect has been adequately factored in the Crisil Ratings analysis.
  • External credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 31.11 crore (9.99% of the initial pool principal) which is in the form of fixed deposits with a bank and in favour of the trustee.

 

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS, reserve accounts and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) to provide loss absorption against stressed shortfall in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths

  • Structural credit enhancement for Series A RMBS payouts available at the time of issuance:
    • Internal credit enhancement from subordination of principal amounting to Rs 31.38 crore (10.08% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of excess cash flow
    • Internal credit enhancement from EIS; current yield on the PTC is 7.89% and average yield on the initial pool is 13.1%. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulates an average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool, with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. Minimum interest rate on individual loans to be 11%. This aspect has been adequately factored in the analysis.
    • External credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 31.11 crore (9.99% of the initial pool principal), which is in the form of fixed deposits, with a bank and in favour of the trustee.

 

  • Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
    • The weighted average number of equated monthly instalments paid in the pool is 30.7, leading to presale amortisation of 10.6%
    • The underlying borrowers have a weighted average bureau score of 758
    • All contracts in the pool were current as of the pool cut-off date. Additionally, since disbursement, 100% of the pool has been current. 

 

Weaknesses

  • Prepayment risk
    • The PTC maturity is non-coterminous with the pool tenure, and hence, for timely repayment to investors on quarterly basis, there is a certain degree of dependence on prepayments in the transaction.
  • Basis risk
    • There is basis risk in the transaction as both pool yield (linked to originator's floating reference rate) and PTC yield (linked to the MOIS) are floating. As per the term sheet, the selection criteria stipulates an average interest rate of atleast 13.0% for initial and additional loans in the pool, with interest rate on individual loans ranging between 11.0% and 16.0%. The minimum interest rate on individual loans to be atleast 11%.
    • Borrowers in the underlying pool could come under pressure due to a challenging macroeconomic environment, with headwinds such as increasing interest rate and elevated costs on account of inflation and geopolitical uncertainties. These may impact the free cash flow of the borrowers and hamper pool collection ratios.

 

These aspects have been adequately factored in its rating analysis by Crisil Ratings.

Rating Sensitivity Factors

Upward factors:

  • None, as pool is rated ‘AAA (SO)’

 

Downward factors:

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) failing to cover loss of 3.5 times the estimated base case shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool owing to weaker-than-expected pool collection or interest rate risks.
  • Downgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator.
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating.

Liquidity: Strong

Liquidity is strong given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the base case shortfall in the pool.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cash flow

The contracts in the pool pertain to home loans originated by Grihum. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (July 31, 2026) are outlined below:

  • Pool loans have weighted average seasoning (instalments paid) of 30.7 months, during which it has amortised by 10.6%.
  • The average disbursement amount for pool loans was ~Rs 13.9 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 13.1%, weighted average loan-to-value (LTV) of 65.3% and weighted average original tenure of ~15.9 years.
  • None of the pool loans have had any overdues as of the cut-off date.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTC investors are taking direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected in the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool-specific characteristics.
  • To assess the base case collection shortfall for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the static pool performance (with information on 90+ delinquency) of loans originated by Grihum over fiscal 2021 to the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (with performance till March 2026).
  • Crisil Ratings has also analysed the dynamic portfolio delinquencies of Grihum’s portfolio. As of March 2026, the 90+ delinquency for Grihum’s HL portfolio was 1.9%. Base case shortfall in the portfolio is adjusted based on pool characteristics –including seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate and loan amount. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in risks arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.
  • Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. The prepayment risk has been assessed based on historical levels of prepayments for similar pools and prepayment in the portfolio.

 

Assumptions:

  • After making the adjustments on these factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is 3.5–4.5% of the pool principal.
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5–1.5%, along with additional sensitivities, has been applied to the pool principal.

About the company- Originator/Servicer profile

Grihum is an affordable housing finance company with more than 200 branches across 18 states or union territories, catering to more than 84,000 live borrowers as on March 31, 2026.

 

The assets under management (AUM) of Rs 9,178 crore as on March 31, 2026, are geographically diversified, with the northern region contributing ~41%, east ~4%, south ~22% and west ~33%.

 

The share of affordable home loans in AUM was 77% as on March 31, 2026. The average ticket size of the book was ~Rs 11 lakh as on March 31, 2026.

 

In February 2013, erstwhile Magma Fincorp acquired GE Money Housing Finance. Post acquisition, the company was renamed Magma Housing Finance Limited. Magma Housing Finance Limited was rebranded as Poonawalla Housing Finance Limited, post the acquisition by Rising Sun Holdings Private Limited (via Poonawalla Fincorp Limited) in February 2021.

 

Thereafter, in December 2022, Poonawalla Fincorp Limited announced its decision to sell its entire stake in Poonawalla Housing to Perseus SG Pte Limited (TPG group). The transaction was completed in July 2023, after receiving the requisite approvals from all necessary regulatory bodies. Post the acquisition of 98.33% stake by the TPG group in the housing finance entity, the latter was renamed Grihum as part of the rebranding exercise in December 2023.

 

Grihum has acquired ~5% holding in RMBS Development Company Limited (RDCL), which is established by National Housing Bank (NHB) with an objective to promote growth in RMBS market in India.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer covered as part of the transaction documents are as follows:

  • The trustee acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint, the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the collection and payment account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction document.
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer: The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors), as per the terms of the Servicer Agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.
