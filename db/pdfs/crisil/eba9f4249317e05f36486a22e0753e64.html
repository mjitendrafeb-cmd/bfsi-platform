Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the short-term fixed deposit programme of The South Indian Bank Limited (SIB).

 

The ratings continue to reflect SIB’s adequate capitalisation along with a stable resource profile as indicated by its high share of retail deposits. These strengths are, however, partially offset by improving, albeit modest, asset quality and earnings profile as well as bank’s modest scale of operations marked by high geographic concentration.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profile of SIB.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Adequate capital position

SIB has an adequate capital position for its scale of business. As on June 30, 2026, networth stood at Rs 11,801 crore with Tier-I and overall, CAR of 18.9% and 19.6% respectively (Rs 11,404 crore, 18.8% and 19.7% respectively as on March 31, 2026), supported by equity infusion and rise in positive internal accruals. Bank’s networth coverage for net NPAs improved to 44.2 times as of June 30, 2026 as compared 39.6 times as of March 31, 2026 (12.8 times as on March 31, 2025). Crisil Ratings expects the capital position of the bank to remain comfortable over the medium term. Nevertheless, the bank will need incremental capital to grow at a robust rate and the timely equity raise remains a key monitorable.

 

Stable resource profile as indicated by high retail deposits.

The deposit profile benefits from the stable pool of non-resident Indian (NRI) deposits and retail depositor franchise. The deposit base of the bank witnessed growth of ~15% y-o-y during fiscal 2026, growing to Rs 123,346 crore as of March 31, 2026, which further grew to Rs 125,817 crore as of June 30, 2026, clocking a 11% y-o-y growth. Amongst the total deposits, share of retail deposits (retail term deposits and savings) stood stable at ~93% as of June 2026, as against ~92% as of March 2026 (~92% as of March 2025). NRI deposits formed around 29% of total deposits as on June 30, 2026.

 

During fiscal 2026, average cost of deposits remained rangebound at ~5.4% as compared to fiscal 2025, although improved to ~5.1% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Current account and savings account (CASA) ratio remained range-bound at ~32.98% as on June 30, 2026 as compared to 32.1% as on March 31, 2026 (31.37% as on March 31, 2025). 

 

While the high proportion of retail deposits provides stability to the resource profile, improving share of CASA deposits will play a key role in enhancing the overall deposit profile of SIB.

 

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Improving, albeit modest, asset quality and earnings profile

Under the new underwriting regime post pandemic, SIB made incremental disbursements primarily to corporate borrowers having a credit rating of A or higher, along with selective and conservative disbursements towards granular MSME, whilst increasing exposure towards gold loans and other retail segments gradually. The book thus generated post September 2020, christened as ‘new book’, has performed relatively well in terms of asset quality with its gross NPA at 0.38%, as compared to gross NPA of the old book at 8.85%, as of March 31, 2026.

 

The share of new book to overall is also on a rising trend, as it rose to ~88% as on March 31, 2026. This has resulted in an improvement in overall gross NPA to 1.43% as on March 31, 2026 compared to 3.2% as on March 31, 2025. Further, gross NPA improved to 1.38% as on June 30, 2026 (3.15% as on June 30, 2025). The ability of the bank to ascertain timely recoveries and controlled level of delinquencies from the old book remains a key monitorable.

 

Moreover, Crisil Ratings notes that SIB has also increased its provisioning metrics, as it maintained a provision coverage ratio (PCR; excluding writeoffs) of 81.4% on GNPA as on June 30, 2026, as compared to 79.9% as on March 31, 2026 (71.8% as on March 31, 2025), leading to an improvement in overall net NPA ratio to 0.26% as on June 30, 2026, as against 0.29% as on March 31, 2026 (0.92% as on March 31, 2025).

 

Given the improvement in asset quality profile, with the bank focusing on increasing exposure towards higher rated corporate borrowers, credit costs remained significantly lower at ~0.3% during fiscal 2026 and 0.4% during fiscal 2025 which resulted in an improvement in the overall earnings profile of the bank as it reported net profitability of Rs 1,455 crore in fiscal 2026, as compared to Rs 1,303 crore in fiscal 2025, translating to a return on average total assets (RoA) of ~1.09% during fiscal 2026 as against 1.08% in fiscal 2025. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, on an annualised basis, the credit cost stood stable at 0.23%, while RoA remained stable at 1.06% with PAT of Rs 378 crore supported by expansion in net interest margins and stable operating cost.

 

Going forward, any significant impact on the earnings profile due to any unanticipated slippages thereby impacting credit costs, remains a key monitorable.

 

Modest scale of operations with geographical concentration

SIB operates as a mid-sized bank largely in and around Kerala. With gross advances of Rs 104,368 crore as on June 30, 2026, the bank had a small market share of ~0.5%. The Kerala region alone formed 29% of total advances as on June 30, 2026 (30% as on June 30, 2025), although the bank has an established position in the state. Modest scale and limited geographic reach make operations susceptible to local regulatory concerns, economic environment, and other calamities such as the Kerala floods.

Liquidity Strong

SIB runs a conservative asset liability management (ALM) profile with positive cumulative mismatches across buckets over the next 12 months as of June 2026. The treasury department estimates and closely tracks the cash flow of the next one month – current and next fortnight. The liquidity coverage ratio for as on July 31, 2026 stood at ~144%. In addition, the bank also has access to systemic sources of liquidity

ESG Profile

Environment, social, and governance (ESG) profile

Crisil Ratings believes the ESG profile of SIB supports its already strong credit risk profile.

The ESG profile for the financial sector entities factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has a reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base and can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not have direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment.

SIB has an ongoing focus on strengthening the various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

Key ESG highlights:

  • The bank’s exposure to environmentally polluting sectors is relatively low compared with peers in banking sectors
  • The bank’s Scope 1 and 2 emissions and energy consumption intensities at ~2.3 tCO2e and ~3.5 MWh per employee, respectively is relatively high compared to peers
  • The bank has established an ‘Environmental and Social Management System (ESMS) policy’ to mitigate environmental and social risks from the company’s lending portfolio by integrating environmental and social factors in high value lending and investment decisions. Further, the bank mobilized cumulative green deposits of Rs. 258.37 crore in fiscal 2026
  • The bank’s turnover rate is at 6% and its gender diversity at ~46%, were better compared with the peer average
  • The bank also has loans exposure towards sectors with high positive societal impact, with exposure to micro finance, MSME, agriculture and infrastructure sectors. Further, its customer complaint intensity, at ~0.3 complaints per Rs crore of loans and advances, was in line with the peer average.
  • The bank’s board is categorized by high share of independent directors (60%) on the board and board committees coupled with an independent chairman of the Board. Though share of women on the board was low at 10%. Further, it has a dedicated investor grievance redressal mechanism and has reported 100% redressal of investor complaints during fiscal 2026.

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward Factors

  • Significant deterioration in asset quality metrics and its consequent impact on earnings profile
  • Weakening in capital position with CET-I declining below 9% 
  • Material reduction in deposits

About the Bank

SIB was set up in 1929, is a private sector bank. The Thrissur (Kerala)-based bank had a network of 953 branches and 1,274 automated teller machines as on June 30, 2026. Advances stood at Rs 104,368 crore as on June 30, 2026, with retail, small and medium enterprises, and agricultural advances accounting for 60% of the book. The proportion of corporate loan book stood at 40% as on June 30, 2026, decreasing from 42% as on June 30, 2025. The management intends to focus on highly rated large corporate accounts as share of A and above rated above entities increased to 98.8% (as a percentage of large corporate advances) in June 2026. Deposits stood at Rs 125,817 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs 112,922 crore as on June 30, 2025).
