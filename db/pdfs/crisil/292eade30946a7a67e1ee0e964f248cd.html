Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’ rating on the non-convertible debentures (NCDs) of Rs 20,000 crore of Bajaj Finance Ltd (BFL) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+' ratings on the existing bank loan facilities and debt instruments.

 

Crisil Rating has also withdrawn its ratings on NCDs amounting to Rs 1,000 crore (see Annexure - Details of rating withdrawn for details) as requested by BFL and on receipt of the requisite documentation. Crisil Ratings has received an independent confirmation that these instruments have been redeemed. The withdrawal is in line with the Crisil Ratings policy on withdrawal of ratings.

 

BFL and its subsidiaries, BHFL and Bajaj Financial Securities Ltd are together referred herein as the Bajaj group.

 

The ratings continue to reflect the Bajaj group’s strong business risk profile, given its position as one of India’s largest retail asset financing non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), and expectation of sustenance of strong capitalisation metrics. Further, the healthy earnings profile also underpins the ratings. Besides, the ratings also factor in BFL’s strategic importance to its ultimate holding company, Bajaj Holdings and Investments Ltd (BHIL), and parent, Bajaj Finserv Ltd (Bajaj Finserv). These strengths are partially offset by the company’s sizeable exposure to risky asset classes. Nevertheless, Crisil Ratings believes the group’s continued focus on risk management and data analytics will mitigate inherent asset quality challenges in risky asset segments.

 

As per the draft guidelines released by the Reserve Bank of India dated August 6, 2026 on Non-Banking Financial Companies – (Credit Facilities), which restrict non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) from offering revolving credit facilities and offer only term loans. Crisil Ratings understands some of BFL’s product segments, in their current form, could be impacted if the guidelines were to be implemented as is. Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor the developments in this regard

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the consolidated business and financial risk profiles of BFL and its subsidiaries, BHFL and Bajaj Financial Securities Limited, together referred to as the Bajaj group. Further, Crisil Ratings has factored in its strategic importance of BFL to its ultimate holding company Bajaj Holdings and Investments Ltd (BHIL), and parent, Bajaj Finserv Ltd (Bajaj Finserv).

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Large, well-diversified NBFC

The Bajaj group has emerged as one of the largest retail-focused NBFCs in India. with its two-pronged strategy of building scale and maximising profit. Segments such as mortgage, small business loans and commercial lending are focused on building scale, while consumer durable loans, personal loans, and two and three-wheeler financing are aimed at driving profitability.

 

On a consolidated basis, the assets under management (AUM) of BFL have grown at a CAGR of 27.2% over the last 5 years to ~Rs 5,09,975 crore as on March 31, 2026. In fiscal 2026, the company’s AUM grew at 22.4% on consolidated and 20.8% on standalone basis.  As on June 30, 2026, the consolidated and standalone AUM stood at Rs 5,46,944 crore and Rs 4,00,388 crore, respectively.

 

The company has recently entered into segments such as new car loans, commercial vehicle (CV) loans, tractor financing, gold loans and loans to microfinance institutions (MFIs). These form a minuscule portion of the overall book on a consolidated basis. As on June 30, 2026, on a consolidated basis, mortgages (including loans against property [LAP]) and home loans accounted for 32% of AUM (of this, lease rental discounting accounted for 19%), personal and consumer durables loans (36%), loans to micro, small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) (9%), two and three-wheeler financing (2%), loan against securities (7%) and others (14%), including gold loans, MFI lending and CV and tractor finance, amongst others. Further, the secured book (excluding consumer durables) accounted for more than 60% of the AUM as on March 31, 2025 and March 31, 2026 (61% as on March 31, 2024).

 

BFL is also enhancing its presence through an expanded branch network which will further support growth.

 

Strong capitalisation levels

Capitalisation is strong, with sizeable, consolidated net worth of Rs 1,20,398 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs. 113,999 crore as on March 31, 2026). Standalone networth stood at Rs 108,862 crore as on the same date (Rs 103,122 crore as on March 31, 2026).

 

On December 2, 2025, to meet regulatory requirements, BFL divested ~2% of its stake in BHFL through the open market mechanism and gained Rs 1,416 crore (net of selling expenses). Post this transaction, Bajaj Finance's holding in BHFL has declined to 86.71% from 88.70%. BFL is expected to further reduce its stake to or below 75% to comply with Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) norms. Healthy capitalisation enhances the ability to absorb potential losses on the portfolio; adjusted networth to net non-performing assets (NPAs) ratio was healthy at 62 times as on June 30, 2026 (59 times as on March 31, 2025), on a consolidated basis and at similar level on a standalone basis.  

 

As on June 30, 2026, gearing stood at 3.2 times and 3.8 times on a standalone and consolidated basis, respectively. The group follows a conservative gearing policy. Despite sustenance of healthy growth, adjusted gearing has remained below 6 times for the past five fiscals. At every instance where the gearing inched closer to 6 times, capital raising plans have been initiated and concluded. This is supported by timely and regular equity raise and healthy cash accrual. Hence, the Bajaj group is likely to maintain gearing at less than 6 times on a steady-state basis.

 

Crisil Ratings expects the capital profile to remain comfortable over the medium term supported by regular capital infusion, demonstrated ability to raise capital, and healthy internal cash accrual, providing cushion against asset-side risk.

 

Healthy earnings profile

The earnings profile of the Bajaj group is supported by a large proportion of high-yield businesses and competitive borrowing cost. Additionally, the company has increased efforts to diversify earnings by focusing on various fee-based income avenues, such as existing member identification cards, co-branded credit cards and distribution of third-party products. Nonetheless, the earnings profile continues to be driven by healthy yields, supported by controlled credit cost and operating expenses.

 

On a consolidated basis, the portfolio yield moderated to 15.7% in fiscal 2026, from 16.1% in fiscal 2025, and has marginally improved to 15.9% (annualised) in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Additionally, credit cost has inched up to 1.8% in fiscal 2026, on account of accelerated expected credit loss (ECL) provisioning, as against 1.7% in fiscal 2025, while it had improved 1.3% annualised for the first quarter fiscal 2027, aided by healthy asset quality. Moderation in yield, coupled with slight rise in credit cost, has led to return on average managed assets (ROMA) of 3.7% in fiscal 2026, as against 3.9% in fiscal 2025 and 4.4% in fiscal 2024. Nonetheless, RoMA stood at 4.1% for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, aided by better credit cost and marginally improved cost of funds.

 

Standalone RoMA was also healthy and stable at ~4.7% (annualised) for the first quarter fiscal 2027, against 4.4% in fiscal 2026.

 

BFL’s earnings remain susceptible to volatility in credit cost, especially during macroeconomic stress, despite the conservative provisioning policy. While the company maintains a healthy earnings profile, its ability to manage credit cost and cost of borrowings will be key monitorable.

 

Strategic importance to the ultimate holding company BHIL, and parent Bajaj Finserv

BFL is strategically important to the Bajaj group as the company gets significant financial, managerial and operational support from its parent, Bajaj Finserv. BFL is one of the crucial entities of the group's financial services business and its established track record of profitable growth enhances its strategic importance. Crisil Ratings believes BFL will continue to benefit from synergies with the Bajaj group.

 

The financial flexibility of Bajaj Finserv has improved, supported by performance of its operating companies, including insurance ventures. In the unlikely event of BFL requiring group support in an extraordinary situation, BHIL has sufficient liquidity in the form of cash and bank balance and portfolio of quoted investments to address the requirement. In addition, Crisil Ratings believes there is sufficient flexibility inherent in the market standing of various listed and unlisted financial services entities in the group. Crisil Ratings also believes that financial flexibility will be sufficient to support any material requirements of BFL even if the group were to step up its stake in the insurance ventures.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Sizeable exposure on risky asset classes

On a consolidated basis, gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) were comfortable at 0.96% as on June 30, 2026, as against 1.01% as on March 31, 2026, 0.96% as on March 31, 2025, and 0.85% as on March 31, 2024.

 

GNPAs, including write-offs during the first nine months of fiscal 2026, remained steady at 2.54% as on June 30, 2026 as against 2.73% as on March 31, 2026. At a standalone level, GNPA and NNPA were comfortable at 1.20% and 0.49%, respectively, as on June 30, 2026, as against 1.27% and 0.52%, respectively, as on March 31, 2026. Healthy provisions have enabled the company to sustain NNPAs at the current level.

 

However, the company has a large exposure to asset segments such as personal loans and consumer durable loans (including lifestyle and digital loans). These segments, which accounted for ~36% of the consolidated loan portfolio as on June 30, 2026, are vulnerable to economic cycles. Furthermore, BFL offers flexi-loans with a moratorium on repayment of principal across segments, including consumer B2C, SME and mortgages.

 

While the company’s asset quality remains healthy currently, its ability to sustain healthier asset quality metrics going forward while continuing to scale up operations remains a key monitorable

Liquidity Superior

The liquidity profile of BFL on consolidated basis is comfortable with positive cumulative mismatches across all buckets in the asset liability statement as on June 30, 2026. As on July 31, 2026, BFL had unencumbered liquidity (in the form of cash, equivalents and unutilised working capital lines) of Rs 24,429 crores and have static liquidity cover of 1.08 times for subsequent 2 months. The company’s liquidity coverage ratio was 115% as on June 30, 2026.

Environment, Social, and Governance (ESG) profile

Crisil Ratings believes that BFL’s ESG profile supports its already strong credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base, and can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector has no direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment.

 

BFL has an evolving focus on strengthening various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

Key ESG highlights:

  • BFL has intensified its climate commitments with a goal to achieve carbon neutrality by fiscal 2032. It has been actively integrating sustainability into its products, processes and subsidiary businesses, for a positive environmental impact
  • The Scope 1 and 2 emissions intensity has declined at a CAGR of ~33% from fiscal 2023 to fiscal 2025 standing at ~380 kg CO2e per employee which is relatively better than its peers
  • Furthermore, energy consumption intensity has declined in compounded terms by ~18% over fiscals 2023 to 2025, to ~0.6 MWh per employee with a nominal share of energy consumed from renewable sources (~0.3%)
  • BFL has implemented various energy-efficiency initiatives, including advanced illumination through LED fittings, daylighting, rooftop solar installations and electric vehicle (EV) shuttle services
  • Also, BFL has reported Scope 3 emissions with intensity of ~190 kg CO2e per employee for four categories (including category 1, 3, 5, 6) in fiscal 2025
  • The lending portfolio of BFL is diversified across sectors such as housing, small and medium enterprises, and retail finance, which have a relatively low negative impact on the environment, while having a positive societal impact
  • So as to reduce carbon footprint through offerings, BFL has financed over 6,000 loans for solar panels in the last three years, over 79,000 EVs and over 12 lakh eco-friendly devices in fiscal 2025. It also has financed green building projects through its key subsidiary
  • Its zero-paper initiative resulted in ~95% digital KYCs, 92% E-mandate adoption and ~98% e-Agreements, reducing physical documentation from ~23% to ~13%
  • The share of women in the total workforce has increased by ~2 percentage points from fiscal 2023, standing at ~7% in fiscal 2025
  • BFL reported an attrition rate of ~18% in fiscal 2025, which is amongst the lowest in the sector, and relatively better than its peers
  • Also, BFL’s financial inclusion initiatives include microfinance loans to individuals and small businesses often excluded from the traditional banking system due to lack of collaterals or credit history. Having a presence in 4,263 locations including ~62% in rural /smaller towns and villages, BFL has microfinance and group-based lending operations carried out at 418 branches to serve underserved communities.
  • BFL has also launched affordable housing loans to support homeownership for underserved and low-income communities
  • Furthermore, the company has undertaken CSR initiatives across education, healthcare, livelihoods, skilling and inclusion of People with Disabilities (PwDs), with 213 projects implemented in fiscal 2025
  • The governance structure is characterised by a higher share of independent directors (~70%) on the board, segregation in chairperson and executive positions, presence of a dedicated investor grievance redressal mechanism, and extensive disclosures
  • There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. BFL’s commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given its high share of market borrowings in its overall debt and access to both domestic and foreign capital markets.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believes BFL will remain strategically important to, and continue to receive support from BHIL and Bajaj Finserv. The company will also sustain its healthy financial risk profile.

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward factors:

  • With the current AUM mix, any significant deterioration in asset quality translating into pressure on earnings profile thereby leading return on managed assets to drop below 2.0% on a sustained basis. This excludes any significant change in AUM mix due to an increase in the secured portfolio that will inherently impact return on assets negatively.
  • Significant increase in consolidated leverage metrics.

About the Company

Set up in 1987, BFL is a subsidiary of Bajaj Finserv (51.30% ownership), the financial services arm of the Bajaj group. BFL has a diversified product suite comprising vehicle loans (two and three-wheelers manufactured by Bajaj Auto), consumer durable loans, personal loans, mortgage loans, small business loans, loans against securities, commercial finance and rural finance.The company has recently started providing loans for new cars and CVs, tractor financing and gold loans.

About the parent

Set up in 2007, Bajaj Finserv Limited is a financial services company, primarily engaged in various sectors such as lending, insurance and wealth management. Bajaj Finserv operates through its subsidiaries, including BFL and Bajaj General Insurance offering range of lending and insurance products. Bajaj Holdings & Investment Ltd currently holds 38.41% in Bajaj Finserv Limited.
