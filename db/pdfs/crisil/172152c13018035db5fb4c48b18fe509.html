Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Provisional Crisil A+ (SO)’ rating to Series A pass-through certificates (PTCs) issued by Celebi 2026 under a securitisation transaction originated by Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited (Dvara KGFS; ‘Crisil BBB+/Stable’), backed by a pool of unsecured joint liability group (JLG) loan receivables and micro enterprise loan (MEL) receivables.

 

The rating is based on credit support available to PTCs, credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, originator’s origination and servicing capabilities, and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

Payment structure

The transaction has a Par with Excess Interest Spread (EIS) structure. Dvara KGFS will assign the loan pool to Celebi 2026, a trust settled by CTL Trusteeship Limited (CTL), which will issue instruments to investors in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 90.0% of future pool principal outstanding as on the cut-off date (July 31, 2026).

 

The transaction structure also includes trigger-based turbo amortisation, wherein 100% EIS will be utilised for amortisation of principal of Series A PTCs till tranche maturity, if any of the following events take place:

 

  • If PAR 30 of the Pool exceeds 4.00% of the initial Pool Principal
  • If the rating of the Series A PTCs falls below its current rating of ‘A+ (SO)’
  • If the rating of Dvara KGFS falls two notches below its current rating of ‘BBB+’ by any of the existing rating agencies rating the Originator
  • Any adverse regulatory action is initiated against Dvara KGFS by a competent regulatory authority.

 

Series A PTC holders are promised timely interest on a monthly basis and principal on ultimate basis on the maturity date of the PTCs. Investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, principal subordination and subordination of EIS.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, principal subordination and EIS. On a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest payments in case of shortfall in collection from the pool to Series A PTCs. On the final maturity date of Series A PTCs, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised principal repayment in case of a shortfall in collection from the pool.

The credit enhancement available in the transaction structure for Series A PTC is given below:

  • Series A PTCs are supported by internal credit enhancement subordination, which includes overcollateralisation amounting to Rs 4.83 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 8.90 crore (18.4% of initial pool principal)
  • The PTCs are also supported by external credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 5.80 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal), in the form of a fixed deposit created in the name of the trust.

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers and Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit support available in the structure

−                     External cash collateral in the structure amounting to Rs 5.80 crore (12.0% of pool principal) for Series A PTCs

−                     Internal credit enhancement assuming zero prepayment aggregating Rs 13.73 crore (28.4% of initial pool principal) for Series A PTCs – including principal subordination of Rs 4.83 crore (10.0%of pool principal) and EIS of Rs 8.90 crore (18.4% of pool principal).

  • Seasoning profile of contracts in the pool

−                     The pool has a weighted average seasoning of 9.0 paid instalments and pre-securitisation amortisation of 23.6%

−                     All the contracts in the underlying loan pool were current as of the cut-off date (July 31, 2026).

  • Legal soundness of the transaction structure

−                     The legal structure envisaged for the transaction entails bankruptcy remoteness of the pool of receivables, credit enhancement from the originator and adherence to prevailing regulations on securitisations

−                     These shall be certified through independent legal opinion from an external legal counsel.

 

Weaknesses:

  • High geographic concentration

−                     The pool is geographically concentrated with the top state (Tamil Nadu) and the top 10 districts accounting for 72.8% and 70.9%, respectively, of the initial pool principal.

  • Vulnerability to sociopolitical risks

−                     Borrower cash flows in the unsecured MEL segment remain susceptible to risks related to sociopolitical issues and policy or regulatory changes. These events can disrupt loan repayments of underlying borrowers.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A PTC: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 1.9 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool owing to sustained healthy collection from the pool
  • Upgrade in rating of the servicer or originator

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A PTCs: Credit enhancement in the structure failing to cover 1.7 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall owing to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool
  • Sharp downgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity Strong

Liquidity is strong given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the current estimated base shortfall.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cash flow

The transaction is backed by JLG and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS. The key pool characteristics are outlined as follows:

  • The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning of 9.0 paid monthly instalments, which has led to principal amortisation of 23.6% as on the pool cut-off date
  • The pool is moderately concentrated in terms of geography with the top three states and the top 10 districts accounting for 86.8% and 70.9%, respectively, of the initial pool principal
  • The average ticket size for contracts in the pool is Rs 1.01 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 26.9%
  • All the contracts in the underlying loan pool were current as on the cut-off date

Rating assumptions

Background

PTC investors are taking direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfall expected on the portfolio, which is adjusted for pool-specific characteristics.

 

To assess the base case collection shortfall for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the portfolio delinquency movement (with information on 30+ and 90+ delinquencies) of JLG and MEL loans until April 2026. Crisil Ratings has also analysed delinquency performance in various geographies. As of June 2026, the 30+ delinquency for the MEL portfolio of Dvara KGFS was 5.1% and 90+ delinquency was 3.3%. The 30+ delinquency for the JLG loan portfolio was 7.5% and 90+ delinquency was 7.1%.

 

Base case shortfall on the portfolio is adjusted based on pool characteristics, which includes seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate and geography (state/district). Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in risks arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.

 

Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. The prepayment risk has been assessed based on historical levels of prepayment for similar pools.

 

Assumptions 

  • After making the adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is in the range of 11.0% to 13.0% of pool cash flow
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5% to 1.5% has been applied to the pool cash flow

About the company - Originator/Servicer profile

Dvara KGFS was set up in fiscal 2008 by Dvara Trust for extending unsecured and secured loans to rural areas in the country. The company was founded by Bindu Ananth and Nachiket Mor. Dvara KGFS focuses on extending multiple financial products for the lending, savings and insurance requirements of individuals in rural areas. The company positions itself as a rural wealth manager providing loans and financial products to customers. It largely extends JLG and unsecured enterprise loans, which account for more than 95% of total loans. The JLG loans have ticket size of up to Rs 50,000 and enterprise loans up to Rs 5 lakh. The company lends at interest rate of 24.5–27.75% for JLG loans and 26–29% for enterprise loans.

 

In fiscal 2019, the business correspondents and corporate agency business of the IFMR Rural Channels and Services Pvt Ltd (holding company of Dvara KGFS) and IFMR Holdings Pvt Ltd (ultimate holding company) were amalgamated with the company.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

As per indicative transaction terms, the key points on the role of the servicer to be covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

 

  • The trustee acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the collection and payment account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees, which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the waterfall mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors), as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.

Performance of outstanding rated transactions

Crisil Ratings has ratings outstanding on multiple securitisation transactions backed by JLG and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS. For the pools with amortisation higher than 40.0%, cumulative collection ratios ranged from 95.2% to 99.1% as of July 2026 payouts.
