Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Crisil AA+*/Stable’ ratings on the debt instruments of ICICI Bank Limited (ICICI Bank). 

Crisil Ratings has also withdrawn its rating on Rs 40.4 crore bonds (See ‘Annexure - Details of rating withdrawn' for details) in line with its withdrawal policy. Crisil Ratings has received independent verification that these instruments are fully redeemed.

 

The ratings continue to reflect the bank’s healthy capitalisation, strong market position, comfortable resource profile and stable earnings and asset quality.

 

On September 30, 2026, the bank had gross advances and deposits of Rs 18.5 lakh crore and Rs 20.5 lakh crore, respectively, on a standalone basis. Pursuant to Reserve Bank of India’s swap facility, the bank mobilised gross foreign currency non-resident (FCNR) deposits of around $18 billion. Including this, the bank’s deposits grew by 27.1% as compared with 17.0% excluding the FCNR deposits on year-on-year basis. On the assets side, the overall loan portfolio grew by 30.0% on-year basis as against to 24.1% excluding loans provided by international branches of the bank against FCNR (B) deposits. Furthermore, it remains among the three domestic systemically-important banks (D-SIBs), as classified by Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

 

On the same date, the bank’s reported networth was Rs 3.5 lakh crore (standalone) and Rs 3.9 lakh crore (consolidated, excluding minority interest) whereas overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR, standalone) was 16.7% (including profit in the first half of fiscal 2027). 

 

Asset quality has also improved over the years and remained stable, with gross and net non-performing assets (gross non-performing assets [GNPAs] and net non-performing assets [NNPAs]) of 1.3% and 0.3%, respectively, as on September 30, 2026. The bank’s ability to maintain this metric at sound levels, alongside sustained growth, will remain monitorable. 

 

Lastly, profitability remained stable with a consolidated net profit of Rs 31,773 crore for half year ended September 30, 2026, as compared with Rs 26,915 crore for the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Healthy capitalisation

Networth was sizeable at Rs 3.5 lakh crore while tier I capital adequacy ratio (CAR) was 16.0% and overall CAR was 16.7%, as on September 30, 2026, substantiating the bank’s healthy capital position. In fiscal 2021, the bank raised equity capital of Rs 15,000 crore under QIP (qualified institutional placement) to further strengthen its capital position. Subsequently, consistent healthy internal accretion coupled with controlled improving asset quality, networth coverage for net non-performing assets (NPAs) (non-performing assets) was strong at 61.2 times as on September 30, 2026 (11.5 times as on March 31, 2020).

 

Moreover, the bank has adequate flexibility to raise resources through sale of stake in subsidiaries (more than Rs 16,000 crore raised in the past decade). Backed by healthy cash accrual and demonstrated ability to raise capital, the bank is likely to maintain strong capitalisation to support overall credit risk profile and adequately cover for asset-side risks while pursuing credit growth over the medium term.

 

Strong market position

ICICI Bank remains among the largest private sector banks in India with consolidated asset base of Rs 32.9 lakh crore as on September 30, 2026 (standalone asset base of Rs 27.3 lakh crore). With gross advances and domestic deposits of Rs 18.5 lakh crore and Rs 20.5 lakh crore, respectively, as on September 30, 2026, on standalone basis, it is also one of the three banks that has been classified as domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs) by RBI, highlighting its significance to the overall financial system.

 

Across various businesses within the ICICI group, size, scale and positioning have been key strengths. As a leading full-service universal banking group with pan-India footprint, the group has strong presence in life and general insurance, asset management, private equity and retail broking. Wide geographical spread is reflected in its network of 7,656 branches as on September 30, 2026.

 

The bank’s established market position shall continue to benefit from its strategy of expanding penetration and coverage in the domestic lending space, particularly in retail lending, while maintaining strong liability franchise.

 

Comfortable resource profile

In the scheme of growth, the bank’s resource franchise has also remained comfortable - supported by high proportion of low-cost current account and savings account (CASA) deposits. On September 30, 2026, the bank reported total domestic deposit base of Rs 20.5 lakh crore, which marks a healthy on-year growth of 27% over September 2025. As a proportion of total deposits, CASA, though moderated, remained healthy at 36.1% as on September 30, 2026, as against 40.9% as on September 30, 2025. This was because of one-time mobilisation of FCNR term deposits pursuant to recent RBI regulations. Adjacently, the cost of deposits reduced to 4.5% in the first half of 2027 vis-à-vis 4.8% in the first half of 2026 and 4.6% for full fiscal 2026 pursuant to slower repricing of deposits on back of lower repo rates. Furthermore, retail deposits form a significant part of the total deposits and provide stability to the resource profile. With large mobilisation of FCNR term deposits and expected policy tightening with respect to repo rates, the bank’s cost of deposits may inch up though still remain range bound.

 

Wide branch network and strong focus on digital banking shall help maintain higher-than-industry-average CASA levels and competitive funding cost over the medium term.

 

Stable earnings and asset quality

The bank’s profitability has remained stable over the recent past, reflected in a 3-year average return on assets (RoA) of 2.3% through fiscal 2026. For fiscal 2026, the bank reported a net profit of Rs 50,148 crore vis a vis Rs 47,227 crore for the previous fiscal. RoA for the respective fiscals was 2.2% and 2.4%. Stable interest margins and other income with controlled operating and provisioning expenses, have contributed to the overall stability of the earnings profile.

 

Asset quality of the bank has also witnessed gradual improvement over the past few years and, remains stable with a GNPA (as a percentage of gross advances) of 1.3% as on September 30, 2026, having improved from 1.7% on September 30, 2025. This traction was demonstrated across most asset segments. Contrary to the past decade - where high delinquencies in the corporate loan portfolio constrained the overall asset quality, slippage within this portfolio have subsided. With regards to the retail and SME (small and medium enterprises) segments, while delinquencies have normalized from previous low levels, the overall slippage to NPAs have remained range bound.

 

GNPAs (as a percentage of gross advances) in the retail (including rural) segments, improved marginally to 1.3% as on September 30, 2026, from 1.5% as on September 30, 2025. Provision coverage ratio (excluding technical write-offs) was also comfortable at 75.0% on this date (75.6% as on September 30, 2025) resulting in NNPAs of 0.3% on the same date.

 

The bank has demonstrated resilience in the past while tiding over asset quality pressures across cycles by putting in place an institutionalised risk assessment framework involving enhanced control and monitoring mechanisms. Apart from this, various schemes launched by the Government of India and the RBI, such as the emergency credit line guarantee scheme that have benefitted the micro, small and medium enterprises, have also aided asset quality for lenders, including ICICI Bank, within this segment.

 

Moreover, the bank’s strategy of increasing the proportion of retail (including rural loans) assets, which stood at ~52% of overall net advances as on September 30, 2026, is expected to granularise the loan book further and support asset quality. Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor the sustenance in asset quality at sound levels, alongside growth in advances.

Liquidity Superior

Liquidity continues to be supported by a strong retail deposit base that forms a significant part of the total deposits. As on September 30, 2026, liquidity coverage ratio was 124% (on consolidated basis). Liquidity also benefits from access to systemic sources of funds such as the liquidity adjustment facility from the RBI, access to the call money market and the refinance limits from National Housing Bank and National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development.

ESG Profile

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of ICICI Bank supports its already strong credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has a reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base and can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not have a direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment.

 

ICICI Bank has an ongoing focus on strengthening the various aspects of its ESG profile.

ESG profile

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of ICICI Bank supports its already strong credit risk profile.


The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has a reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base and can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not have a direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment. 
ICICI Bank has an ongoing focus on strengthening the various aspects of its ESG profile.


Key ESG highlights of ICICI Bank:

  • The bank’s exposure to environmentally polluting sectors is relatively low compared with peers in banking sectors
  • The bank’s Scope 1 and 2 emissions and energy consumption intensities at ~1 tCO2e and ~2 MWh per employee, respectively is lower compared to peers, also relatively high share of renewable energy (~30%) in its overall energy mix compared to peers
  • Furthermore, the bank has reported that its exposure towards green sectors like renewable energy, electric vehicles, green buildings and water and waste management, stood at ~Rs 30,704 crore as on March 31, 2026, also the bank’s premise of near 5 million square feet is certified by the Indian Green Building Council (IGBC)
  • The bank continued to consider ESG parameters in lending decisions and risk management framework, it has adopted assessment of ESG risk which includes social and environmental evaluation of project financing proposals, integrating climate change and ESG issues into the credit evaluation
  • The bank’s turnover rate at ~16% and its gender diversity at ~32%, were better compared with the peer average
  • The bank’s board[1] is categorised by high share of independent directors (69%) on the board and board committees coupled with an independent chairman of the board. Though share of women on the board was low at 15%. Furthermore, it has a dedicated investor grievance redressal mechanism and has reported 100% redressal of investor complaints during fiscal 2026.

[1] Data for Board and committee composition is as on September 01, 2026

Outlook Stable

ICICI Bank will maintain its strong market position, healthy capitalisation and comfortable resource profile over the medium term.

Rating Sensitivity Factors

Downward Factors

  • Significant deterioration in asset quality impacting earnings on sustained basis
  • Decline in CARs (including capital conservation buffer [CCB]) with CET I (common equity tier I) remaining below 11% on sustained basis.

About the Company

Promoted by the erstwhile ICICI Limited, ICICI Bank was incorporated in 1994. In 2002, ICICI Limited was merged with ICICI Bank.

 

The bank had a consolidated asset base of Rs 32.9 lakh crore as on September 30, 2026, with net advances of Rs 19.4 lakh crore. On standalone basis, asset base and gross advances were Rs 27.2 lakh crore and Rs 18.5 lakh crore, respectively, on the same date. The advances mix consisted of 52% retail and rural loans, 21% business banking, 19% domestic corporate, and 8% overseas advances as on September 30, 2026.

Standalone profit after tax (PAT) was Rs 50,146 crore in fiscal 2026 against Rs 47,227 crore in the previous fiscal. At the consolidated level (with subsidiaries and other associate entities), reported PAT was Rs 54,208 crore against Rs 51,029 crore.

 

For the first half ended September 30, 2026, ICICI Bank reported standalone PAT of Rs 29,647 crore against Rs 25,127 crore in the corresponding period previous fiscal. At the consolidated level (with subsidiaries and other associate entities), the bank reported PAT of Rs 31,773 crore against Rs 26,915 crore for the same periods.
