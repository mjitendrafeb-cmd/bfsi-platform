Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A+/Crisil PPMLD A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the debt instruments of Edelweiss Financial Services limited (EFSL; part of the Edelweiss group).

 

Crisil Ratings has withdrawn its rating on the non-convertible debentures of Rs 489.68 crore in line with its withdrawal policy (See ‘Annexure - Details of rating withdrawn') and on receipt of independent confirmation that these instruments are fully redeemed.

 

On September 25, 2026, EFSL completed the divestment of a 45% stake in Nido Home Finance Ltd (Nido) to Carlyle-affiliated CA Sardo Investments and Aditya Puri’s investment vehicle; Salisbury Investments Private Limited, following receipt of requisite approvals. Consequently, Nido ceased to be a subsidiary of the EFSL group. Post transaction, EFSL now holds ~40% equity stake in Nido, which would translate to a ~26% stake on a fully diluted basis upon conversion of the outstanding warrants.

 

Accordingly, the analytical assessment of EFSL and its subsidiaries will incorporate Nido only to the extent of EFSL’s ownership stake, as opposed to the earlier full consolidation approach. Crisil Ratings believes that the dilution of EFSL’s stake in Nido, resulting in its reclassification from a subsidiary to an associate does not materially alter the group’s overall credit risk profile.

 

The ratings continue to be supported by the Edelweiss group’s adequate capitalisation and diversified business risk profile with good market position in the asset reconstruction and asset management businesses. Growth in retail lending (including MSME [micro, small and medium enterprises]) has, however, been relatively slow. The ratings are constrained by lower-than-expected revival in core profitability and continued high unprovided monitorable portfolio.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of EFSL and its subsidiaries. This is because all the entities, collectively referred to as the Edelweiss group, have significant operational, financial and managerial linkages.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Adequate capitalisation, supported by multiple capital raises

The Edelweiss group has demonstrated its ability to raise capital from global investors across businesses. The group has raised ~Rs 6,000 crore since 2016 across lending, wealth management and asset management businesses. This has helped it to maintain its capital position despite elevated credit cost and absorb asset-side risks. As on June 30, 2026 the group’s networth stood at Rs 5,959 crore against Rs 5,944 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 5,918 crore as on March 31, 2025 and Rs 6,049 crore as on March 31, 2024). The modest accretion in net worth was supported by healthy earnings generation, with consolidated profit increasing by 83% on year in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The decline in networth in fiscal 2025 is attributed to a strategic mark down in the security receipts book. Previously, the reduction in networth was due to the distribution of ~30% of Nuvama’s networth to EFSL shareholders as part of the demerger.

 

The group’s gearing stood at 3.1 times (excluding CBLO[1], 2.8 times) as on March 31, 2026 as against 3.02 times (excluding CBLO, 2.6 times) as on March 31, 2025 and 3.2 times (excluding CBLO, 2.9 times) as on March 31, 2024 (2.4 times as on March 31, 2023, and 2.5 times as on March 31, 2022). As on June 30, 2026, the gearing stood at 3.3 times (excluding CBLO, 2.9).

 

While the group has reduced its net debt from Rs 18,550 crore as on March 31, 2022, to Rs 10,430 crore as on March 31, 2026, it remains elevated due to higher debt in the group’s corporate entities. As on June 30, 2026, the net debt stood at Rs 11,160 crore.

 

The group's focus on fee-based businesses is expected to keep net debt requirement low. Additionally, planned value unlocking in various businesses, recoveries in wholesale exposures, upstreaming of dividends and monetisation of other investments in properties is expected to aid debt reduction. Proceeds from recent stake sales, including stake sale in housing finance business, 15% in the mutual fund business to Westbridge Capital and 4.4% in alternative assets business will also help reduce debt. Further stake sale in alternative assets (SEBI approval received on refiled Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP)), and potentially insurance businesses, will unlock capital and facilitate debt reduction.

 

 The group’s ability to reduce its debt while maintaining adequate capitalisation and gearing will remain monitorable.

 

Diversified business risk profile

The Edelweiss group is a diversified financial services conglomerate with presence across four key verticals: asset management, asset reconstruction, credit (wholesale and retail) and insurance (life and general). Having established a leading position in the alternative assets and asset reconstruction businesses, the group is now focused on expanding its market share in other segments, which is expected to contribute to greater earnings stability over time. With increasing liquidity supported by stake sales, the group is now also focusing on growing its treasury book, which stood at Rs 3,620 crore as on March 31, 2026. As on August 31, 2026, the book increased to Rs 4,333 crore.

 

The asset management business encompasses both mutual fund and alternative asset operations. As a prominent player in the alternative asset space, the group has consistently demonstrated growth in its fee-paying assets under management (AUM), which increased to Rs 44,710 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 33,833 crore as on March 31, 2025. The mutual fund AUM also scaled up to Rs 1,58,200 crore from Rs 1,41,800 crore. As on June 30, 2026, the EAAA India Alternatives Ltd’s fee paying AUM and mutual fund AUM further increased to Rs 48,623 crore and Rs 1,76,200 crore respectively.

 

In the distressed assets segment, Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction Company (EARC) maintains a good market position as one of the top five private asset reconstruction companies (ARC) in India. EARC's AUM was ~Rs 14,717 crore as on March 31, 2025, declining from Rs 31,590 crore as on March 31, 2024, following the write-off of its 5:95 portfolio as well as some 15:85 portfolio, which had completed over eight years. The AUM further declined to Rs 9,229 crore as on March 31, 2026, due to slower acquisitions in line with the industry trend, and higher redemptions of large wholesale exposures. As on March 31, 2026, the fee-paying AUM stood at Rs 7,838 crore against Rs 12,163 crore as on March 31, 2025. As on June 30, 2026, AUM stood at Rs 9,272 crore and fee-paying AUM was reported at Rs 7,684 crore.

 

Having previously focused on wholesale assets, EARC has shifted its attention to the retail and MSME segments, which are less AUM accretive. The company expects the share of retail and MSME assets to increase over the medium term.

 

In the lending business, the group has been focusing on retail (mortgage and MSME loans) through a co-lending model, while reducing its wholesale book. However, retail disbursements have been slower than expected due to delays in onboarding and underwriting with co-lending partners, as well as regulatory changes pertaining to colending business. The regulatory embargo on ECL Finance had a residual impact on growth across the lending businesses. Due to these factors, retail AUM growth was modest at a 2-year CAGR of 5% during fiscals 2023-2025. Growth improved to 14% on year in fiscal 2026 with AUM at Rs 6,138 crore as on March 31, 2026. The wholesale loan book has continued to decline, reaching Rs 315 crore by March 2026. The outstanding security receipts on the book of ECL Finance reduced to Rs 1,500 crore as on March 31, 2026 from Rs 2,260 crore as on March 31, 2025.

 

Out of total lending book, ECL Finance's AUM increased to Rs 1,669 crore as of March 2026, from Rs 1,289 crore as of March 2025. The AUM further rose to Rs 1,965 crore as of June 2026.

 

The group also houses life and general insurance businesses, which are gaining scale and are expected to break even by fiscal 2027.

 

 However, divestment of majority stakes in some of the businesses of the group may reduce the diversity of business risk profile.

 

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Subdued profitability for current size and scale considering presence in multiple businesses

The group’s profitability is lower than that of other large, financial groups, but most of its businesses have consistently reported profits since the last quarter of fiscal 2021.

 

 In fiscal 2026, the group reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 680 crore, higher than Rs 536 crore in fiscal 2025. The return on assets (RoA) improved to 1.6% from 1.3%. In the past few years, the profit was largely supported by gains on investments in Nuvama, wherein the group witnessed considerable fair valuation gains. The profitability for fiscal 2026 was supported by sale proceeds from Edelweiss Asset Management Limited(EAML) and fair valuation of the remaining stake in EAML following its reclassification from a subsidiary to a jointly controlled entity.

 

PAT of operating businesses stood at Rs 520 crore for fiscal 2026 compared with Rs 566 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 458 crore for 2024). This included one-time impact of Rs 143 crore, pertaining to labour code impact, employee stock option plan expenses across businesses and GST impact on life insurance. Furthermore, during the fiscal, the group incurred a management overlay of Rs 920 crore on its discontinued security receipts and an additional provision of Rs 480 crore on assets in a few subsidiaries due to updated risk assessments and prudential considerations arising from evolving market conditions.

 

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, the group reported a PAT of Rs 134 crore with an RoA of 1.2%, compared to a PAT of Rs 103 crore and an RoA of 1.0% in the corresponding quarter of the previous fiscal. The PAT of operating business was reported at Rs 161 crore compared to Rs 179 crore in the previous period, supported by Rs 49 crore of one-off gains in insurance business.

 

Although the business profitability is still affected by losses in the insurance business, the losses have been on a declining trend and the entities are expected to break even by fiscal 2027.

 

Profitability in the credit businesses has also remained subdued over the years. The NBFC business has benefited from recoveries in the erstwhile wholesale portfolio (reflected in net gain on fair value changes).

 

The corporate segment, which comprises 11 entities of the group and is engaged in investments, corporate services, merchant banking, technology services, portfolio management services, and trading, continues to report intermittent quarterly losses. These losses can be attributed to the high interest cost associated with the debt in these entities. This debt accounts for a significant proportion of the group’s total gross debt (63% of gross debt, excluding CBLO, as on March 31, 2026). The event-based gains in this segment can offset these losses, as and when these gains happen. On a full year basis, in the past three fiscals, the maximum loss incurred in the segment has been ~Rs 51 crore. In fiscal 2026, this segment reported a profit of Rs 161 crore supported by stake sale in EAML as well as alternative assets business. In first quarter of fiscal 2027, this segment posted a loss of Rs 26 crore compared to loss of Rs 76 crore in the corresponding period of the previous quarter.

 

Of the various businesses, the asset reconstruction and alternative assets, remain the largest contributors to overall profitability (forming 90% of the overall PAT for fiscal 2026). Notably, EARC’s profitability was supported by healthy redemptions even as there were nil acquisitions during the embargo period.

 

Looking ahead, the alternative assets business is expected to continue supporting profitability.

 

However, any additional provisioning required on the monitorable book based on the pace and extent of recovery from underlying assets will need to be closely monitored.

 

Furthermore, the group's ability to maintain profits in the asset reconstruction across business cycles, grow retail lending while controlling credit costs, and reduce corporate debt and associated costs will be crucial and a key focus area over the medium term

 

Asset quality monitorable with elevated level of monitorable portfolio

The overall gross stage III assets in the lending business of the group stood at Rs 459 crore (7.1% of gross loans) as on June 30,2026 compared with Rs 431 crore (7.1% of gross loans) as on March 31, 2026, Rs 416 crore (7.9% of loans) as on March 31, 2025, Rs 720 crore (13.0%) as on March 31, 2024 and Rs 794 crore (10.5%) as on March 31, 2023. Retail gross stage III assets were reported at Rs 147 crore (2.7%) as on June 30, 2026  against Rs 120 crore (2.3%) as on March 31, 2026, Rs 105 crore (2.3%) as on March 31, 2025, Rs 78 crore (1.84%) as on March 31, 2024, and Rs 124 crore (3.3%) as on March 31, 2023.

 

The group has been consciously running down the wholesale portfolio through various modes. While recoveries have also contributed, the reduction has been primarily due to sell-down to ARCs (both internal and external) and alternative investment funds (AIFs) in earlier periods.

 

The Edelweiss group has retained risks and rewards on a large portion of this and hence, Crisil Ratings tracks the monitorable portfolio to assess the asset quality of the group. This includes gross stage III accounts in the lending book (Rs 459 crore), security receipts held by the group (including those in EARC) related to sell down transactions (Rs 4,611 crore) and loans sold down to AIFs (Rs 1,038 crore). As on June 30, 2026, the overall monitorable portfolio stood at Rs 6,108 crore as against Rs 6,111 crore as on March 31, 2026, lower than Rs 7,446 crore as on March 31, 2025. Although, the monitorable portfolio has decreased from Rs 12,097 crore as on March 31, 2022 (and Rs 11,383 crore as on March 31, 2021), it remain elevated. Crisil Ratings notes that while majority of this monitorable portfolio represents on-book exposure of the Edelweiss group, a portion of it pertains to exposure of external ARC or AIF wherein the group has extended a put option.

 

The group has made provisions of 54% against the outstanding monitorable portfolio resulting in a net monitorable portfolio of Rs 2,827 crore as on June 30, 2026 against Rs 2,879 crore as on March 31, 2026, Rs 4,366 crore as on March 31, 2025, and Rs 6,018 crore as on March 31, 2024. According to management estimates, there is a reasonable level of collateral cover for most of the portfolio, providing some protection against potential losses.

 

However, any disruptions to the planned recoveries could necessitate higher provisioning and likely exert pressure on the group’s profitability. As such, the group's ability to realise the expected recoveries from the monitorable portfolio will remain a key area of focus and monitoring.

Liquidity Adequate

As on August 31, 2026, the group had liquidity of Rs 5,949 crore, of which Rs 1,414 crore was in the form of bank balances, fixed deposits and investments in mutual funds, Rs 4,333 crore in the form of liquid treasury book, and Rs 202 crore in the form of available lines. This is sufficient to meet debt obligations and operating expenses for about six months, even after assuming nil business inflow and no incremental fund raising. Furthermore, the group’s liquidity is expected to be supported by contractual receivables from the retail book and ongoing recoveries from wholesale exposures and the planned stake sales.

ESG Profile

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social and governance (ESG) profile of EFSL supports its credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile of financial institutions typically factors in governance as a key differentiator among them. The sector has a reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base, and as it can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not have a direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment and other sustainability related factors.

 

The Edelweiss group has an evolving focus on strengthening various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

ESG highlights:

  • EFSL is committed to using natural resources efficiently and reduction in GHG emissions
  • The GHG emissions decreased by 33% in fiscal 2026, as compared to previous year.
  • The company increased its solar power generation capacity by establishing 1.2 MW capacity installation at Solapur, Maharashtra.
  • EFSL recycled 35.647 megaliters of waste and sewage water through a sewage treatment plant. Also, compost production from food waste increased to 37 tonnes & 2.39 tons of paper waste converted into green edelweiss writing pads.
  • Nil lost time injury frequency rate (LTIFR) and fatalities of employees were reported in the fiscal.
  • No complaints were received from employees, customers & value chain partners in fiscal 2026.
  • In fiscal 2026, 207548 hours of training covering 5698 learners were delivered, 100% of the employees were trained on skills and safety.
  • The company’s governance structure is characterized by fair mix of independent directors on Board. Additionally, the company has mechanisms for redressal of investor grievances and extensive disclosures.
  • ESG Council is in place to provide effective governance for addressing ESG parameters, Edelweiss' ESG agenda is further augmented by the CSR Committee and EdelGive Foundation

 

 There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. The Edelweiss group’s commitment to ESG will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the presence of foreign investors.

Outlook Stable

The ‘Stable’ outlook factors in the group’s strengthened liquidity and flexibility to raise additional capital through asset monetisation avenues, if needed.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • Substantial improvement in the overall business profitability of the group with return on equity of over ~15% on a sustained basis
  • Significant growth in scale across all business segments, including those beyond asset reconstruction and asset management businesses.
  • Sharp organic reduction in the monitorable portfolio

 

Downward factors

  • Any material deterioration in profitability from the current levels
  • Gearing levels increasing to 4.0 times
  • Funding access challenges with limited fundraising at optimal costs by the group
  • Slower traction in resolution of monitorable portfolio.

About the company

EFSL was incorporated in 1995 as Edelweiss Capital Ltd. The company, on a standalone basis, is primarily engaged in investment banking services and provides development, managerial and financial support to group entities.

 

On standalone basis, EFSL’s reported networth stood at Rs 6067 crore as on March 31, 2026 compared with Rs 5,517 crore as on March 31, 2025.  For the fiscal 2026, the company reported PAT of Rs 448 crore against a loss of Rs 52 crore for the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.

 

For first quarter of fiscal 2027, company reported a PAT of 70 crore as against a PAT of Rs 65 crore in corresponding period of the previous fiscal

 

About the group

The Edelweiss group comprised 27 subsidiaries and associates as on March 31, 2026. The group had 255 offices in 135 cities as on March 31, 2026. As part of streamlining its operating structure, including reducing the number of group entities from 74 as on March 31, 2016, the group has restructured the businesses into four verticals — credit, insurance, asset management and asset reconstruction.

 

The group is present across alternative and domestic asset management, asset reconstruction, loans to individuals, mortgage finance - loans against property, MSME finance, and life and general insurance. In addition, the group has a corporate business which functions as an investment vertical and houses the treasury management function as well.

 

On a consolidated basis, the group reported PAT of Rs 680 crore on a total income (net off interest expense) of Rs 8,374 crore for fiscal 2026, as against PAT of Rs 536 crore on a total income of Rs 6,982 crore for fiscal 2025.

 

In quarter ended June 30, 2026, EFSL group reported a PAT of Rs 134 crore on a total income of Rs 1,861 crore as against a PAT of Rs 103 crore on a total income of Rs 1,595 crore in quarter ended June 30, 2025.
