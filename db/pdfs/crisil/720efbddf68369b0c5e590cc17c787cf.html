Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the commercial paper programme of The National Small Industries Corporation Limited (NSIC).

 

The rating reflects the strategic importance of the company for development of the micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSME) sector in India and the expectation of continued support from the Government of India (GoI), which is a 100% shareholder in the entity. The rating also factors in the stable asset quality and earnings of the company.

 

These strengths are partly offset by limited presence in the overall MSME segment and an average resource profile.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has assessed the standalone credit risk profile of NSIC and has factored in strong support from the parent GoI, both on an ongoing basis and in the event of distress. This is given the 100% shareholding by GOI and the strategic importance of the company for development of the micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSME) sector in India.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Strategic importance to, and majority ownership by, GoI

The company acts as a facilitator for the Indian MSME sector and supports its overall growth. This sector employs the largest share of Indian population, after agriculture, making it essential for overall economic development. The company plays an important role in implementing Certain Government Schemes for MSMEs, such as Raw Material Assistance (RMA) scheme, PM Vishwakarma scheme, National SC-ST Hub scheme and other training activities.

 

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, the company received grants and subsidies of Rs 1 crore (Rs 98 crore in fiscal 2026 and Rs 90 crore in the previous fiscal) for implementation of these schemes/activities. These inflows are expected to remain in a similar range over the medium term. Earlier, between fiscals 2012 and 2014, GoI provided capital support by infusing Rs 300 crore in three tranches into the company. This demonstration of need-based support is expected to continue.

 

The government is also committed to offer distress support and has provided a sovereign guarantee against borrowing from multilateral and bilateral agencies. The company is likely to remain an important institution for GoI to support and strengthen the MSME sector. Hence, the government is expected to continue providing necessary support to sustain the credit profile over the medium term.

 

Stable earnings with asset quality supported by loans being backed by guarantees

The loan portfolio is largely backed by bank guarantees (BGs) from schedule commercial banks, resulting in zero ultimate loss. As on June 30, 2026, 93% of total advances were backed by BGs and historically, this share has remained above 90%. Furthermore, the company has a well-defined credit policy where loans are given up to a maximum of 99% of the BG value. These are short-term loans with maturity of 180 days and have provision to roll over subject to the availability of BG limits and performance of the account. Furthermore, the average ticket size is only about Rs 1 crore, thereby providing granularity to the credit portfolio.

 

NSIC’s reported Gross Non-Performing Assets (GNPAs) stood at 3.3% as on June 30, 2026, having decreased from 3.4 % as on March 31, 2026 and 3.9% as on March 31, 2025 largely because of recoveries in the legacy portfolio. Most of these accounts are legacy accounts and are fully provided for.

 

Resultantly, credit costs have remained negligible. Other elements of profitability, such as net interest margins and operating expenses, have remained range bound. The company reported a profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 159 crore and return on assets (RoA) of 4.5% for fiscal 2026 as against Rs 146 crore and 4.4% respectively for the previous fiscal. For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, PAT was Rs 33 crore and RoA was 3.6% (annualised).

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Limited coverage of the MSME sector

The company caters to around 1 lakh MSME units, through its various product offerings, including services such as RMA, single-point registration for securing government contracts, consortia and tender marketing, debt syndication, and technology services, etc., as well as lending.

 

The overall portfolio outstanding as on March 31, 2026, was Rs 3,271 crore as compared to Rs 2,898 crore as on March 31, 2025 (Rs 3,336 crore as on June 30, 2026), marking a modest year-on-year growth of 13%. Of this, 92% was deployed in the RMA scheme followed by another 5% extended towards Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS) and 2% towards bill discounting as on March 31, 2026. Apart from traditional portfolios such the RMA scheme, the company has recently started growing its TReDS portfolio that is likely to continue over the medium term as well. 

 

In the near to medium term, NSIC’s coverage of the MSME sector is likely to remain small and linked to its purpose of facilitating growth of MSMEs.

 

Average resource profile

Resources remain dominated by bank borrowing with 95% of borrowing being in the form of working capital demand loans or Term Loans. The proportion of low-cost long-term funding from multilateral agencies is very small. The weighted average cost of borrowing for fiscal 2026 was 6.4% (7.4% for fiscal 2025).

Liquidity Strong

Asset liability management (ALM) profile of NSIC remains adequate given short-term borrowings of matching tenure of short-term financing provided under raw material financing wherein the average credit period is around 180 days. As on June 30, 2026, the ALM profile shows positive cumulative mismatches in the 0-7 days bucket, and negative cumulative mismatch in the next (8-14 days) bucket. This is due to payments on short-term funding availed by NSIC; however, this mismatch is usually bridged using rolling over of short-term payments and using further utilisation of sanctioned but unutilised lines.

 

Further, adequate liquidity buffer was available in terms of cash and equivalents of Rs 68 crore and unutilised bank lines of Rs 2,758 crore as on August 31, 2026 as against Rs 1,651 crore upcoming debt repayments till November 2026. Further, NSIC has provided liquidity back-up letter and that will maintain liquidity back-up to the extent of the short-term debt (rated and unrated) maturing during the subsequent seven days. Liquidity is further cushioned due to the expected support from GoI in the event of distress.

Rating Sensitivity Factors

Downward Factors:

  • Decline in GoI shareholding and/or diminution of support extended to the institution by GoI, in future
  • Significant deterioration in asset quality, leading to pressure on profitability with RoA less than zero on a sustained basis.

About the Company

NSIC was established by GoI in 1955 and is 100% owned by GoI and is under the purview of the Ministry of MSME. The company is also registered with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) as a non-deposit-taking, non-banking financial company (NBFC). However, being a GoI undertaking, it was exempted from NBFC prudential regulations as specified by RBI. However, as per the Withdrawal of Exemptions Granted to Government Owned NBFCs circular issued by RBI on May 31, 2018, all the prudential norms and NBFC regulations are also applicable to government-owned NBFCs including NSIC from March 31, 2019. The company had 109 offices across India and around 600 employees, as on June 30, 2026.

 

NISC acts as facilitator and provides various promotional and commercial schemes to MSMEs.  RMA, both on credit and advance payment, single-point registration for securing government contracts, consortia and tender marketing, debt syndication, and technology services are some of the services provided. It also provides a couple of support services - infomediary services - through a business-to-business portal, software technology parks and incubators for MSMEs.

 

NSIC has been appointed by the government to manage a Rs 10,000 crore funds-of-fund, which it administers through its wholly owned subsidiary NSIC Venture Capital Fund Limited. It has been created as an alternative investment fund for providing growth capital to MSMEs, through equity/quasi-equity/equity like structured instruments.
