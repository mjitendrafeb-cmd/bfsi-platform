Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has converted its provisional rating to final rating of ‘Crisil AA (SO)’ for Series A Pass-Through Certificates (PTCs) and ‘Crisil A (SO)’ for Series B PTCs issued by ‘Cedric 07 2026’ under a securitisation transaction backed by a pool of personal loan receivables originated by EarlySalary Services Private Limited (ESPL; ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’),

 

The ratings reflect the credit support available to the PTCs, the credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, the origination and servicing capabilities of ESPL as well as the soundness of the legal structure governing the transaction.

 

Crisil Ratings has now received the final legal/executed documents for this transaction. Amended and restated Trust Deed, Deed of Assignment of receivables in the process of securitisation and Agreement to assign were executed in place of Trust Deed and Assignment Agreement, respectively. Collection and Servicing Agent Agreement was executed in place of the Collection and Servicing Agreement. However, these changes are not material and do not have any impact on the credit risk profile of the instruments. The other transaction terms are in line with the terms envisaged at the time of the provisional rating. Hence, the provisional ratings are converted to final ratings.

Payment structure

The transaction has a Par with Turbo amortisation structure. The trust settled by the transaction’s trustee, Catalyst Trusteeship Limited, has issued Series A and Series B PTCs to investors in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 82.0% and 6.0%, respectively, of the pool principal at the time of securitisation.

 

The PTCs are issued under a replenishment structure, with a door-to-door tenure of 36 months, implying legal final maturity of PTCs in October 2029. The initial six months serve as the replenishment period followed by an amortisation period. Maximum maturity of the loans that can be added during the replenishment period must have a scheduled repayment date or maturity date of no later than one month prior to the Series A1 PTCs legal final maturity date.

 

During the replenishment period, Series A PTC holders and Series B PTC holders are entitled to receive interest on monthly basis. Residual collection, including but not limited to, excess interest spread (EIS), prepayment, receipt of overdue principal, receipt of overdue interest and any other amounts received as part of monthly collection or otherwise, will then be used to replenish the purchase of the replenishment pool into eligible receivables, defined as per the selection criteria.

 

During the amortisation period, Series A and Series B PTC holders are entitled to receive interest on monthly basis. Principal repayment for Series A PTCs is expected on monthly basis but promised on ultimate basis — on legal final maturity. Post redemption of the Series A PTCs, principal repayment to Series B PTCs is expected on monthly basis but promised on ultimate basis — on legal final maturity. Also, 100% of the residual cash flow will be used for redemption of Series A PTCs. Post redemption of Series A PTCs, 100% of the residual cash flow will be used for redemption of Series B PTCs.

 

The Series B PTCs principal repayment is fully subordinate to the Series A PTCs. The Series A and Series B PTCs have timely interest and ultimate principal structure (TIUP).

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts are supported by external cash collateral, over collateral and EIS. During the replenishment and amortisation periods, on a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to pay the senior expenses and promised interest payments to Series A and Series B PTCs. On legal maturity date, the cash collateral can be used to pay the promised principal to Series A PTCs and Series B PTCs, respectively.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction:

Series A PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of rated Series B PTCs principal amounting to Rs 3.68 crore (6.0% of the initial pool principal), subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 12.00 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).

 

For Series B PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 11.98 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).

 

The external credit enhancement will be maintained as fixed deposits with the bank for and on behalf of the trust.

 

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provides loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit enhancement in the structure:
  • Series A PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of rated Series B PTCs principal of Rs 3.68 crore (6.0% of the initial pool principal), subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 12.00 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).
  • For Series B PTCs: Internal credit enhancement from subordination of over collateral principal amounting to Rs 7.35 crore (12.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 11.98 crore (19.6% of the initial pool principal); external credit enhancement from cash collateral amounting to Rs 6.13 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal).
  • Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
  • The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning (number of instalments paid) of 7.4 and consequently, the pool had amortised by 19.1% as on the cut-off date of September 30, 2026.

 

Weaknesses:

  • High risk profile of underlying asset class
  • Unsecured nature of loans and risk profile of underlying borrowers: The pool comprises personal loans extended to salaried and self-employed borrowers. In a stressed environment, such loans are vulnerable to higher delinquencies. Given the unsecured nature of these loans, recovery prospects from delinquent borrowers are limited. The pool’s collection performance will remain susceptible to risks inherent in this asset class.
  • Geographical concentration
  • The top three states (Maharashtra: 20.0%; Karnataka: 15.8% and Telangana: 11.7%) account for 47.5 % of the pool principal.
  • Replenished pool composition
  • There may be changes in the pool during the replenishment period; however, these changes are controlled within certain boundary conditions established through well-defined eligibility criteria for new loans added to the pool and replenishment termination events to prevent excessive build-up of risks in the pool.
  • These aspects have been adequately factored in the rating analysis of Crisil Ratings.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 3.0 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collection from the pool
  • For Series B PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 2.1 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collection from the pool
  • Upgrade in rating of the servicer or originator

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 2.7 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool
  • For Series B PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 1.9 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool
  • Downgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator

 

Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating.

Liquidity: Strong

Liquidity is strong for Series A PTCs and Series B PTCs given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the currently-estimated base shortfall.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cash flow

The transaction is backed by personal loan receivables originated by ESPL. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (September 30, 2026) are outlined below:

  • The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning of 7.4 monthly instalments, which has led to principal amortisation of 19.1% as of the pool cut-off date.
  • The pool is concentrated in terms of geography, with the top three states accounting for 47.5%.
  • The average ticket size for contracts in the pool is Rs 2.24 lakh with a weighted average interest rate of 22.7%.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTC investors are taking a direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool-specific characteristics.
  • To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the static pool performance (with information on 90+ delinquency) of personal loans originated by ESPL over the first quarter of fiscal 2024 to the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (with performance as of September 2026).
  • Crisil Ratings has analysed the dynamic portfolio delinquencies of ESPL’s portfolio. As of September 2026, the 90+ delinquency for ESPL’s portfolio was 1.4%. Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which include seasoning profile and repayment track record, and parameters such as original tenure, interest rate and loan-to-value. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in risks arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.

 

Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. The prepayment risk has been assessed based on historical levels of prepayments for similar pools.

 

Assumptions:

  •                   After making adjustments on these factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is 7.0–9.0% of the pool cash flow.
  •                    

Monthly prepayment rate of 2.0–4.0% has been applied to the pool cash flow.

ESPL is registered as a non-banking financial company with the RBI and is engaged in providing unsecured personal loans to professionals and salaried employees. The company provides purpose-driven financing across education, healthcare, insurance premiums and residential rooftop solar.

 

ESPL is a wholly owned subsidiary of SWTL, incorporated in October 2015, by the founders, Akshay Mehrotra and Ashish Goyal. The group follows a branchless digital lending model and serves customers across India. Consolidated assets under management (AUM) stood at Rs 8,603 crore as on March 31, 2026. The group reported profit after tax of Rs 257 crore in fiscal 2026.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer covered as part of the transaction documents are as follows:

 

  • The trustee, acting for and on behalf of the investors, shall appoint the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the collection and payment account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction document.
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the waterfall mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.

It is India’s foremost provider of ratings, data, research, analytics and solutions with a strong track record of growth, culture of innovation, and global footprint.

It has delivered independent opinions, actionable insights, and efficient solutions to over 100,000 customers through businesses that operate from India, the US, the UK, Argentina, Poland, China, Hong Kong and Singapore.

It is majority owned by S&P Global Inc, a leading provider of transparent and independent ratings, benchmarks, analytics and data to the capital and commodity markets worldwide.
