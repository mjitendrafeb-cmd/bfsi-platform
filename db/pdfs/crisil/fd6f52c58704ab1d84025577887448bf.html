Detailed rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Provisional Crisil A (SO)’ rating to Series A1 pass-through certificates (PTCs) issued by Aven Trust 2026, under a securitisation transaction originated by Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Pvt Ltd (Dvara KGFS; rated ‘Crisil BBB+/Stable’), backed by a pool of unsecured joint liability group (JLG) loan receivables and micro enterprise loan (MEL) receivables.

 

The rating factors in the credit support available to PTCs, credit quality of underlying pool receivables, origination and servicing capabilities of the originator and the soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

Payment structure

The transaction has a ‘Par with excess interest spread (EIS)’ structure. Dvara KGFS will assign the loan pool to ‘Aven Trust 2026’, a trust settled by Catalyst Trusteeship Limited, which will issue instruments to investors in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 90.0% of future pool principal outstanding as on the cut-off date (August 31, 2026).

 

The transaction structure includes trigger-based turbo amortisation, where 100% EIS would be utilised for amortization of Series A1 PTCs principal till the tranche maturity, if any of the following events take place:

  • If PAR 30 of the pool exceeds 5.00% of the initial pool principal
  • If the rating of the Series A1 PTC falls below its current rating
  • If the rating of Dvara KGFS falls two notches below its current rating from any rating agency
  • Any adverse regulatory action is initiated against Dvara KGFS by a competent regulatory authority

 

Series A1 PTC holders are promised timely interest on a monthly basis and principal on ultimate basis on the maturity date of the PTCs. Investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, principal subordination, and subordination of EIS.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for Series A1 PTCs are supported by cash collateral and subordination of EIS. On a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest payments to Series A1 PTC holders in case of a shortfall in collections from the pool. On the final maturity date, the cash collateral can also be used to make the promised principal repayment in case of a shortfall in collections.  All prepayment collections will be utilised for accelerated redemption of the Series A1 PTCs.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure for Series A1 PTC is as below:

  • Series A1 PTCs are supported by internal credit enhancement subordination, which includes overcollateralisation amounting to Rs 1.58 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 2.55 crore (16.2% of initial pool principal).
  • The PTCs are also supported by external credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 1.26 crore (8.0% of the initial pool principal), in the form of a fixed deposit created in the name of the trust.

 

Based on the assessment of Crisil Ratings, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key rating drivers & detailed description

Strengths:

  • Credit support available in the structure
    • External cash collateral in the structure, amounting to Rs 1.26 crore (8.0% of pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs
    • Internal credit enhancement assuming zero prepayments, aggregating Rs 4.13 crore (28.2% of initial pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs – including principal subordination of Rs 1.58 crore (10.0% of pool principal) and EIS of Rs 2.55 crore (16.2% of pool principal).
  • Seasoning profile of contracts in the pool
    • The pool has a weighted average seasoning of 9.6 paid monthly instalments and pre-securitisation amortisation of 23.6%.
    • All the contracts in the underlying loan pool are current as on the cut-off date (August 31, 2026).
  • Legal soundness of the transaction structure
    • The legal structure envisaged for the transaction entails bankruptcy remoteness of the pool of receivables, credit enhancement from the originator, and adherence to prevailing regulations on securitisations.
    • These shall be certified through an independent opinion from an external legal counsel.

 

Weaknesses:

  • Exposure to high geographic concentration risk
    • The pool is geographically concentrated with top state (Tamil Nadu) and top 10 districts forming 76.1% and 76.1% of the initial principal, respectively.
  • Vulnerability to socio-political risks
    • Borrower cash flow in the unsecured MEL segment remains susceptible to socio-political issues and policy / regulatory changes. Such events can disrupt loan repayments of underlying borrowers.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure, exceeding 1.75 times the estimated adjusted base shortfalls on the residual cash flow of the pool, due to sustained healthy collections from the pool.
  • Sharp upgrade in rating of the servicer/originator

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 1.6 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool
  • Sharp downgrade in the rating of the servicer/originator
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity Strong

Liquidity remains adequate as credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover losses exceeding 1.5 times the currently estimated base shortfall.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cashflows

The transaction is backed by JLG loans and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS. The key pool characteristics are outlined below:

  • Contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning of 9.6 monthly instalments paid, which has led to principal amortisation of 26.5% as on the pool cut-off date
  • The pool is moderately concentrated in terms of geography with top three states and top 10 districts accounting for 76.1% and 76.1% of the initial pool principal, respectively
  • The average ticket size for contracts in the pool is Rs 0.91 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 26.7%.
  • All the contracts in the underlying loan pool were current as on the cut-off date

Rating assumptions

Background:

PTC investors are seeking direct exposure on the repayment ability of underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through base-case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool specific characteristics.

 

To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the portfolio delinquency movement (with information on 30+ and 90+ delinquency) of JLG and MEL loans until April 2026. Crisil Ratings has also analysed the delinquency performance in various geographies. As of June 2026, the 30+ delinquency for Dvara’s MEL portfolio was 5.1% and 90+ delinquency was 3.3%. 30+ delinquency for Dvara’s JLG loan portfolio was 7.5% and 90+ delinquency was 7.1%

 

Base-case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which include the seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate, and geography (state/district). Crisil Ratings has additionally factored risk arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.

 

Prepayment is a form of market risk, which will reduce the EIS in the transaction. This risk has been assessed based on historically observed levels of prepayments for similar pools.

 

Assumptions: 

  • After making the adjustments on above factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction lies in the range of 11.0% to 13.0% of pool cashflow
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5% to 1.5% has also been applied to the pool cashflow.

About the company- Originator/Servicer profile

Dvara KGFS was set up in fiscal 2008 by Dvara Trust, for extending unsecured and secured loans to people in rural areas across India. The company was founded by Ms Bindu Ananth and Mr Nachiket Mor. Dvara KGFS focuses on extending multiple financial products for the lending, savings and insurance requirements of individuals in rural areas. The company positions itself as a rural wealth manager providing loans and financial products to customers. It largely extends JLG and unsecured enterprise loans, which comprise more than 95% of the total loans. The JLG loans are up to a ticket size of Rs 50,000 and the enterprise loans up to Rs 5 lakh. The company lends at interest rates of 24.5-27.75% for the JLG loans and 26-29% for the enterprise loans.

 

During FY2019, the business correspondents and corporate agency business of the IFMR Rural Channels and Services Pvt Ltd (holding company of Dvara KGFS) and IFMR Holdings Pvt Ltd (ultimate holding company) were amalgamated with the company.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

  • The Trustee acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint, the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the Receivables into the Collection and Payment Account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the waterfall mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a servicer event of default.

 

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer: The Trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the Servicer Agreement and upon the occurrence of servicer’s event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer

Performance of outstanding rated transactions

Crisil Ratings has ratings outstanding on multiple securitisation transactions backed by JLG and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS. For the pools with amortisation exceeding 40.0%, cumulative collection ratios range from 95.2% to 99.1% as of July 2026 payout.
