Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the certificates of deposits of The Karur Vysya Bank Limited (KVB).

 

The rating continues to reflect the bank’s comfortable capitalization and stable retail resource profile, and improving asset quality, and sustenance of healthy profitability. These strengths are partially offset by the small scale of operations, albeit growing, and exposure to geographical concentration risk.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profiles of KVB.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Comfortable capitalisation and stable resource profile

KVB has comfortable capitalisation metrics, as indicated by common equity tier I (CET-I) capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 18.0% (10% higher than the regulatory requirement), and overall CAR of 18.6% as on June 30, 2026. The networth to net non-performing assets (NPAs) coverage ratio improved to 76.2 times as on June 30, 2026, from 75.8 as on March 31, 2026, and 71.9 times as on March 31, 2025.

 

The bank's overall deposits increased by ~24% on annualised basis to Rs 122,587crore as on June 30, 2026, up from Rs 115,666 crore as on March 31, 2026. The bank has strong relationships with depositors in semi-urban and rural areas of Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, which collectively accounted for ~74% of the total deposits as on June 30, 2026.

 

Current and savings account (CASA) ratio inched up marginally to 27.55% as on June 30, 2026, from 26.91% as on March 31, 2026, although the ratio has declined from 30% as on March 31, 2024. Nevertheless, the bank has a stable term deposit base, with retail deposits (deposits below Rs 1 crore) accounting for ~69% of its term deposit base as on June 30, 2026. While the high proportion of retail deposits enhances the stability of the bank’s resource profile, maintaining the CASA deposit ratio will be crucial in strengthening the deposit profile of KVB.

 

Improving asset quality and sustenance of healthy profitability

The asset quality has been on an improving trend with for last few fiscals with GNPA and NNPA at 0.74% and 0.19% respectively, as on June 30, 2026, and  0.75% and 0.19% as on March 31, 2026 as as against 0.76% as on March 31, 2025 1.4% and 0.4% as on March 31, 2024). This improvement largely stems on account of healthy recoveries along with shift towards retail and gold loans reduction in the share of corporate loan book  over the last few years.

 

The corporate book as a % of total advances has reduced from ~19% in fiscal 2024 to ~14% in fiscal 2026 as well as in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Moreover, the NPA of corporate segment increased to  1.5% as on June 30, 2026 and 1.8% as on March 31, 2026 as against 0.8% as on March 31, 2025 owing to few chunky corporate accounts that slipped during Q2 of fiscal 2026. .

 

The overall credit cost on average assets has improved to 0.3% (annualised) in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, from 0.6% in fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025 owing to improvement in asset quality.. Moreover, the bank has also been maintaining healthy provisioning coverage ratio (PCR) of ~75.0% as on June 30, 2026. The net interest margin (NIM) (as a % of average assets) has also improved to  4.1% (annualized) in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, up from 3.9% in fiscal 2026 and 3.8% in fiscal 2025 owing to reduction in cost of funds and improvement in yield on advances.. The operating expenses (as a % of average assets) has also , decreased to 2.2% in first quarter of fiscal 2027 as against 2.3% in fiscal 2026 and 2.6% in fiscal 2025. As a result, the return on assets (RoA) has improved  to 2.2 in quarter 1 % in fiscal 2027 as compared to 2.0% in fiscal 2026 and  at 1.7%  in  fiscal .

 

Going forward, the return on assets may see some decline owing to compression in net interest margin, however will likely sustain at healthier levels.  Any significant impact on the earnings profile due to any unanticipated slippages and, therefore, credit costs remain a key monitorable.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Small, albeit growing, scale of operations with geographical concentration

The banks’ gross advances and deposits grew by 16.9% and 13.3% in fiscal 2026 to Rs 98,754 crore and Rs 1,15,666 crore respectively as on March 31, 2026. Further the advances and deposits grew to Rs 1,04,680 crores and Rs 1,22,587 respectively as on June 30, 2026. However, the bank has a small share of around 0.5% of deposits and advances in the overall Indian banking system as on June 30, 2026. The bank also has limited reach, with 903branches and 2,169 automated teller machines (ATMs) and cash recyclers as on June 30, 2026.

 

Moreover, operations are concentrated in South India, particularly Tamil Nadu. As on June 30, 2026, about 41% of advances and 56% of deposits were from Tamil Nadu. The financial risk profile therefore remains susceptible to adverse changes in the economic and business environment in the region because of the small scale and high regional concentration in operations.

Liquidity Strong

KVB’s liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) stood at 123.61% as on June 30, 2026. The bank maintained excess statutory liquidity ratio (SLR) of 4.56% as on June 30, 2026. Liquidity also benefits from access to systemic sources of funds, such as the liquidity adjustment facility from the Reserve Bank of India and access to the call money market.

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward factors

  • Increase in net NPAs to above 3%
  • Weakening capital position with CET Tier I CAR below 9%
  • Significantly higher credit cost leading to deterioration in profitability

About the Bank

KVB, set up in 1916, is a private sector bank headquartered in Karur, Tamil Nadu. It has a network of 903 branches, primarily in south India, and 2,169 ATMs and cash recyclers as on June30, 2026. It provides both commercial and consumer banking services. Gross advances and deposits stood at Rs 104680 crore and Rs 1,22,587 crore, respectively, as on June 30, 2026. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, net profit was Rs 756 crore on total income (net of interest expenses) of Rs 1,865 crore, against Rs 521 crore on total income of Rs 1,527 crore, respectively, in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.
