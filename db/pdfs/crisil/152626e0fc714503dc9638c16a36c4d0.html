Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’ rating to the subordinated debts of Jio Credit Limited (JCL). Crisil Ratings has also reaffirmed its ratings on the existing long term bank facilities and debt instruments of JCL at ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’.

 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the announcement made by Jio Financial Services Limited (JFSL) and Bank of America Corporation (BofA) on August 12, 2026, disclosing the signing of a definitive agreement. Under the agreement, BofA, through its wholly owned subsidiary, NB Holdings Corporation, would acquire up to a 49.9% interest in JCL, JFSL's wholly owned subsidiary, for a total consideration of up to Rs 18,268 crore. The transaction remains subject to requisite regulatory and statutory approvals. Crisil Ratings will continue to monitor the progress of the transaction.

 

JFS is a holding company operating a set of financial services businesses through its subsidiaries (collectively referred to as JFS group). In fiscal 2024, JFS was demerged from Reliance Industries Ltd (RIL; rated 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+') and listed as a separate entity.

 

Mr. Mukesh D. Ambani is the promoter of JFS. The promoter group[1] has raised the stake in JFSL post listing to the current level of 49.1% from the initial 45.8% reflecting their long-term commitment. The promoter group is not expected to materially dilute its stake in JFS in the near term from current levels.

 

The ratings factor in JFS group’s healthy capital structure, robust liquidity including its holding of 6.1% of RIL shares, and its experienced management team. The ratings are further driven by the demonstrated track record of the promoter group in building businesses across various sectors. These strengths are partially offset by JFS group's relatively limited track record in the financial services business.

 

As on June 30, 2026, JFS held a significant stake in RIL. JFS group derives financial flexibility from the 6.1% stake that it holds in RIL.

 

Through its subsidiaries, JFS is involved in businesses such as retail and merchant lending (through JCL), payments solutions, payments banking, insurance broking and asset management. The company has expanded its presence in the financial services sector through strategic joint ventures (JVs). It has established a joint venture with BlackRock to enter the asset management business, which has completed its first year of operations. Additionally, the company has entered a joint venture with Allianz to strengthen its presence in the insurance business, under which the reinsurance entity has been established. Within lending, the company intends to grow corporate as well as retail lending with primary focus on the secured segment. On a consolidated basis, JFS had a sizeable networth of Rs 1.3 lakh crore as on September 30, 2026.

 

JCL has scaled up rapidly over the past year, with assets under management increasing to Rs 37,320 crore as on September 30, 2026, from Rs 10,077 crore as on March 31, 2025.

[1] Refers to promoter group entities consolidated

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has taken a consolidated approach of JFS and its subsidiaries/associates. JCL’s operating income is expected to continue to contribute significantly to the consolidated operating income of the JFS group, with growth in its AUM. Further, JCL is currently well capitalised and its capitalisation profile is expected to benefit further from robust liquidity of the JFS group. Additionally, JFS through its subsidiaries and associates holds 6.1% stake in RIL. Further, the ratings factor in the strategic importance of the JFS group to, and the demonstrated track record of, the promoter group in building and supporting its businesses.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Proven track record of the Promoter Group 

JFS marks the foray of the promoter group into the financial services business and will be a critical part of the promoter group’s overall strategy. The promoter group has an extensive track record in building successful and leading operations in sectors such as petrochemicals, retail and telecom.

 

The promoter group has demonstrated its ability to incubate and scale up multiple consumer-oriented businesses, including Reliance Retail Ventures Ltd (RRVL, rated 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+') and Reliance Jio Infocomm Ltd (RJIL, rated 'Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+').

 

The promoter group has long-term commitment towards the JFS group. It had increased its stake in JFS post listing to the current level of 49.1% from 45.8%. The promoter group has also publicly articulated that, like Jio and Retail, JFS too will prove to be an invaluable addition to the Reliance ecosystem of customer-facing businesses.

 

Further, usage of the “Jio” brand, is a positive given the wide recognition among retail customers, merchants and small businesses. The brand recall and the approach leveraging the ecosystem could enable the JFS group to acquire customers at a lower acquisition cost and enable faster product scale up, allowing strong brand recognition in a competitive market. As on September 30, 2026, JCL’s product portfolio primarily includes business and merchant loans, home loans, loan against securities and loan against property. These products are being distributed through a combination of digital journey and direct relationship.

 

Crisil Ratings expects that these aspects, combined with robust financial strength of the JFS group, will help JCL derive benefits to successfully scale up its businesses over the medium term.

 

Healthy capitalisation and liquidity, which will support planned growth

On a consolidated basis, JFS reported a networth of Rs 1.3 lakh crore as on September 30, 2026 (standalone networth of JCL on the same date stood at Rs 7,379 crore). JFS has infused Rs 2,000 crore in JCL in February 2026. The JFS group’s strong lineage is expected to provide significant benefits in terms of access to capital markets for expanding funding sources and implementing a well-diversified funding plan.

 

Gearing for JFS continues to be at negligible levels. However, with the scale up of lending operations, JCL’s standalone on-book gearing increased to around 5.7 times as on September 30, 2026 (3.04 times as on March 31, 2026), from 1.2 times in fiscal 2025. The company will continue to benefit from robust capitalisation and liquidity of JFS group. As on March 31, 2026, group’s liquidity was strong with cash and cash equivalents of Rs 3,598 crore and liquid investments of Rs 16,099.36 crore. Along with this it had 6.1% stake in RIL and had total outstanding debt of Rs 21,768 crore. 

 

Experienced management team

JFS has an experienced management team to run its operations and build scale, especially in retail financing. The management's ability to scale up the business and tap into opportunities within the group’s ecosystem is further strengthened by an experienced board of directors.

 

The management is developing a unified application to address the diverse financial needs of customers and actively engage with them throughout their life journey. This will also allow the company to harness the potential synergies within the JFS group arising from the multiplicity of touch points and diversified product offerings. The management is focused on putting in place adequate systems, processes and policies to scale up the business.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Relatively limited track record in the financial services business

JFS, through its subsidiaries, will be involved in financial services businesses such as retail lending, merchant lending, payments bank operations, payments solutions and insurance broking.

 

It is expected that JFS will benefit from harnessing group synergies and build on its brand franchise across its different businesses as it ramps up its financial services verticals.

 

JCL as the lending arm of the JFS group houses the consumer and merchant lending businesses. The assets under management (AUM) has grown at a healthy pace to Rs 37,320 crore as on September 30, 2026, from Rs 25,816 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 10,077 crore as on March 31, 2025). The book primarily includes home loans and loan against property (44.5%), followed by corporates and SME (44.2%) and loan against securities (11.3%). Part of the portfolio was inorganic, which stood at around Rs 3,460     crores as on September 30, 2026.

 

The company leverages Jio and Reliance group’s ecosystem of vendors and also offers loans to other corporate entities sourced organically through other channels. The company had a standalone networth of Rs 7,379 crore as on September 30, 2026, and profit after tax of Rs 216 crore for H1 fiscal 2027 (Rs 7,163 crore as on March 31, 2026, with a profit after tax of Rs 224 crore).

 

JCL plans to grow the loan book prudently over the medium term with initial focus towards building a secured book. The top 20 largest borrowers in the corporate book (cumulative exposure - Rs 5,078 crore) formed 20% of the total book and almost 45% of the corporate book, as on March 31, 2026, which presents some concentration risk. As on September 30, 2026, the company continues to report negligible non-performing assets with gross non-performing assets (GNPA) and net NPA at 0.07% and 0.05%, respectively (0.03% and 0.02% respectively as on March 31, 2026). Nevertheless, given the early stage of operations and strong growth since inception, underwriting and asset quality performance are yet to be tested. Core operating earnings may remain modest in the near term and gradually improve with recognition of interest revenue from the recently scaled loan book and further scaling-up of operations

 

Ability to successfully scale up the business, while maintaining healthy asset quality and profitability will remain monitorable.

Liquidity Superior

As per JCL’s asset liability profile on March 31, 2026, the company had positive cumulative mismatch in all buckets up to 5 years. As of September 30, 2026, the company had Rs.697 crore of cash and cash equivalents, Rs 11,303 crore of liquid investments and Rs 2109 crore of unutilised bank lines. Against this, the company has Rs 13,330 crore of repayment due over the next 6 months, of which Rs 2,464 crore is Term Loan, Rs 8,832 crore is working capital loan, and Rs.2,034 Crores of Commercial Paper, of which are working capital and commercial paper expected to be rolled over.    

Outlook Stable

JCL and in turn JFS should continue to benefit from robust capitalisation and the flexibility derived from the 6.1 % stake held in RIL.

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward factors:

  • Any significant decline in shareholding by the promoter group
  • Inability to achieve steady-state profitability from core lending operations over a sustained period

About the Company

JCL is a non-deposit-taking, systematically important non-banking financial company (NBFC), and a wholly owned subsidiary of JFS. The entity plans to provide consumer finance, through primarily secured loans, corporate lending (including working capital loans, supply chain financing and term loans) and lending to small and medium enterprises (working capital funding and term funding).  The focus in the medium term will be on building a secured lending book.

 

JCL offers a range of loan products, including corporate loans for enterprises and retail loans such as home loans, loan against property, loan against shares/mutual funds, and vehicle and device leasing solutions to employees of large corporate entities. It operates through 24 branches across 8 states and has over 1033 employees as on March 31, 2026.

 

JCL generated a PAT of Rs 224 crore on total income of Rs 1,496 crore in fiscal 2026 against PAT of Rs 105 crore on a total income of Rs 358 crore, respectively, in fiscal 2025. Additionally, the company reported a PAT of Rs 216 crore on a total income of Rs 1,619 crore in the H1 of fiscal 2027.

About the Group

JFS was incorporated as Reliance Strategic Investments Pvt Ltd on July 22, 1999. The company was renamed Reliance Strategic Investments Ltd on January 14, 2002. It subsequently became Jio Financial Services Ltd on July 25, 2023, following which, a new certificate of incorporation was issued. JFS has also submitted an application to the Reserve Bank of India for conversion to a core investment company (CIC) from an NBFC. JFS, through its subsidiaries, will be involved in the business of retail lending, merchant lending, payments bank operations, payments solutions and insurance broking.

 

JFS has received approval from the Securities and Exchange Board of India to start a mutual fund business in India. Jio Blackrock AMC has started offering mutual fund products to investors, marking a significant expansion of JFS’s offerings.

 

JFS generated a PAT and return on asset (RoA) of Rs 1,561 crore and 1.1%, respectively, in fiscal 2026 as compared to Rs 1,613 crore and 1.2%, respectively, in fiscal 2025. The company reported a PAT of Rs 1,171 crore and RoA of 1.4% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.
