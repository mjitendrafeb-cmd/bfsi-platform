Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the existing bank facilities and debt instruments of HDB Financial Services Limited (HDBFS).

 

Crisil Ratings has also withdrawn its rating on the non-convertible debentures of Rs 7,361.50 crore and subordinated debt of Rs 220 crore as these have been redeemed and Crisil Ratings has received independent verification that these instruments are fully redeemed. The withdrawal (See Annexure 'Details of Rating Withdrawn' for details) is in-line with its withdrawal policy.

 

The ratings of debt Instruments of HDBFS continue to reflect strategic importance to and expectation of continued support from its parent and majority owner HDFC Bank Ltd (HDFC Bank; 'Crisil AAA/Crisil AA+*/Stable/Crisil A1+'). The shared logo also enhances the moral obligation of HDFC Bank towards this entity. The ratings are also underpinned by the company's established presence in the retail finance space, and its healthy capitalisation. These strengths are partially offset by the company’s moderate, though stable, asset quality and earnings profile.

 

*Tier I Bonds

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has analysed the business and financial risk profiles of HDBFS. Crisil Ratings has also factored in the strong support that HDBFS is expected to receive from its parent, HDFC Bank, as and when required.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Majority ownership by, and strategic importance to HDFC Bank

HDBFS is of strategic importance to the bank (HDFC Bank) as it complements the latter's product portfolio, distribution network and also supports the collection activities for the retail portfolio of HDFC Bank. The shared logo also enhances the moral obligation of HDFC Bank towards this entity. Further, post the initial public offering in July 2025, HDFC Bank is still the majority shareholder of HDBFS and held 74.1% stake as on June 30, 2026. The company also benefits from regular funding support from HDFC Bank, in the form of equity and debt if required.

 

Established presence in the retail finance segment

HDBFS has emerged as one of the larger players in the retail financing space over the past few years with assets under management (AUM) of Rs 1,22,048 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs 1,18,733 crore as on March 31, 2026). The company caters primarily to underserved and underbanked customers in low to middle income households with minimal or no credit history. As a result, the company focuses primarily on Tier 2+ cities for business expansion. As on June 30, 2026, 71% of total company branches located in Tier 4+ towns.

 

The company has a diversified product base serving multiple credit needs of customers. As on June 30, 2026, the asset financing portfolio, which includes commercial vehicle, construction equipment, auto, tractor and gold loans constitute ~47% of the total AUM, while mortgage loans (loan against property) and unsecured loans (business loans/personal loans) account for ~24% and 20%, respectively. Furthermore, HDBFS also offers consumption loans (consumer durables, two-wheelers and micro finance) which have grown significantly over the past few years, increasing their share of the AUM to 9% as on June 30, 2026, from 3% as on March 31, 2019.

 

Healthy capital position

The capital position remained robust with overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR) of 21.3% as on June 30, 2026 (21.4% as on March 31, 2026), up from 19.2% as on March 31, 2025. The improvement in CAR was on account of the successful listing of the company in July 2025, wherein the company raised Rs 12,500 crore, comprising Rs 2,500 crore fresh issue and Rs 10,000 crore offer for sale (OFS). Consequently, the company's networth increased to Rs 21,317 crore as on June 30, 2026, (Rs 20,664 crore as on March 31, 2026) from Rs 15,820 crore as on March 31, 2025, while gearing reduced to 4.8 times (4.8 times) from 5.5 times during the period. Cushion for asset side risks remained adequate as reflected in networth coverage for net non-performing assets (NPAs) at 16.9 times as on June 30, 2026. Additionally, the capital position is supported by the steady internal accrual of the company.

 

While capital generation remains comfortable, the capital profile is also aided by ability to raise capital from the parent (HDFC Bank) as and when required.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Moderate, albeit stable asset quality and earnings profile

Overall asset quality metrics exhibited improvement between fiscals 2023 and 2024 following the overhang of Covid-19; however, the company’s reported gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) witnessed some deterioration, thereafter, increasing to 2.8% as on December 31, 2025, from 2.3% as on March 31, 2025, and 1.9% as on March 31, 2024, primarily due to elevated delinquencies in the vehicle financing and unsecured business loan portfolios. Nevertheless, the asset quality metrics are showing signs of recovery as GNPAs improved to 2.3% as on June 30, 2026, from 2.4% as on March 31, 2026. The provisioning coverage on non-performing loans remained modest and stood at 55.7% as on June 30, 2026, resulting in net NPA of 1.0% as on the same date.

 

Improvement in asset quality metrics led to normalisation in the credit costs to 2.2% of average total assets for first quarter of fiscal 2027 compared with 2.4% in fiscal 2026 (2.1% in fiscal 2025). However, this is partially offset by marginal reduction in other income (2.1% of average total assets in first quarter of fiscal 2027 as against 2.2% in fiscal 2026 and 2.4% in fiscal 2025) due to decline in the BPO income. Nevertheless, the earnings profile is supported by healthy interest margin given the relatively higher yielding segments the company operates in. The net interest margin (NIM) expanded to 8.0% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 from 7.7% in fiscal 2026 (7.4% in fiscal 2025). This was primarily on account of reduction in total interest expenses on account of 125 basis points (bps) repo rate cut. Furthermore, operating expenses have been on an improving trend and stood at 4.6% of average total assets in first quarter of fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2026 as against 4.8% in fiscal 2025. Resultantly, the company’s return on assets (RoA) increased to 2.5% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 from 2.2% in fiscal 2026 (2.2% in fiscal 2025).

Liquidity Superior

HDBFS’s asset–liability management profile had positive cumulative mismatches across all buckets as on June 30, 2026. Further, as on this date, liquidity was adequate in the form of cash and bank balances, investments in mutual funds and government securities (face value) of Rs 5,664 crore and unutilised bank lines of Rs 5,353 crore aggregating to Rs 11,017 crore which is sufficient to meet the next month’s debt obligation. The company’s liquidity is further cushioned by healthy inflows from assets, option to securitise loans and funding support from HDFC Bank, if required.

ESG Profile

Crisil Ratings believes that HDBFS’s Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) profile supports its already strong credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile for financial sector entities typically factors in governance as a key differentiator. The sector has reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base, and it can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector does not have a direct adverse environmental impact, the lending decisions may have a bearing on environment.

 

HDBFS has a continuous focus on strengthening various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

HDBFS’s key ESG highlights: 

 

  • The company has lower than peer average energy consumption intensity (~0.4 MWh per employee) and Scope 1 and 2 emission intensity (0.3 tCO2e per employee).
  • The company continues to enhance its environmental performance through initiatives such as water stewardship, sustainable resource management, energy efficiency improvements, emissions reduction, and green infrastructure development.
  • Majority of the company’s lending is exposed to vehicle financing which has an adverse impact on the environment. However, the remaining of the loans are directed towards loan against property, gold loans and business loans which has limited direct environmental impacts.
  • The company has developed an ESG framework which covers its commitment towards climate change mitigation and procurement strategies.
  • Lower-than-peer average customer complaints intensity (~0.2 complaints per Rs crore of loans and advances) coupled with ~97% redressal rate, and nil instances of data breach. Also, the company has an extensive network with ~71% branches catering to customers in Tier 4 and beyond areas.
  • Its governance structure is characterized by adequate board size with 78% being independent directors and 22% being women directors, coupled with split in chairman and CEO positions and independent chairman on board.

 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. HDBFS commitment to ESG principles will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given the sizable share of market borrowings in its overall debt and access to both domestic and foreign capital markets.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings factors in strong financial support for HDBFS from HDFC Bank. On a standalone basis, HDBFS should maintain its strong position in the retail finance space.

 

The rating on perpetual bonds remains sensitive to the capital buffer maintained by HDBFS, over regulatory capital requirements, and rating transition on these instruments could potentially be sharper than those on other debt instruments and bank facilities.

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward factors:

  • Downward change in the credit risk profile of HDFC Bank by 1 notch could have a similar rating change on HDBFS
  • Diminution in expected support from HDFC Bank, caused by a significant decline in the bank's ownership, or in strategic importance of HDBFS to HDFC Bank

About the Company

HDBFS was set up as a non-banking finance company by HDFC Bank in June 2007. The company began operations in fiscal 2008. As on June 30, 2026, HDFC Bank owned 74.1% of HDBFS’s equity shares.  On the same date, the company had 1,710 branches across 1,165 cities in India. Apart from the lending business, HDBFS is also engaged in the distribution of general and life insurance products for HDFC Ergo General Insurance Company and HDFC Standard Life Insurance Company, respectively. The company also runs BPO services that undertake collection services, back office and sales support functions under a contract with HDFC Bank.

 

HDBFS reported profit after tax of Rs 2,544 crore on total income (net of interest expenses) of Rs 11,609 crore in fiscal 2026 as against Rs 2,176 crore and Rs 9,910 crore, respectively, in fiscal 2025.

 

For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the company reported profit after tax of Rs 785 crore on total income (net of interest expenses) of Rs 3,185 crore as against Rs 568 crore and 2,726 crore, respectively, for the corresponding period of previous fiscal.

For details on Crisil Ratings' use of 'PP-MLD' please refer to the notes to Rating scale for Debt Instruments and Structured Finance Instruments at the following link: https://www.crisilratings.com/en/home/our-business/ratings/credit-ratings-scale.html
