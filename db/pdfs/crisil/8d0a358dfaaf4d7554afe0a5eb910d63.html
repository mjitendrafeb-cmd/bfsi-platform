Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Provisional Crisil AAA (SO)’ rating to Series A1 pass-through certificates (PTCs) and ‘Provisional Crisil BBB+ (SO)’ rating to Equity tranche PTCs issued by Peter PSL CV Trust June 2026 under a securitisation transaction backed by comercial vehicle (CV) loan receivables originated by IndoStar Capital Finance Limited (IndoStar; ‘Crisil AA-/Stable/Crisil A1+’).

 

The ratings are based on credit enhancement available to the PTCs, the expected credit quality of underlying receivables, origination and servicing capabilities of IndoStar, the transaction’s payment mechanism and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

Payment structure

The transaction has Par with turbo amortisation structure. IndoStar will assign the pool to Peter PSL CV Trust June 2026, a trust settled by Axis Trustee Services Limited, which will, in turn, issue Series A1 PTCs and Equity Tranche PTCs to investors in exchange for purchase consideration of 91.0% and 9.0% of the initial pool principal, respectively. IndoStar will continue to service the underlying loan pool.

 

Series A1 PTC holders are promised timely interest payments on a monthly basis. Principal repayment, while expected on a monthly basis, is promised only on ultimate basis by the instrument’s final maturity date.

 

Equity Tranche PTCs are fully subordinated to Series A1 PTCs. Equity tranche PTC investors are expected to receive residual excess interest spread (EIS), if any, on a monthly basis; however, the rating on Equity tranche PTCs only addresses the likelihood of principal repayment and not payment of residual EIS amount.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for Series A1 PTCs are supported by cash collateral, subordination of rated equity tranche and subordination of EIS. On a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest payments in case of shortfall in collection from the pool to Series A1 PTCs. On the final maturity date of Series A1 PTCs, the cash collateral can also be used to make the promised principal repayment in case of shortfall in collection from the pool.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction is as follows:

  • External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 2.23 crore (5.0% of the initial pool principal), which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits.
  • Internal credit enhancement (assuming no prepayments) of Rs 13.43 crore, of which equity tranche principal amounts to Rs 4.01 crore (9.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS amounts to Rs 9.42 crore (21.2% of the initial pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs.
  • Subordination of scheduled EIS of Rs 9.42 crore (21.2% of the initial pool principal) for the principal repayment of Equity Tranche PTCs.

 

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provide loss absorption against stressed shortfall in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit enhancement available in the structure:
    •                  External credit enhancement from a cash collateral amounting to Rs 2.23 crore (5.0% of the initial pool principal), which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits.
    •                  Internal credit enhancement (assuming no prepayments) of Rs 13.43 crore, of which equity tranche principal amounts to Rs 4.01 crore (9.0% of the initial pool principal) and subordination of scheduled EIS of Rs 9.42 crore (21.2% of the initial pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs.
    •                  Subordination of scheduled EIS of Rs 9.42 crore (21.2% of the initial pool principal) for the principal repayment of Equity Tranche PTCs.

 

  • Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
    •                  The contracts in the pool have a weighted average seasoning (number of instalments paid) of 11.4 months, and consequently, the pool was amortised by 27.2% as of the cut-off date on August 31, 2026.

 

Weaknesses:

  • Potential macroeconomic headwinds:
    •                  Borrowers in the underlying pool may come under pressure due to challenging macroeconomic environment. Headwinds such as increased fuel costs, increasing interest rates and moderation in demand on account of inflation and geopolitical uncertainties may hamper pool collection ratios. 

 

  • Geographic concentration:
    •                  The pool is moderately concentrated in terms of geography. The top three states—Tamil Nadu (30.9%), Madhya Pradesh (21.6%) and Rajasthan (8.8%)—account for 61.3% of the pool principal.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: None.
  • For Equity Tranche PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) exceeding 1.4 times the estimated base case shortfall.

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) below 2.5 times the estimated base case shortfalls
  • For Equity Tranche PTCs: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) falling below 1.3 times the estimated base case shortfall
  • Downgrade in the rating of the servicer/originator.
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating.

Liquidity: Strong for Series A1 PTCs and Adequate for Equity Tranche PTCs

For Series A1 PTCs - Liquidity is strong given that the credit enhancement (internal and external combined) in the structure is above 1.5 times the estimated base shortfall on the residual pool cash flow.

 

For Equity Tranche PTCs - Liquidity is adequate given that the credit enhancement (internal and external combined) in the structure is above 1.1 times the estimated base shortfall on the residual pool cash flow.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cashflows

The transaction is backed by receivables from a pool of CV loans originated by IndoStar. The pool’s key characteristics as of the cut-off date (August 31, 2026) are outlined below:

  • The contracts in the pool pertain to vehicle loans originated by IndoStar.
  • All the contracts in the pool are current as of cut-off date.
  • The minimum seasoning of contracts is eight monthly paid instalments.
  • Pool loans have seen a weighted average seasoning of 11.4 months prior to securitisation, during which the total disbursed amount for pool loans has amortised by 27.2%.
  • The average disbursement amount for pool loans was Rs 4.60 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 18.9% and weighted average original tenure of 43.0 months.

 

Furthermore, the originator also represented the following:

  • None of the loans in the pool has been restructured or rescheduled.
  • The loans are not hypothecated to any lender and do not have any encumbrances on the date of securitisation.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTCs investors are taking a direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool specific characteristics.
  • To assess the base case shortfalls for the portfolio, Crisil Ratings has analysed the static pool performance (with information on 90+ delinquencies) of loans originated by IndoStar Capital during the first quarter of fiscal 2023 to the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (with performance data till June 2026). Crisil Ratings has also analysed the dynamic portfolio delinquencies of IndoStar’s farm equipment loan book. As of June 2026, the 90+ delinquency for CVs was 4.3%. Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which include seasoning profile and repayment track record, original tenure, interest rate and loan-to-value. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in risk arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.
  • Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. Prepayment risk has been assessed based on historically observed levels of prepayments for similar pools.

 

Assumptions:

  • After making adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is 6.0–8.0% of pool cash flow.
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5–1.0% has also been applied to the pool cash flow.

About the company- Originator/Servicer profile

Incorporated in July 2009, IndoStar is registered with the Reserve Bank of India as a systemically important, non-deposit taking, non-banking financial company. The company was founded and incorporated by private equity players (Everstone, Goldman Sachs, Baer Capital Partners, ACPI Investment managers and CDIB International) with initial capital of around Rs 900 crore. In May 2020, Brookfield invested Rs 1,225 crore and became the largest shareholder and promoter. Brookfield holds 56.20% stake in the company, followed by the Everstone group at 17.4%.

 

The company started operations as a wholesale financier in fiscal 2011 and entered the SME finance (loans against property) segment in fiscal 2015. In fiscal 2018, it started offering vehicle finance and housing finance (through wholly owned subsidiary, IndoStar Home Finance Pvt Ltd). In fiscal 2019, IndoStar Capital acquired the CV finance business of IIFL Finance Ltd.

 

The company plans to focus on CV financing and gradually build the micro-LAP portfolio. As on March 31, 2026, the company had an AUM of Rs 8,056 crore which consisted of vehicle loans (93%), micro LAP (3%), SME loans (3%) and corporate loans (1%)

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

As per indicative transaction terms, the key points on the role of the servicer to be covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

 

  • The trustee, acting for and on behalf of the investors, shall appoint the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the receivables into the Collection and Payment Account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.
