Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AAA/stable’ rating to Rs 250 crore bond of NABKISAN Finance Ltd (NABKISAN) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’ rating on the existing bank facility and subordinated debt.

 

The rating continue to centrally factors in the expectation of strong financial, operational and managerial support to NABKISAN from its parent, National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD; rated ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’), both on an ongoing basis and in the event of distress. NABKISAN is strategically important to NABARD as it furthers NABARD’s objective by expanding the impact in rural and agricultural development. NABARD’s support is demonstrated by articulation of its intention to maintain majority shareholding in NABKISAN and sustain management oversight so that the company remains well-capitalised for its business needs and is compliant with all applicable contractual and regulatory requirements, including discharging its debt and other obligations in a timely manner. Furthermore, brand association and infrastructural synergies, along with management oversight and monitoring by the parent on an ongoing basis, strengthen integration of NABKISAN with NABARD.

 

The rating also continues to factor in NABKISAN’s healthy capitalisation and adequate profitability, though its sustenance will be monitored. These strengths are partially offset by modest scale of overall operations and inherent vulnerability of asset quality. .

 

Crisil Ratings also takes note of the consummation of the merger of NABARD’s erstwhile subsidiary, NABSAMRUDDHI Finance Ltd with NABKISAN on December 16, 2025. NABSAMRUDDHI was engaged in financing small and mid-sized non-bank finance companies and micro finance institutions. After the consummation of the merger, NABSAMRUDDHI has ceased to exist and all operations are now being carried out under NABKISAN.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone credit risk profile of NABKISAN and has factored in expectation of support that NABKISAN is to receive from NABARD, both on an ongoing basis and in the event of exigencies.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Strategic importance to, and expectation of strong support from, NABARD

NABARD, as the apex financial institution, implements the Government of India’s policies on planning and credit dispensation to agricultural and rural sectors. It has a mandate of providing and regulating credit and other facilities to drive impact in agriculture and rural development.

 

NABKISAN is strategically important to NABARD as it furthers the parent’s objective by expanding its impact in rural and agricultural development to the grassroots level. NABARD, along with other shareholders, has infused capital in NABKISAN as and when required. NABARD has infused equity of Rs ~562 crore till date in NABKISAN . The parent has also extended refinance lines along with credit guarantee and grants to NABKISAN. While majority of the borrowings of NABKISAN (as on June 30, 2026) are from NABARD, NABKISAN has also started  borrowing  from external sources and also has tapped into capital market.

 

NABARD continues to provide strong strategic and managerial support to NABKISAN in the form of board representation wherein three of the eight board members are from NABARD and also by  monitoring growth strategies and performance through a dedicated department i.e. Department of Subsidiaries and Strategic Investments. The board is led by independent chairman, two independent directors, one nominee director from NABARD, Government of Tamil Nadu and Indian bank each and one NABKISAN’s management representation,Mr G Immanuvel (Managing Director. Also, majority of the key management personnel at NABKISAN are on deputation from NABARD.

 

NABKISAN also benefits from infrastructural synergies with NABARD. NABKISAN taps into the FPO(Farmer Producer Organization) ecosystem promoted by NABARD to source leads and by nominating FPOs who have borrowed from NABKISAN, in case the FPO required guidance and support. NABKISAN also shares premises in NABARD’s regional branches and undertakes its sourcing and monitoring-related operations through these branches.

 

NABARD holds 90.12% of stake as on August 31, 2026 in NABKISAN and is committed to hold the majority stake in the company in the long term. Crisil Ratings, therefore, believes that the extensive managerial, operational, and financial linkages, along with the strategic importance, and majority holding implies strong moral obligation on the part of NABARD to support NABKISAN on an ongoing basis and in exigencies. Crisil Ratings believes that NABARD will continue to have majority ownership in, and management control of NABKISAN, in the long term.

 

Healthy capitalisation

Capitalisation continues to be healthy in respect to the company’s scale. Also, the merger of NABSAMRUDDHI with NABKISAN has further enhanced NABKISAN’s capitalisation with reported networth of Rs 1047 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs 1016 crore as on March 31, 2026) as compared to Rs 532 crore as on March 31, 2025. Overall capital adequacy ratio stood at 22.8% as on June 30, 2026 (22.26% as on March 31, 2026). The gearing stood at 5.5 times as on June 30, 2026  (6.0 times as on March 31, 2026). On a steady state basis, NABKISAN plans to keep gearing below 6 times.  NABARD has infused equity capital of around Rs 284 crore in August 2026, which will also support capitalisation. Nevertheless, capitalisation is expected to remain healthy, backed by steady cash accrual, and capital support from the parent as and when required.

 

Adequate profitability; sustenance to be monitored

NABKISAN reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 31 crore and Rs 132 crore  and return on average assets (RoMA) of 1.7% and 2.1%  for quarter one  of fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2026 respectively. Post merger, RoMA has moderated from 2.5% in fiscal 2025.  The moderation in profitability is driven by a reduction in total income (net of interest expense) to 3.2% in first three months of fiscal 2026 from 3.7% in fiscal 2025, primarily due to rise in cost of funds. The company’s credit costs and operating expenditure, however, remained under control at less than 1% during fiscal 2026. The company’s operating efficiency benefits from centralised operations and shared infrastructure with parent. Going forward, the company’s ability to improve and sustain profitability while scaling up portfolio will remain monitorable.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Modest scale of overall operations

The company was initially focused on extending credit for promotion, expansion and commercialisation of enterprises in agriculture, allied and rural non-farm activities. However, in 2018, the NABARD Act was amended to include MSME (micro small medium enterprises) within its scope to drive rural development. This is when the company’s loan book started to scale.

 

Post merger, NABKISAN’s loan book grew to Rs 6,629 crore as on March 31, 2026 as against Rs 3068 crore as on March 31, 2025.The company’s loan book further grew by ~14%(annualized) in first quarter of fiscal 2027 and stood at Rs 6863 crore as on June 30, 2026.

 

Of the loan book as on June 30, 2026, ~92% is corporate lending wherein it finances mid-sized NBFCs and microfinance institutions, and the remaining is to FPOs and others. The company plans to scale up by diversifying into newer products in rural and agri value chain. Ability to substantially scale up over the medium term will be monitorable.

 

Inherent vulnerability of asset quality

NABKISAN’s overall gross stage III has improved to 0.9% as on June 30, 2026 and March 31, 2026 as against %1.3% as on   March 31, 2025. The Net non performing assets also stood comfortable at 0.4% as on June 30, 2026 as against 0.3% as on March 31, 2026  as compared to 0.2% as on March 31, 2025.

 

As on June 30, 2026, gross stage III of corporate book was 0.6% as against 0.3% as on March 31, 2026. The gross NPA in FPO is high given the risky borrower segment. However, this book is backed by guarantee extended by the Government of India, covering exposure of up to Rs 2 crore per FPO, wherein the risk is covered up to 85%. NABARD’s subsidiary, Nabsanrakshan Finance Ltd, manages this guarantee. 

 

That said, the company’s loan exposure to FPOs is risky as the exposure is to the agriculture segment, which is cyclical and the FPOs are in nascent stages with limited flexibility in cash flows. Therefore, the ability to maintain asset quality metrics as the portfolio scales up will remain a key monitorable.

Liquidity Superior

The company’s asset liability statement as on 30th June, 2026, has no negative mismatches in any of the time buckets. The company has unencumbered liquidity in the form of cash bank balances and liquid investments of ~Rs 293 crore as on August 31, 2026. Against this the company has debt obligation of ~212 crore in September and october2026. However, the company also has refinancing options available with NABARD. Moreover, the company on a monthly basis, collects ~Rs 300 crore on an average which further strengthens its liquidity position. Crisil Ratings believes that the company’s liquidity will also be supported by timely support from parent, in case of any exigencies.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believes NABKISAN will remain strategically important to the parent, and will continue to receive financial, managerial, and operational support from the parent on an ongoing basis. Furthermore, the company would continue to maintain healthy capitalisation. 

Rating sensitivity factors

Downward factors

  • Downgrade in the rating of NABARD by one or more notches
  • Material change in the parent’s shareholding (below 50%) in NABKISAN and/or change in support philosophy of NABARD towards NABKISAN.

About the Company

Incorporated in 1997, NABKISAN is a non-banking financial company (NBFC). It is a subsidiary of NABARD, which holds 90.2% with equity participation from the Governor of Tamil Nadu (3.5%), Indian Bank (1.9%), Government of Andhra Pradesh (1.5%), Government of Telangana (1.1%)Canara Bank (0.6%), Tamilnad Mercantile Bank (0.3%), Indian Overseas Bank (0.2%) and others. The company is engaged in lending to mid-sized non-banks and micro finance institutions to enable them to reach grassroots level by lending in rural areas to micro-, small-, and medium-sized enterprises, microfinance borrowers and individuals for income generation, agriculture and allied activities, rural housing, among others. The company’s key focus area is also to promote and extend financing to Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs). The company’s operations are centralised and it has established its footprints in 22 Indian states.
