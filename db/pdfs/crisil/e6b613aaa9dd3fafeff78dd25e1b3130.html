Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil A/Stable’ rating to the long term bank facilities and non convertible debentures (NCDs) of Share India Fincap Private Limited (SIFPL; part of the Share India group) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A/Stable’ rating on the existing NCDs. The company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Share India Securities Ltd (Share India; ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’), which is the flagship entity of the Share India group.

 

The rating centrally factors in the continued strategic importance of SIFPL to the parent and expectation of strong financial and managerial support from it, both on an ongoing basis and in the event of distress.  The rating also reflects benefits from the well-established position of Share India in the capital market segment and adequate capitalisation. These rating strengths are partially offset by modest scale of operations, limited track record in the small and medium enterprises (SME) segment and lack of diversity in the resource profile.

 

SIFPL is a non-banking financial company (NBFC), registered with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). It mainly offers micro loans for income generation to women entrepreneurs as well as business loans. The company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Share India and has been promoted by Rajesh Gupta, Praveen Gupta and Yash Pal Gupta. Currently, Rajesh Gupta (Director) and Aastha Gupta (Chief Executive Officer) manage the day-to-day operations.

 

In terms of scale of operations, the assets under management (AUM) as on June 30, 2026, stood at Rs 268.2 crore against Rs 265.3 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 260 crore as on March 31, 2025). There has been de-growth in the women entrepreneurship loan (WEL) portfolio due to slow-down in disbursements because of challenges faced by the micro finance industry during the last couple of years. Out of the overall portfolio, 37% was for WELs as on June 30, 2026, against 72% as on March 31, 2024.

 

Portfolio asset quality has improved over the last few quarters, amid challenges such as overleveraging, loan waiver schemes and adverse climatic conditions. While these issues have been industry-wide, SIFPL saw improvement in adjusted 90+ days past due (dpd) at 5.6% as on March 31, 2026, compared with 7.2% as on March 31, 2025. It further improved to 5.2% as on June 30, 2026.

 

Earnings were impacted during fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025 due to elevated credit costs. The company reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 10.2 crore on total income of Rs 61.7 crore in fiscal 2026 and PAT of Rs. 10.1 crore on a total income of Rs. 61.8 crore in fiscal 2025 as compared to profit of Rs 33.1 crore on total income of Rs 83 crore in fiscal 2024. As it is engaged in the micro finance segment, the ability to improve portfolio quality along with growing scale of operations will be a key rating sensitivity factor.

Analytical Approach

To arrive at the rating, Crisil Ratings has assessed the standalone financial and business risk profiles of SIFPL. The rating also factors in the company’s strategic importance to the Share India group and expectation of strong financial and managerial support from them.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Strategic importance to the parent and expectation of strong managerial and financial support

The rating factors in expectation of strong support from Share India, both on an ongoing basis and in the event of any exigency. SIFPL is the only NBFC arm of the Share India group. As part of its long-term strategy of providing end-to-end financial services under a single roof, the group has entered the financing space through SIFPL. Given the full ownership, shared name and common branding, Crisil Ratings believes Share India has a strong moral obligation to support SIFPL. Furthermore, the founders of the group, Rajesh Gupta, Praveen Gupta and Yash Pal Gupta are also on the board of SIFPL. The parent has the financial flexibility to infuse capital to support growth.

 

Adequate capital position

SIFPL is also adequately capitalised in relation to its current and expected scale and nature of operations. As on March 31, 2026, the company reported networth of Rs 183 crore and gearing of 0.5 time compared to Rs 123 crore and 1.2 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. Networth improved marginally to Rs 188 crore as on June 30, 2026, with gearing of 0.5 time. The promoters have infused capital and can infuse equity as and when required. It expects to maintain a steady-state gearing of around 3 times throughout. Any increase in gearing beyond the committed threshold will be a key rating sensitivity factor.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Small scale of operations with limited vintage, as indicated by geographical concentration

The scale has been improving, as reflected in loan portfolio of around Rs 268.2 crore and network of 68 branches as on June 30, 2026 (Rs 265.3 crore and 61 branches, respectively, as on March 31, 2024). Uttar Pradesh and Punjab together account for over 85% of AUM as on March 31, 2026. However, during the last and current fiscals, the management has opened new branches in Bihar, Odisha and Madhya Pradesh. The company is in the process of expanding its operations to different states, including Maharashtra, which is also the reason for elevated operating expenditure. The ability to grow its scale and diversify to other regions/states will be critical in mitigating concentration risk going forward. 

 

Modest asset quality

The share of adjusted 90+dpd in overall AUM improved to 5.2% and 5.6% as on June 30, 2026 and March 31, 2026, respectively from 7.2% on March 31, 2025 (5.2% as on March 31, 2024). Over the past few quarters, asset quality has been impacted by issues such as increase in average customer indebtedness, heightened attrition and other challenges faced by the industry. However, there have been improvements in overall collection efficiency during the last few months.

 

The asset quality has shown a steady improvement over the past few quarters, with credit costs (as a % of total managed assets) declining to 4.1% (annualised) as on June 30, 2026, from 5.8% for fiscal 2026 and 5.6% a year ago. Decline in credit costs improved the overall profitability at a return on managed assets (RoMA) level of 6.8% (annualised) for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 (reported profit of Rs 4.8 crore) compared to 1.9% (reported profit of Rs 1.4 crore) in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. In fiscal 2026, the company reported PAT of Rs 10.2 crore with RoMA of 3.7%. 

 

Susceptibility to local socio-political issues inherent in the microfinance sector 

A significant portion of the company’s portfolio comprises WEL under the joint-liability group (JLG) mechanism. Its customers generally have below-average credit risk profiles with lack of access to formal credit. Such borrowers are typically farmers, tailors, cattle owners/traders, small vegetable vendors, teashop owners and dairy farmers. The incomes of these households could be volatile and dependent on the performance of the local economy.

 

The microfinance sector has witnessed various events over the years, including regulatory and legislative challenges, that have disrupted operations. Some of these events include the Andhra crisis, demonetisation in 2016, Covid-19 pandemic and sociopolitical issues in certain states. These events adversely affected the sector, elevated delinquencies and hurt the profitability and capitalisation metrics of NBFC-MFIs. These challenges underscore the vulnerability of the microfinance business model to external risks. Covid-19 introduced new challenges, aggravating existing vulnerabilities in the microfinance sector by heightening credit risks and the likelihood of loan default by borrowers. While the sector has navigated these events, it remains susceptible to issues, including local elections, natural calamities and borrower protests, which may increase delinquencies for a while. Nevertheless, the company was able to manage its portfolio well without any significant impact on recoveries. However, MFIs remain vulnerable to socially sensitive factors and the macroeconomic scenario. Furthermore, the sector is regulated by multiple bodies which, from time to time, have been providing several directives to maintain credit discipline and avoid over indebtedness for borrowers.

Liquidity Adequate

As on June 30, 2026, the asset-liability maturity (ALM) profile was comfortable with positive cumulative mismatches in the up to one-year bucket. The liquidity stood at Rs 6.05 crore (excluding undrawn term loan) as on June 30, 2026, against debt obligation of Rs 3.61 crore for the next one month and Rs 10.83 crore in the next three months. This represents a liquidity position of 1.67 times (assuming nil collections) for next month and a liquidity position at a cover of 0.55 times  considering operating expenses and debt repayment over the next two months through August 2026.

Outlook Stable

SIFPL is expected to receive strong financial and managerial support from its parent over the medium term, while maintaining adequate capitalisation.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors:

  • Upward revision in the rating of the parent, translating into similar revision in rating of SIFPL
  • Substantial scale up in operations, along with stable asset quality (90+ dpd) below 2% on  sustained basis

 

Downward factors:

  • Downward revision in the credit rating of the parent or any change in its support stance
  • Weaker asset quality exerting pressure on profitability
  • Increase in gearing to over 5 times on steady-state basis.

About the Company

SIFPL (erstwhile, Windpipe Finvest Pvt Ltd), was incorporated on December 24, 1996, as an NBFC with the RBI. The company is a wholly owned subsidiary of Share India. It provides various financial services such as WEL, small and micro enterprise loans and loans against securities.

 

SIFPL started operations in 2000, by offering vehicle finance. The company is mainly funded by loans from directors and related parties. In 2019, it started offering unsecured business loans and WEL. The average ticket size is Rs 5 lakh and Rs 40,000, respectively, for these loans. WEL is extended to women in tier-III cities and rural areas. This segment has around 64,000 clients, who are managed by a network of 68 branches across Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Bihar and Madhya Pradesh.

About the Group

Incorporated in 1994, the Share India group provides a wide array of financial services such as stock broking, commodity broking, mutual fund distribution, currency derivatives broking, portfolio management and research analysis to its retail and institutional clients. It is a registered member of the National Stock Exchange (NSE; both cash and F&O segments) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), Multi Commodity Exchange of India Ltd (MCX), National Commodity & Derivatives Exchange Ltd (NCDEX), Indian Commodity Exchange of India Ltd (ICEX) and depository participant of National Securities Depositories Ltd (NSDL) and Central Securities Depositories Ltd (CDSL).

 

Since 2019, the group has started expanding its business portfolio. Merger with the Total group in 2019 helped expand the business significantly. The group has also diversified its portfolio by entering mutual fund and insurance distribution and lending and merchant banking services. To further scale up and diversify revenue, the group acquired three fintech companies, mainly Algo wire Trading Technologies and U trade solutions. With these acquisitions, the group intends to provide financial analytics, advisory and trading platforms for retail clients.
