Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has converted its provisional rating on Series A1 pass-through certificates (PTCs) issued by Adheera 07 2026 to a final rating of 'Crisil A (SO)' under a securitisation transaction originated by Dvara Kshetriya Gramin Financial Services Private Limited (Dvara KGFS; ‘Crisil BBB+/Stable’), backed by a pool of unsecured joint liability group (JLG) loan receivables and microenterprise loan (MEL) receivables.

 

The rating is based on credit support available to PTCs, credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, origination and servicing capabilities of the originator and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

 

Crisil Ratings has received the following legal/executed documents for this transaction. Amended and restated Declaration of trust was executed in place of the Trust deed. Series A PTCs were issued in place of Series A1 PTCs. However, these changes are not material in nature and do not have any impact on the credit risk profile of the instrument. Other terms of the documents are in line with the transaction terms envisaged at the time of the provisional rating. Hence, Crisil Ratings has converted the provisional rating to a final rating.

Payment structure

The transaction has a Par with Excess Interest Spread (EIS) structure. Dvara KGFS has assigned the loan pool to Adheera 07 2026, a trust settled by IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited (CTL), which has issued instruments to investors in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 90.0% of future pool principal outstanding as on the cut-off date (June 30, 2026).

 

Transaction structure also includes trigger based turbo amortisation where 100% EIS would be utilized for amortization of Series A1 PTCs principal till the tranche maturity, if any of the following events take place – 1) the PAR 90 in the Pool exceeds 5% of the initial pool principal, 2) Collection efficiency of the pool is less than 95% for any 6 consecutive payouts.

 

Series A1 PTC holders are promised timely interest on a monthly basis and principal on ultimate basis on the maturity date of the PTCs. Investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral, principal subordination and subordination of EIS.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for PTCs are supported by cash collateral and principal subordination and EIS. On a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest payments in case of shortfall in collection from the pool to Series A1 PTCs. On the Series A1 PTCs final maturity date, the cash collateral can also be used to make the promised principal repayment in case of shortfall in collection from the pool.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure for Series A1 PTCs:

  • Series A1 PTCs are supported by internal credit enhancement subordination, which includes overcollateralisation amounting to Rs 5.14 crore (10.0% of the initial pool principal), and subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 8.24 crore (16.0% of initial pool principal).
  • The PTCs are also supported by external credit enhancement from cash collateral of Rs 3.60 crore (7.0% of the initial pool principal) in the form of a fixed deposit created in the name of the trust.

 

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS and principal subordination; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provides loss absorption against stressed shortfall in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit support available in the structure
  • External cash collateral in the structure amounting to Rs 3.60 crore (7.0% of the pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs
  • Internal credit enhancement assuming zero prepayment aggregating Rs 13.38 crore (26.0% of the initial pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs – including principal subordination of Rs 5.14 crore (10.0% of the pool principal) and EIS of Rs 8.24 crore (16.0% of the pool principal).
  • Seasoning of contracts in the pool
  • The pool has weighted average seasoning of 9.9 paid instalments and pre-securitisation amortisation of 28.8%.
  • All the contracts in the underlying loan pool are current as of the cut-off date (June 30, 2026).
  • Legal soundness of the transaction structure
  • The legal structure envisaged for the transaction entails bankruptcy remoteness of the pool of receivables, credit enhancement from the originator and adherence to regulations on securitisations.
  • These are certified through an independent legal opinion from an external legal counsel.

 

Weaknesses:

  • High geographic concentration risk
  • The pool is geographically concentrated with the top state, Tamil Nadu, and the top 10 districts concentration at 30.0% and 38.5%, respectively, of the initial pool principal.
  • Vulnerability to socio-political risks
  • Borrower cash flow in the unsecured MEL segment remains susceptible to risks arising out of socio-political issues and policy / regulatory changes. Such events can disrupt loan repayment ability of the borrowers.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • For Series A1 PTC: Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 1.75 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool due to sustained healthy collection from the pool.
  • A sharp upgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator.

 

Downward factors

  • For Series A1 PTCs: Credit enhancement available in the structure failing to cover 1.6 times the estimated adjusted base shortfall due to weaker-than-expected collection performance of the pool.
  • A sharp downgrade in the rating of the servicer or originator.
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity: Strong

Liquidity remains strong as the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the estimated base shortfall.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cash flow

The transaction is backed by JLG loans and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS. The key pool characteristics are as follows:

  • The contracts in the pool have weighted average seasoning of 9.9 monthly paid instalments, which has led to principal amortisation of 28.8% as of the pool cut-off date.
  • The pool is moderately concentrated in terms of geography, with the top three states and top 10 districts contributing 62.6% and 38.5%, respectively, of the initial pool principal.
  • The average ticket size for contracts in the pool is Rs 1.19 lakh, with a weighted average interest rate of 28.4%.

 

All the contracts in the underlying loan pool were current as of the cut-off date.

Rating assumptions

Background:

PTC investors are taking direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. The credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected in the portfolio, which are adjusted for pool-specific characteristics.

 

To assess the base case collection shortfalls for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed the portfolio delinquency movement (with information on 30+ and 90+ delinquencies) of JLG and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS during March 2018 to April 2026, with performance till April 2026. Crisil Ratings has also analysed delinquency performance in various geographies. As of April 2026, the 30+ delinquency for Dvara’s JLG loan portfolio was 9.5% and 90+ delinquency was 8.8%, while for the MEL portfolio the 30+ delinquency was 3.6% and 90+ delinquency was 2.5%.

 

Base case shortfalls on the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which includes seasoning profile and repayment track record, parameters such as original tenure, interest rate and geography (state/district). Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in risks arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.

 

Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. The prepayment risk has been assessed based on historical levels of prepayments for similar pools.

 

Assumptions:

  • After making the adjustments on the above factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is 11.0–13.0% of the pool cash flow.
  • Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5–1.5% has also been applied to the pool cash flow.

About the company- originator / servicer profile

Dvara KGFS was set up in fiscal 2008 by Dvara Trust for extending unsecured and secured loans to rural areas in the country. The company was founded by Bindu Ananth and Nachiket Mor. Dvara KGFS focuses on extending multiple financial products for lending, savings and insurance requirements of individuals in rural areas. The company positions itself as a rural wealth manager providing loans and financial products to customers. It largely extends JLG and unsecured enterprise loans, which account for more than 95% of the total loans. The JLG loans are up to ticket size of Rs 50,000 and enterprise loans have ticket size up to Rs 5 lakh. The company lends at interest rate of 24.5–27.75% for the JLG loans and 26–29% for the enterprise loans.

 

In fiscal 2019, the business correspondents and corporate agency business of the IFMR Rural Channels and Services Private Limited (holding company of Dvara KGFS) and IFMR Holdings Private Limited (ultimate holding company) were amalgamated with the company.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

  • The trustee, acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint, the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the Receivables into the Collection and Payment Account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction document.
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer: The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors) as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.

Performance of outstanding rated transactions

Crisil Ratings has ratings outstanding on multiple securitisation transactions backed by JLG and MEL loans originated by Dvara KGFS. For the pools with amortisation greater than 40.0%, cumulative collection ratios were 95.8–99.1% as of May 2026 payouts.
