Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has converted its provisional rating on Series A pass-through certificates (PTCs) to a final rating of ‘Crisil AAA (SO)’ and its provisional credit opinion to a final credit opinion of ‘Crisil A (SO) Equivalent’ for the second loss facility. This securitisation transaction is backed by receivables from vehicle, construction equipment and three-wheeler loans originated by Cholamandalam Investment Finance Company Limited (CIFCL; ‘Crisil A1+’).

 

The rating/credit opinion is based on the credit support available to the PTCs, the credit quality of underlying receivables, origination and servicing capabilities of CIFCL, the payment mechanism and the soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

 

Crisil Ratings has now received the final legal/executed documents for this transaction. Deed of assignment of receivables in the process of securitisation was executed in place of Deed of assignment. However, this is not a material change and does not impact the credit risk profile of the instrument. The other terms of the executed documents are in line with the transaction terms envisaged at the time of the provisional rating. Hence, Crisil Ratings has converted the provisional rating to a final rating.

Payment structure

The transaction has a Par with excess interest spread (EIS) structure. CIFCL has assigned the pool to Platinum Trust Jul 2026 – Tranche I, which has issued the PTCs to investors. Series A PTC payouts are supported by credit collateral in the form of fixed deposits and EIS. SBICAP Trustee Company Limited has been appointed to monitor the transaction on behalf of the PTC investors.

 

The transaction has a ‘Par with Excess Interest Spread (EIS)’ structure. Principal and Interest payments to Series A PTCs are promised on a monthly basis. The cash collateral would  be used to meet shortfalls in monthly promised Series A PTCs payouts as set out in the waterfall mechanism.

 

Investor payouts for Series A PTCs are supported by cash collateral and subordination of EIS. The residual cash flow or EIS cannot be used to top-up utilised external cash collateral. Residual cash flow will flow back to the originator. CIFCL will continue to service loan contracts in the pool as the servicing agent.

 

Crisil Ratings has estimated base case shortfall in the pool at 3.0–5.0% of the cash flow. This shortfall is further stressed to evaluate the adequacy of credit enhancement and arrive at the rating of PTCs. The total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provides loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

Investor payouts for the PTCs are supported by cash collateral and subordination of EIS.

 

On a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised payments (principal and interest) in case of shortfall in collection from the pool to Series A PTCs.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure to support PTC payouts:

  • External credit enhancement of Rs 91.01 crore (6.5% of the pool principal or 5.1% of the pool cash flow), of which first loss facility of Rs 70.01 crore (5.0% of the pool principal) is in the form of a fixed deposit and second loss facility of Rs 21.00 crore (1.5% of the pool principal) is in the form of a fixed deposit / bank guarantee.
  • Internal credit enhancement from subordination of scheduled EIS amounting to Rs 186.68 crore (13.3% of the pool principal securitised, as of the pool cut-off date, assuming zero prepayment).

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

  • Credit enhancement available in the structure:
    • Credit collateral of Rs 91.01 crore (6.5% of the pool principal or 5.1% of the pool cash flow) provides credit support to Series A PTCs. The PTCs also benefit from internal credit support through scheduled EIS (assuming zero prepayment) aggregating to Rs 186.68 crore (13.3% of the pool principal).
  • Repayment track record of pool borrowers:
    • The pool has a weighted average seasoning of 13.6 months, considering pool cut-off date of June 30, 2026.

 

Weaknesses:

  • Higher internal rate of return (IRR) contracts in the pool
    • Around 46.7% of the pool principal comes from contracts having IRR more than 16.0%, which is performing weaker on the vehicle finance portfolio

 

Crisil Ratings has adequately factored in these aspects in its rating analysis.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

For Series A PTCs :

  • None, given the credit ratings on the Series A PTCs are at the highest level

 

For Second Loss Facility:

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeds 1.5 times the estimated base case shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool

 

Downward factors

For Series A PTCs:

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) falling below 2.3 times the estimated base case shortfall

 

For Second Loss Facility:

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) falling below 1.4 times the estimated base case shortfall
  • Downgrade in the rating of the servicer/originator
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity: Strong

Liquidity is strong given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the currently estimated base shortfall.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cash flow

Salient features of the pool as on the cut-off date (June 30, 2026) based on the loan-level information submitted to Crisil Ratings:

  • The contracts in the pool pertain to vehicle loans originated by CIFCL.
  • The contracts accounting for 99.3% of the pool principal have been current for the past six months as on the cut-off date (June 30, 2026).
  • The minimum seasoning of the contracts is four months.
  • Furthermore, the originator has represented the following:
  • None of the loans in the pool have been restructured or rescheduled.
  • The loans are not hypothecated to any lender and do not have any encumbrances on the date of securitisation.

 

Assuming no prepayment, the cash flow schedule results in subordination in the form of EIS amounting to to Rs 186.68 crore (13.3% of the pool principal securitised). The portfolio performance of CIFCL has been highlighted in the Rating assumptions section. Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provides loss absorption against stressed shortfall in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • PTC investors are taking direct exposure on the repayment ability of the underlying borrowers in the pool. Credit risk in the transaction is factored through the base case shortfalls expected on the portfolio, which are further adjusted for pool-specific characteristics.
  • To assess the base case shortfalls for the portfolio, Crisil Ratings has analysed the asset class-wise static pool performance (with information on 90+ delinquencies) of new and used vehicle loans originated by CIFCL during fiscal 2014 to the third qurater of fiscal 2026 (with performance data till March 2026). Crisil Ratings has also analysed the dynamic portfolio delinquencies of CIFCL’s portfolio across various portfolio segments. As of March 2026, the 90+ delinquency (as % of managed assets) for CIFCL’s vehicle finance portfolio was 3.8%. Base case shortfalls in the portfolio are adjusted based on pool characteristics – which includes seasoning profile and repayment track record, as well as parameters such as original tenure, interest rate and loan-to-value. Crisil Ratings has additionally factored in risks arising from borrower and geographic concentration in the pool.
  • Prepayment is a form of market risk which will result in the reduction of EIS in the transaction. Prepayment risk has been assessed based on historical levels of prepayments for similar pools. 

 

Assumptions:

  • After making the adjustments for these factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is 3.0–5.0% of the pool cash flow.
  • The monthly prepayment rate of 0.2–0.5% has also been applied to the pool’s cash flow.

About the company- originator/servicer profile

Part of the Chennai-based Murugappa group, CIFCL was incorporated in 1978. The company provides vehicle financing and loans against property (LAP) as well as home loans, micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) and agricultural loans. It ventured into new businesses in consumer and MSME ecosystems, namely CSEL, SBPL and SME finance in the second half of fiscal 2022. It had 1,761 branches across 26 states and 7 union territories in India, with 88% presence across tier III to tier VI cities as on March 31, 2026.

 

Between April 2005 and March 2010, the company operated as a joint venture between DBS Bank and the Murugappa group. In March 2010, DBS Bank sold its 37.5% stake to the Murugappa group. CIFCL exited the unsecured personal loan segment in October 2008 and subsequently the asset management business. The Murugappa group holds 49.25% equity stake in CIFCL, of which 43.76% is held by Cholamandalam Financial Holdings Limited, a group company.

 

CICFL has two subsidiaries: Cholamandalam Securities Limited and Cholamandalam Home Finance Limited, a joint venture with Payswiff Technologies Private Limited and three associates: White Data Systems India Private Limited, Vishvakarma Payments Private Limited and Paytail Commerce Private Limited.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

The key points on the role of the servicer covered as part of the transaction documents are as below:

  • The Trustee acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint, the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the Receivables into the Collection and Payment Account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors), as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.

Performance of outstanding rated transactions

Crisil Ratings has ratings outstanding on instruments issued under 11 securitisation transactions backed by CIFCL-originated loans. Crisil Ratings is receiving monthly performance reports pertaining to these transactions. The cumulative collection efficiency in the underlying pools for these transactions was 98–99% as of June 2026 payouts, with 90+ delinquency remaining at or below 2.5% of the initial pool principal.
