Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has upgraded its rating on the bank facilities and debt instruments of Navi Finserv Limited (NFL) to ‘Crisil A+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ from ‘Crisil A/Stable/Crisil A1’. Crisil Ratings has also assigned its ‘Crisil A+/Stable’ rating to Rs 500 crore non convertible debentures of the NFL.

 

Crisil Ratings has also withdrawn its rating on non-convertible debentures (NCDs) aggregating to Rs 118.29 crore since the outstanding against the same was nil. Crisil Ratings has received independent confirmation that these instruments are fully redeemed. This is in line with Crisil Ratings' withdrawal policy.

 

The ratings upgrade follows strong growth in the assets under management (AUM) of the company alongside improving asset quality and profitability. The ratings also continue to factor in healthy capitalisation and diversified resource profile.

 

The company’s technology-led origination model, anchored by the Navi application and its payments franchise, enabled strong growth in fiscal 2026 and the first quarter of fiscal 2027, with  AUM increasing to Rs 23,440 crore as on June 30, 2026, from Rs 19,740 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 11,505 crore as on March 31, 2025. Digital personal loans continue to be the focus segment accounting for ~93% of AUM as on June 30, 2026, with the  rest comprising home loans and loans against property (LAP). This growth has been underpinned by customer acquisition through the Navi app, which has seen a steady expansion in its unified payments interface (UPI) user base, resulting in Navi Ltd (parent entity, Navi) becoming the fourth largest player in the segment by transaction count.

 

Amid strong business growth, the company has continued to improve its asset quality and profitability metrics. Adjusted 90+ days past due (dpd; including trailing 12-month write-offs) improved to 3.2% as of June 30, 2026, from 3.9% as of March 31, 2026, and 6.9% as of March 31, 2025. Quarterly vintages originated from H2 FY2025 have also exhibited improved credit performance, with peak 90+ delinquencies moderating to below 3.0% of disbursements. This trend is corroborated by sustained improvement in collection efficiencies and moderation in bounce rates.

 

Profitability has also improved, with the company reporting a standalone profit after tax (PAT) of Rs. 199 crore in the quarter ended June 2026, as compared to Rs. 292 crore for full year fiscal 2026. The improvement in earnings is driven by higher net interest margins, better operating leverage from strong growth in disbursements and improving credit costs. Consequently, profitability has been sequentially improving quarter-on-quarter with return on managed assets (RoMA) increasing to 3.2% in the first quarter of this fiscal compared to 2.5% in the preceding quarter and 1.6% for fiscal 2026.

 

Capitalisation remains healthy, supported by regular capital infusion as well as internal accruals. Consolidated net worth improved to Rs 3,501 crore as on March 31, 2026 compared to Rs 2,972 crore as on March 31, 2025. A further capital raise is expected from the parent, Navi, of which ~Rs 600 crore is expected to be infused into NFL, with Rs 300 crore already infused as of date. Gearing and capital adequacy ratio stood at 3.3 times and 23.8% respectively as on March 31, 2026, with gearing expected to be sustained below 4 times on a steady state basis.

 

The resource profile is fairly diversified across instrument and lender type, and the cost of borrowing has been improving. Of the total external outstanding borrowing of Rs 13,802 crore as on August 31, 2026, ~49% was in the form of NCD, ~33% of term loans, 5% commercial paper, 5% pass-through certificates, 4% sub-debt and ~2% in working capital demand loan (WC/DL).

 

These strengths are partially offset by the inherent vulnerability in asset quality given the predominantly unsecured nature of the loan book. Furthermore, any material adverse impact on the business risk profile on account of regulatory changes will remain monitorable.

Analytical Approach

For arriving at the ratings, Crisil Ratings has consolidated the business and financial risk profile of NFL and its 100% subsidiary NFPL.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Healthy capitalisation

Mr. Sachin Bansal, Chairman of NFL, holds ~98% stake in Navi, which holds 93.4% stake in NFL as on June 30, 2026 (on a fully diluted basis). Most of Navi’s consolidated net worth has been deployed in NFL as equity and convertible debt. Net worth improved to Rs 3,501 crore as on March 31, 2026 compared to Rs 2,972 crore as on March 31, 2025 driven  by capital infusion during fiscal 2026, coupled with healthy internal accruals. A further capital raise is expected from Navi, of which ~Rs 600 crore is expected to be infused into NFL, with Rs 300 crore already infused as of date. NFL’s gearing and capital adequacy ratio also remained comfortable at 3.3 times and 23.8% respectively as on March 31, 2026. Crisil Ratings believes NFL’s capitalisation will remain healthy over the medium term. The gearing is expected to be sustained below 4 times on a steady state basis over the medium term.

 

Scalable business model aided by technology-based sourcing and assessment

The company has shown healthy growth with AUM increasing to Rs 23,440 crore as on June 30, 2026, from Rs 19,740 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 11,505 crore as on March 31, 2025.

 

The company’s unsecured personal loan business is supported by a fully digital, end-to-end operating model, encompassing customer acquisition, underwriting, and disbursement. Lead sourcing is primarily through the Navi app along with other digital channels. Increasingly new customer acquisition is through the Navi app and its growing UPI customer base. The App ecosystem is expected to be a strong growth driver going forward. Furthermore, the largely automated and digitised risk assessment process, with limited manual intervention, supports faster turnaround times and scalability. Hence, the healthy growth in AUM is expected to sustain over the medium term. 

 

Improving earnings profile

The company reported a steady improvement in profitability wherein, standalone profit was Rs 199 crore in the quarter ended June 2026 as compared to Rs 135 crore in the preceding quarter and Rs 292 crore for fiscal 2026. This translated into a RoMA of 3.2% for the quarter ended June 2026, and 2.5% in the preceding quarter compared to 1.6% in fiscal 2026. On a consolidated basis, the company reported a PAT of Rs 93 crore in fiscal 2026. With change in the corporate structure, the technology and allied services are housed in NFL’s 100% subsidiary NFPL. While NFPL’s reported net losses of only Rs 37 crore in fiscal 2026, the moderation at consolidated level is arising on account of difference in accounting treatment of income and expenses between NFL (NBFC) and NFPL (Lending Service Provider).

 

At NFL level, the total income (net of interest expenses, as a percentage of average managed assets) improved to 9.2% (annualised) for the quarter ended June 2026 compared to 8.3% (annualised) for the preceding quarter and 7.6% for fiscal 2026. This improvement was driven by revision in the pricing policy which came into effect in FY26. The weighted average rate of interest (ROI) on the personal loan disbursements has moved up from ~20.5% in April 2025 to ~24.3% in August 2026, in addition to a processing fee which has also been introduced. Profitability is further supported by improving operating leverage on the back of strong growth in disbursements, wherein, operating expenses (as a percentage of average managed assets) improved to 2.8% for the quarter ended June 2026, from 2.9% in the preceding quarter and 3.2% for fiscal 2026. Credit costs improved and remained stable at 2.2% for the quarter ended June 2026,  2.0% for the preceding quarter and 2.2% in fiscal 2026 as against 4.2% in fiscal 2025.

 

Diversified resource profile

NFL’s resource profile is fairly diversified across product and lender type. The lender base has expanded with more banks and other financial institutions coming onboard. While the funding base was skewed towards debt from Navi in the past, the share has declined significantly in favour of increasing term funding and capital market issuances. Of the total external outstanding borrowing of Rs 13,802 crore as on August 31, 2026, ~49% was in the form of NCD, ~33% was term loans, 5% commercial paper, 5% pass-through certificates, 4% sub-debt and ~2% in WC/DL. During the quarter ended June 2026, NFL raised ~Rs 3,020 crore at average cost of borrowing of ~10.4%.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Susceptibility of asset quality given unsecured nature of loans 

The company offers digital personal loans which comprised ~93% of the AUM as on June 30, 2026, wherein the borrower profile remains vulnerable to slippage due to inherent risks. Remaining ~7% of the AUM constitutes home loans and LAP.

 

NFL’s gross non-performing assets (GNPA) and net non-performing assets (NNPA) improved to 1.3% and 0.2% respectively as on March 31, 2026 as against 2.5% and 0.4%, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. As on June 30, 2026 the ratios remained fairly stable at 1.38% and 0.2% respectively. The adjusted 90+ dpd (including trailing 12 months’ write-offs) stood improved to 3.2% as on June 30, 2026, from 3.9% as on March 31, 2026, improving from 6.9% as on March 31, 2025.

However, given the strong AUM growth, the company’s ability to sustain comfortable asset quality as portfolio grows and seasons remains a key monitorable.

Liquidity Strong

Asset-liability maturity profile was comfortable as on June 30, 2026, with positive cumulative mismatches across all buckets. As on August 31, 2026, Navi had ~Rs 2547 crore of unencumbered cash and liquid investments, which was sufficient to cover debt obligations and operating expense commitments for over 2 months.

Outlook: Stable

Crisil Ratings believes NFL’s capital position will remain healthy in relation to the scale and the nature of its operations.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upside factors

●        Sustained improvement in profitability with RoMA maintained at above 3.0%

●        Sustenance of asset quality metrics with increase in scale of operations

●        Diversification in business mix with increasing share of secured asset classes

 

Downward Factors

●       Any change in the stance of support by Navi to NFL, potentially leading to capital position being weaker than estimated; significant and sustained rise in the gearing for NFL to beyond 3.5-4 times

●        Sustained deterioration in asset quality and profitability.

About the Company

NFL is a non-deposit taking, systemically important non-banking financial company registered with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and is a wholly owned subsidiary of Navi. NFL obtained a certificate of registration in March 2016 from the RBI. NFL offers lending products such as personal loans and home loans under the Navi brand. Mr Sachin Bansal, the Chairman of NFL, holds about 98% stake in Navi as on June 30, 2026.

 

In fiscal 2026, NFL reported PAT of Rs 93 crore in fiscal 2026 and total income of Rs 2697 crore as compared to Rs 172 crore and total income of Rs 2,299 crore in fiscal 2025.
