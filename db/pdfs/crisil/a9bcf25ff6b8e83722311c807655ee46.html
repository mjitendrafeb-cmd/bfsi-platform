Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil BB/Stable' rating on the long-term bank facility of D.S.Integrated FinSec Private Limited (DSIFPL).

 

The company mainly operates in Gujarat through four branches in Rajkot, Jamnagar, Jetpur, and Ahmedabad, and has recently entered Maharashtra by setting up two branches in Mumbai. The company serves over 2,500 customers as on date and offers micro business loans, Suchit loans#, gold loans, Dr. Flexi loans*, and bill discounting products. While the recent branch additions may support business growth and geographic diversification, their contribution remains to be demonstrated.

 

The scale of operations remains modest, as reflected by assets under management (AUM) of ~Rs 37.13 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs 35.27 crore as on March 31, 2026). The portfolio is moderately diversified across products, comprising micro business loans (39% of AUM), unsecured business/personal loans (33%), gold loans (20%), Suchit loans (4%), and balance in bill discounting and other exposure (3%). Disbursements stood at Rs 7.75 crore for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, as against Rs 28.68 crore in fiscal 2026.

 

The rating factors in the company’s adequate capital position for its present scale of operations. However, the rating remains constrained by the modest scale of operations and earnings profile and moderate asset quality indicators.

 

#A type of loan against property with imperfect collateral, where the property is built on land previously governed by the Urban Land Ceiling and Regulation Act (ULCRA).

 

*a short-term financing facility extended against receivables from health insurance claims that are under processing.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has combined the credit risk profiles of DSIFPL and its associates, Samvedna Builders Pvt Ltd (Samvedna) and GSD Trading and Fin Services Pvt Ltd (GSD). DSIFPL holds 37.06% stake in Samvedna and 20.42% stake in GSD.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Adequate capital position:

Given its small scale of operations, the company’s capital position remains adequate. Networth improved to Rs 23.78 crore as on June 30, 2026, from Rs 23.47 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 22.58 crore as on March 31, 2025, supported by steady profit accretion. On-book gearing was low at 0.67 time as on June 30, 2026. The company has reported an average profit of around Rs 79 lakh over the past three fiscals. Capital adequacy ratio (CAR) remained comfortable at 65.50% as on June 30, 2026, significantly above the regulatory requirement. Further, the company is in the process of raising Rs 100 crore through a proposed initial public offer (IPO), targeted for 2027, which should strengthen the capital base and support growth plans, with the requisite regulatory approvals under process. Nevertheless, the ability to augment capital in line with business growth on a timely basis will remain monitorable.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Moderate scale of operations and geographical concentration:

Considering the product offerings and geographical concentration, the loan portfolio was modest at Rs 37.13 crore as on June 30, 2026. The AUM stood at Rs 35.27 crore in fiscal 2026 and Rs 27.47 crore in fiscal 2025. The company operates mainly from Gujarat, but has recently forayed into Maharashtra through establishment of two branches in Mumbai. The company initially offered a range of products, including vehicle loans and corporate business loans, but following the lockdown, it has shifted focus to small business loans, which are now a key focus area. Despite the gradual scaling up of the portfolio, the business profile remains constrained by geographic concentration, with the benefits of the recent expansion into Maharashtra yet to materialise.

 

Modest asset quality and earnings profile:

Asset quality remains modest, with the gross non-performing  assets (GNPA) ratio increasing to 3.07% as on June 30, 2026 from 2.58% as on March 31, 2026, and 1.32% as on March 31, 2025, reflecting the seasoning of the micro business loan portfolio.

 

As on June 30, 2026, delinquencies in the 30+ and 90+ days past due (dpd) categories stood at 4.3% and 3.1%, respectively, compared with 3.6% and 2.6% as on March 31, 2026, reflecting moderation in asset quality metrics. The average collection efficiency, measured as current and overdue (OD) collections relative to current billings, improved to ~102% in fiscal 2026, from ~95% in fiscal 2025. Nevertheless, the metric masks the underlying collection pressures. Moreover, average collection efficiency, measured as current and overdue collections relative to total current and overdue billings, weakened to a low of ~58% in fiscal 2026, from 62% in fiscal 2025, indicating continued stress in recoveries and overdue collections, largely on account of seasoning and relatively weaker collection performance of the micro business loan portfolio.

 

The core earnings profile remains modest. This is primarily due to elevated operating expenses and borrowing, even as yields remain steady and credit cost remains under control.

 

Given the vulnerable borrower segment, the company has been able to risk-price its loan assets, as reflected in yields of ~16.2% on average AUM over the last two fiscals. Against this, the borrowing cost was ~12% of average borrowings during the same period. Other income has grown from 3.23% in fiscal 2024, to 4.01% in fiscal 2026 and 2.01% in June 2026, mainly aided by recovery of bad debts. Operating expenses remain elevated at 11.69% of average managed assets (AMA) as on June 30, 2026, as compared to 12.34% in fiscal 2026 and 10.55% in fiscal 2025. Higher operating expenses have been incurred to meet advertising expenses, technological maintenance and due diligence cost.

 

Credit cost rose to 1.51% in fiscal 2026, from 0.91% in fiscal 2025, and further to 3.22% for the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Higher credit cost could be attributed to a write-off of a legacy micro business loan portfolio. Overall, the company reported profit of Rs 29 lakh for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, with a return on managed assets (RoMA) of 2.8%, vis-à-vis Rs 89 lakh and 2.4%, respectively, in fiscal 2026, and Rs 75 lakh and 2.41%, respectively, in fiscal 2025.

 

Core profitability will improve as the loan book scales up and the company derives benefits of operating leverage. Also, as the portfolio seasons, the ability to manage credit cost will be closely monitored.

Liquidity Adequate

The company has adequate liquidity with cash and cash equivalents at Rs 5.21 crore as on July 31, 2026. Against the same, the company has debt repayments including operating expenses of Rs 4.67 crore for the next 5 months ending December 2026.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believes DSIFPL will continue to benefit from its adequate capitalisation metrics.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factors:

  • Ability to significantly scale-up loan book with material and sustained improvement in asset quality
  • Improvement in profitability with control over operational and credit cost thereby RoMA remaining above 3% on continuous basis
  • Substantial improvement in the resource profile with reduction in cost of borrowing

 

Downward factors:

  • Deterioration in asset quality leading to an increase in credit costs and weakening of earnings profile
  • Inability to raise equity capital leading to gearing remaining at elevated level of over 6 times.

About the Company

DSIFPL is a non-deposit taking NBFC registered with the Reserve Bank of India. Established in July 1996 in Ahmedabad, the company was taken over in February 2008 and is now headquartered in Rajkot, Gujarat. Their core focus is on meeting the working capital and business credit needs of MSMEs.,. The company has a total of 43 employees.

 

The company operates primarily in Gujarat through four branches located in Rajkot, Jamnagar, Jetpur, and Ahmedabad, with a recent foray into Maharashtra through the establishment of two branches in Mumbai through a network of 150+ direct sourcing agents (DSA) agents with an AUM of Rs 37.13 crore and over 2,500 customers.

 

Rajesh J. Daftary, 51, is the promoter-Director of the company, with over a decade of experience in busines and real estate and overseas overall operations at DSIFPL.
