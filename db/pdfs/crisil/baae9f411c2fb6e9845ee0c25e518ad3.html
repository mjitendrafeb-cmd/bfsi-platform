Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has placed its rating on the long-term debt instruments of IDBI Bank Limited (IDBI Bank) on ‘Rating Watch with Positive Implications’ and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil A1+’ rating on the certificate of deposit programme.

 

Crisil Ratings has also withdrawn its ratings on Rs 5 crore of Omni Bonds on redemption. The withdrawal is in line with Crisil Ratings policy for withdrawal of ratings.

 

The rating action follows the announcement made by IDBI Bank on October 14, 2026, that the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) has approved Fairfax Financial Holdings Limited (Fairfax; rated 'A-/Stable' by S&P Global Ratings [S&P Global]), as the strategic buyer for acquisition of a 60.72% stake in the bank.

 

Under the proposed transaction, Fairfax will acquire a 60.72% stake in IDBI Bank from the Government of India (GoI; 30.48%) and Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC; 30.24%) at a price of Rs 81 per share, for an aggregate consideration of around Rs 52,878 crore. Upon completion of the transaction, Fairfax will become the majority shareholder of the bank, while the shareholding of GoI and LIC will reduce to around 15% and 19%, respectively. Crisil Ratings believes that IDBI Bank is likely to benefit from Fairfax's majority ownership and strategic commitment as the majority shareholder. Fairfax has announced its intention to make IDBI Bank a leading global Indian bank providing diversified financial services. To achieve this objective, Fairfax proposes to contribute its interests in Go Digit General Insurance Limited, Go Digit Life Insurance Limited and IIFL Capital Services Limited to the bank. These proposed transactions, which will be at their respective market prices, are subject to regulatory approvals.

 

Over the past few years, IDBI Bank has demonstrated a sustained improvement in its standalone credit profile, reflected in improved asset quality, healthy profitability and comfortable capitalisation profile. The proposed acquisition has the potential to further strengthen the bank's business profile by providing access to Fairfax's broader financial services ecosystem, including insurance and wealth management businesses. The transaction is likely to support the bank’s long-term growth prospects, with business diversification. Crisil Ratings notes that, in line with applicable regulatory guidelines on promoter shareholding or voting rights in banks, Fairfax has been guided by the regulator to gradually reduce its shareholding to 40% by 10 years and to 26% by 15 years post the acquisition.

 

The successful consummation of this transaction remains subject to requisite approvals and Crisil Ratings will continue to track the progress of the same and have management discussions to understand Fairfax’s support stance, commitment, strategic importance and long-term business and financial strategy for the bank. The rating watch will be resolved once all requisite regulatory approvals are in place, and greater clarity emerges on the said aspects. 

 

The overall ratings also factor in the steady improvement in asset quality of the bank, its healthy profitability, stable and healthy deposit base and sustenance of healthy capitalisation. These strengths are partially offset by moderate overall market share.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has considered the standalone business and financial risk profile of IDBI Bank. Post consummation of the transaction, Crisil Ratings is likely to factor in benefit from Fairfax's majority ownership and strategic commitment as the majority shareholder.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Strong capitalisation and healthy deposit profile

The bank remains well-capitalised, marked by Tier-I and overall capital adequacy ratio (CAR; under Basel III) of 26.6% and 27.1%, respectively, as on September 30, 2026, compared to 25.6% and 26.7% as on March 31, 2026, and 23.5% and 25.1% on March 31, 2025. Networth improved to Rs 71,551 crore as on September 30, 2026, from Rs 67,638 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 60,251 crore as on March 31, 2025, backed by healthy accrual. Further, networth coverage for net non-performing assets (NPAs) was comfortable at ~155 times as on September 30, 2026. Capitalisation of the bank is expected to continue to remain strong and is expected to receive need-based support from GoI and LIC till the proposed transaction is consummated.

 

Total deposits for the bank stood at Rs 3,55,684 crore as on September 30, 2026, as against Rs 3,47,163 crore as on March 31, 2026, and Rs 3,10,294 crore as on March 31, 2025. The share of current account savings account (CASA) deposits was 43.82% as on September 30, 2026, which reduced from 44.59% as on March 31, 2026, and 46.56% as on March 31, 2025. While the share of CASA deposits has declined, it continues to be higher than the banking sector average. The bank’s fixed deposit base is also granular in nature with around 50% of fixed deposits under ticket size of Rs 1 crore as on September 30, 2026.

 

Improving earnings profile

The earnings profile of the bank has been on an improving trend. The bank reported net profit of Rs 9,513 crore and return on average total assets (RoA) of 2.2% in fiscal 2026, as against Rs 7,515 crore and 1.9%, respectively, in fiscal 2025. Further, net profit and RoA for the first half of fiscal 2027 stood at Rs 4,321 crore and 1.8%, respectively.

 

Improvement in RoA in fiscal 2026 is attributed to factors such as reduction in credit cost, as well as controlled operating expenses further supported by recoveries from written-off accounts. Credit cost (as a percentage of average total assets) stood at -0.2% for fiscal 2026, as against 0.1% for fiscal 2025. Operating expenses (as a percentage of average total assets) was stable at 2.1% (annualised) for fiscal 2026, against 2.2% for fiscal 2025. However, the core net interest margin (excluding one-time impact of income tax refunds, and interest income from NPAs and technical write-off accounts), moderated to 3.3% in fiscal 2026, from 3.7% in fiscal 2025. Moderation in RoA in the first half of fiscal 2027 was majorly due to fall in other income (as a percentage of average total assets) to 1.0% (annualised), from 1.5% in fiscal 2026, which benefit from one-time gain from sale of investments.

 

Crisil Ratings expects the bank’s earnings profile should sustain at healthier levels, driven by controlled credit cost. The same will continue to be monitored.

 

Improving asset quality metrics

The bank’s gross and net NPA ratios have improved consistently over the past few years and stood at 2.1% and 0.2%, respectively, as on September 30, 2026, as against 2.3% and 0.2% as on March 31, 2026, and 3.0% and 0.2% as on March 31, 2025. Improvement in NPA is majorly due to lower slippages and write-offs. Slippages (as a percentage of opening gross advances) stood at 0.63% (annualised) in the first half of fiscal 2027, as against 0.76% in fiscal 2026 and 0.98% in fiscal 2025. The total Special Mention Account (SMA) 1 and 2 accounts for the bank stood at Rs 2,521 crore as on September 30, 2026, around 0.91% of total gross advances. Nevertheless, asset quality remains vulnerable to macroeconomic factors and the bank’s ability to improve its asset quality while scaling up the loan book, will be monitorable.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Moderate overall market position

Gross advances of the bank have grown at a healthy pace, registering a compounded annual growth rate (CAGR) of around 15% over the past three fiscals, increasing to Rs 2,59,274 crore as on March 31, 2026, and further to Rs 2,76,932 crore as on September 30, 2026. During this period, the bank maintained its share of the overall banking system advances at around 1.2%. However, the same has come down from levels seen earlier; 1.3% as on March 31, 2023, and 1.7% as on March 31, 2020.

 

As on September 30, 2026, retail advances constituted 69% of the overall loan book, with the remaining 31% comprising wholesale advances. Within the retail portfolio, home loans accounted for 28% of overall advances, followed by loans against property (8%), micro, small and medium enterprise (MSME) loans (9%), gold loans (9%), agricultural loans (5%) and other retail loans (10%).

 

The bank's ability to sustain healthy growth and scale up across segments will remain a key monitorable.

Liquidity Strong

IDBI Bank’s liquidity coverage ratio stood at 123.43% as on September 30, 2026. The bank's liquidity also benefits from access to systemic sources of funds, such as the liquidity adjustment facility from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and access to the call money market.

ESG Profile

Crisil Ratings believes the environment, social, and governance (ESG) profile of IDBI Bank supports its credit risk profile.

 

The ESG profile of financial institutions typically factors in governance as a key differentiator between them. The sector has a reasonable social impact because of its substantial employee and customer base, and it can play a key role in promoting financial inclusion. While the sector has no direct adverse environmental impact, lending decisions may have a bearing on the environment and other sustainability related factors.

 

IDBI Bank has an evolving focus on strengthening various aspects of its ESG profile.

 

IDBI Bank’s key ESG highlights: 

  • IDBI’s scope 1 and 2 emissions and energy consumption intensities stand at ~3 tCO2e and ~4.4 MWh per employee, respectively, in fiscal 2026.
  • Its attrition rate at ~5% and gender diversity at ~36% female employees are better compared with its peers.
  • IDBI’s customer complaint intensity at ~0.26 complaints per Rs crore of loans and advances is lower compared with its peers and it has declined by ~9% CAGR between the fiscal 2024 and 2026.
  • The bank’s governance structure is characterised by ~55% of its board comprising of independent directors, presence of one women director, dedicated investor grievance redressal system and extensive financial disclosures.

 

There is growing importance of ESG among investors and lenders. IDBI Bank’s commitment to ESG will play a key role in enhancing stakeholder confidence, given presence of foreign investors

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • Sustained improvement in earnings profile with ROA sustaining above 2.0% on a steady state basis while growing the book
  • Increase in scale of operations with diversity and higher market share
  • Benefits from Fairfax's majority ownership and strategic commitment as the majority shareholder

 

Downward factors

  • Deterioration in asset quality due to higher slippages and net NPA ratio rising above 5%

About the bank

Industrial Development Bank of India Ltd (IDBI) was constituted by GoI under the Industrial Development Bank of India Act, 1964, and was reconstituted as a banking company on October 1, 2004, to undertake commercial banking and development banking activities. The erstwhile IDBI Bank, IDBI’s subsidiary, was merged with IDBI in 2005. In 2006, IDBI acquired United Western Bank. In 2008, it got its present name.

 

The bank reported net profit of Rs 4,321 crore on total income (net of interest expense) of Rs 10,098 crore in the first half of fiscal 2027, against Rs 9,513 crore and Rs 20,258 crore respectively for fiscal 2026.

Note on tier-II instruments (under Basel III)

The distinguishing feature of tier-II capital instruments under Basel III is the existence of the point of non-viability (PoNV) trigger, which may result in loss of principal to investors, and hence, to default on the instrument by the issuer. As per the Basel III guidelines, the PoNV trigger will be determined by RBI. Crisil Ratings believes that the PoNV trigger is a remote possibility in the Indian context, given the robust regulatory and supervisory framework, and the systemic importance of the banking sector. The inherent risks associated with the PoNV feature have, nevertheless, been adequately factored into the rating on the instrument.

 

Note on hybrid instruments (under Basel II)

Given that hybrid capital instruments (tier-I perpetual bonds and upper tier-II bonds under Basel II) have characteristics that set them apart from lower Tier-II bonds (under Basel II), the ratings on the two instruments may not necessarily be identical. The factors that could trigger a default for hybrid instruments include: the bank breaching the regulatory minimum capital requirement, or the regulator’s denial of permission to the bank to make payments of interest and principal if it reports losses. Hence, the transition from one rating category to another may be significantly sharper for these instruments than in the case of lower Tier-II bonds; this is because debt servicing on hybrid instruments is far more sensitive to the bank’s overall capital adequacy levels and profitability.
