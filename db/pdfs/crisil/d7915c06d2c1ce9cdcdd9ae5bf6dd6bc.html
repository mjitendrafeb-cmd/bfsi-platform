Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable’ rating to Rs 700 crore non convertible debentures of Kisetsu Saison Finance (India) Private Limited (Credit Saison India) and has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the existing bank facilities and debt instruments.

 

The ratings continue to reflect the strategic importance of Credit Saison India to, and expectation of strong financial support from, its parent, Credit Saison Co. Limited, Japan (Credit Saison), and the strong moral obligation of the latter to support the Indian subsidiary. The ratings also factor in the strong capitalisation metrics of the company. These strengths are partially offset by limited seasoning of the portfolio and modest earnings profile.

 

Credit Saison is a flagship entity headquartered in Japan, operating multiple business segments, including payments (credit card services), finance and lending, leasing, real estate, and global services and businesses through various entities, herein referred to as Credit Saison group.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has factored in likely support from Credit Saison Co. Ltd, Japan (Credit Saison Group), given the strategic importance of the company and the strong moral obligation to support the same, given the ownership, shared brand and strong operational integration.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Strategic importance to, and expectation of strong financial support from, the parent, Credit Saison Co. Ltd., Japan 

The Credit Saison group has been in the consumer finance business for over 7 decades, primarily offering credit cards and retail finance products. It has been in the credit card business since inception, with finance and other businesses being added to its portfolio since 2001. Given the track record of operations, Credit Saison is amongst the top credit card companies  in Japan and also offers credit cards in alliance with leading businesses across different industries. The group currently has around ~ 31 million cardholders under its portfolio. It currently operates in six verticals – payments, finance, leasing, real estate, entertainment and global. In order to ensure strong growth, the group has been expanding its operations globally and over the past seven years, it has established a presence in seven countries through its subsidiaries and affiliates. With consistent efforts towards growth via segmental and geographical diversification, the group has been able to reach an asset size (total receivables outstanding) of Rs 2,28,053 crore1 as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 2,06,095 crore2 as on March 31, 2025), at a consolidated level.

 

The group has witnessed sustainable improvement in its capital and earnings profile, having consistently generated strong shareholder equity levels in the last 5 years, with the same remaining above 15% across years. Shareholder equity of the group was 15.40% as on March 31, 2026 against 15.1% as on March 31, 2025 (16.3% in March 2024). Additionally, capitalisation metrics are comfortable, with networth of Rs 45,757 crore1 as on March 31, 2026 and Rs 40,985 crore2 as on March 31, 2025, aided by sufficient internal accrual for the past several years. For fiscal 2026, the group reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 3,692 crore1  as and Rs 3,839 crore2 during fiscal 2025.

 

The group plans to invest heavily towards geographical expansion, specifically in emerging markets, and aims to transform itself into a comprehensive life services group. In line with the overall strategy, India is one of the important markets where the group plans to scale up its business with a focus on consumer and MSME segments. Though Indian operations have commenced only from 2019, the group has already infused equity capital of Rs 2,828 crore, of which Rs 150 crore was infused in September 2025 and Rs 650 crore in March 2026. Support from the parent is also visible in arranging debt funding to Indian operations via common Japanese bank relationships.

 

Further, the group maintains strong oversight on Indian operations, having deployed its senior management personnel on to the board of Credit Saison India. The board is controlled by the parent with Mr Kosuke Mori as common board directors, and Mr. Masaki Negishi, Mr. Kazuo Koshi as and Mr. Koji Sugahara as a director. Ms. Presha Paragash, WTD & CEO of Credit Saison India, has been inducted as an Executive Officer of Credit Saison Japan, reflecting direct executive representation for the India business. Risk management policies, systems and processes used by Credit Saison India are centrally approved by the parent.

 

Additionally, in March 2024, Credit Saison India received equity of ~Rs 1,200 crore from Mizuho Bank, Japan and one of its subsidiaries, following which stake of the Credit Saison group came down to 83.65%. Nevertheless, the parent will retain a majority shareholding and exercise complete management control over the company. Shared brand and complete management control enhance the expectation of support from Credit Saison group, if needed. Any material disruption in Indian operations could, in Crisil Ratings’ view, have a significant impact on the reputation and franchise of the parent. Any change in the management control by, or expectation of support from, the Credit Saison group will remain a key rating sensitivity factor.

 

Strong capitalisation

Capitalisation metrics of Credit Saison India are strongly supported by regular equity infusions by the parent, as well as internal accruals. Networth stood healthy improving to Rs 5,013 crore and gearing of 4.0 times as on June 30, 2026 from Rs 4,874 crore and gearing of 3.8 times, as on March 31, 2026 (networth of Rs 3,654 crore and gearing of 4.3 times, as on March 31, 2025). The capital adequacy ratio stood at 19.8% as on June 30, 2026. Capitalisation metrics were supported by regular equity infusion of ~Rs 2,828 crore till March 2026 by parent and ~Rs 1,200 crore by Mizuho Bank, Japan and one of its subsidiaries (a part of the Mizuho Financial Group), in March 2024.

 

Capitalisation metrics were further strengthened by recent equity infusion of ~Rs 650 crore in March 2026 by the parent. Given the growth plans, the company will continue to raise funds while scaling up operations. It plans to maintain a steady-state net gearing of below 5 times in the medium term.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Limited seasoning of portfolio

Credit Saison India started its operations in 2019, under two verticals – wholesale lending and protected partnership. Under wholesale lending, the company lends majorly to other NBFCs, focused on consumer and MSME segments, whereas under protected partnership, the company ties up with other NBFCs/fin-tech players to lend to consumers or MSMEs, at an agreed ratio. Since February 2022, the company also started direct lending to MSMEs, through a branch-led business model. The company expanded its product portfolio with the addition of Loan Against Property (LAP) in January 2024 and Affordable Housing Loan in September 2025.

 

Credit Saison India’s loan book (excluding off book) grew 31% year-on-year to Rs 23,609 crore as on June 30, 2026. As on March 31, 2026, the book clocked a 24% growth reaching Rs 21,476 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs 17,345 crore as on March 31, 2025. As on June 30, 2026, of the overall own book, protected partnership constituted 45%, followed by direct lending to SME/ Consumer segment (34%) wholesale portfolio (14%), LAP (7%). Further, the Company had also started Affordable Housing Loan in the second quarter of fiscal 2026, the share of which remains negligible at present.

 

The company reported GNPA (on own book) at 1.13% and 1.27% as on June 30, 2026 and March 31, 2026, respectively. The asset quality metrics in terms of adjusted 90+ dpd (on overall AUM), has inched up to 2.41% as on March 31, 2026 as against 1.75% as on March 31, 2025.  

 

Nevertheless, along with the growing portfolio, Credit Saison India has also set up strong risk management systems and policies, and constantly monitors borrowers and its co-lending /fin-tech partners, right from the stage of screening and selection. While in the wholesale vertical, it hypothecates the receivables, in the protected partnership portfolio, it gets a credit enhancement cover in the form of default-loss guarantee (DLG) from the partner entities.

 

However, with scale up of the less seasoned direct lending book, sustenance of asset quality metrics remains a key monitorable.

 

Modest, albeit improving, earnings profile

The company reported net profit of Rs 122 crore for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 and Rs. 336 crore in fiscal 2026 as against net profit of Rs. 107 crore in fiscal 2025. Consequently, return on managed assets (RoMA) improved to 1.8% (annualised) for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, as compared to 1.5% in fiscal 2026 from 0.6% in fiscal 2025, supported by improving total income. The total income (net of interest expense; as a percentage of average managed assets) improved to 10.2% (annualised) for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 from 8.9% in fiscal 2026 and 10.0% in fiscal 2025 supported by higher interest income. Improvement in profitability in fiscal 2026 was driven by lower credit costs and improving operational efficiency. In fiscal 2026, credit cost improved to 2.4% from 3.9% in fiscal 2025, with the operating expense ratio improving to 4.5% from 5.2% (as a percentage of average managed assets). The moderation in credit costs in fiscal 2026 was primarily driven by the revision in DLG guidelines, which resulted in the reversal of provisions created earlier. For the first quarter of fiscal 2027, credit costs and operating expense ratio (on an annualised basis) stood at 2.8% and 5.0% respectively. In fiscal 2025, ROMA declined primarily on account of higher provisioning requirements due to the reversal of DLG benefits as per regulatory guidance resulting in elevated credit costs.

 

Operating expenses are expected to remain moderate in the medium term, as the company continues to invest more on branch expansions (for the direct lending business), technology, employees and risk management. Nevertheless, sustained improvement in earnings profile, along with scale up in operations, while maintaining profitability and diversifying business segments remains a key sensitivity factor.

Liquidity Superior

Liquidity is marked by positive cumulative mismatches across all buckets in the asset liability statement as on June 30, 2026.  As on June 30, 2026, the company had unencumbered liquidity in the form of cash and bank balances (including liquid investments) of around Rs 1,917 crore and undrawn sanction lines of around Rs 5,251 crore. This is sufficient to meet maturing debt obligations for more than the next 1 month.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings believes Credit Saison India will remain strategically important to, and continue to receive financial, managerial, and operational support from Credit Saison Co. Ltd, Japan (Credit Saison Group).

Rating Sensitivity factors

Downward factors

  • Decline in support from the parent, Credit Saison Group or material change in the shareholding, or any downward revision in view of Crisil Ratings on the credit profile of Credit Saison Group
  • Sustained weakening of asset quality with adjusted 90+ dpd exceeding 4%, leading to a sustained impact on the earnings profile.

About Kisetsu Saison India

Kisetsu Saison India, a subsidiary of Credit Saison Japan, was incorporated in June 2018. The company received its NBFC license in September 2019, and started operations via the partnership-led model. Under this model, the company partners with other NBFCs/fin-tech players to build its retail book, mainly in two verticals – wholesale lending and protected Partnership. In February 2022, the company also started direct lending to MSMEs.  Further, the Company also introduced LAP business in the last quarter of fiscal 2024 and affordable housing loans in second quarter of fiscal 2026.

 

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, the company reported a total income of Rs 1,049 crore and PAT of Rs 122 crore and in fiscal 2026, the company reported a total income of Rs 3,355 crore and PAT of Rs 336 crore.

 

About Credit Saison Co. Ltd.

Credit Saison Japan was founded in 1951. The group offers consumer finance/SME finance products. Till 2001, the group was primarily into the credit card business, but subsequently set up presence across multiple segments such as payments (credit card service), finance/lending, leasing, real estate and global. In 2013, the group started expanding its operations globally and is now present in seven countries through its subsidiaries. At the consolidated level, the group had receivables outstanding of Rs 2,28,053 crore1 and Networth of Rs 45,757 crore1  as on March 31, 2026. For fiscal 2026, the group reported profit after tax (PAT) of Rs 3,692 crore[1] as against Rs 3,839 crore2  during fiscal 2025.
