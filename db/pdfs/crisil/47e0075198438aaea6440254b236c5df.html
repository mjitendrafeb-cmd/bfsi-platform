Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has removed its ratings on the debt instruments and bank facilities of Nido Home Finance Limited (Nido) from ‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications' and has reaffirmed the ratings at ‘Crisil A+/Crisil A1+’ while assigning a ‘Stable’ outlook. 

 

The resolution of the watch and rating action follow the consummation of the transaction announced on September 25, 2026. The first phase of the transaction was completed on September 25, 2026, pursuant to which CA Sardo Investments (part of Carlyle Asia Partners, an affiliate of The Carlyle Group) and Salisbury Investments Private Limited (Salisbury; investment vehicle of Aditya Puri and his family) have committed investments of over Rs 2,000 crore in Nido Home Finance Limited (NIDO) through a combination of secondary share purchase and primary capital infusion.

 

In the first phase, Carlyle has acquired a 45%[1] stake in Nido from the Edelweiss Financial Services Limited (EFSL) group for Rs 580 crore and infused Rs 723 crore of primary capital (including Rs 241 crore of upfront warrant subscription) into Nido. Following the transaction, the EFSL group's shareholding stands at ~40%[2], while Carlyle’s affiliate and Salisbury together hold about ~60% (0.4% held by Salisbury).

 

As a result, Nido has ceased to be a subsidiary of the EFSL group.

 

Nido is expected to receive a further Rs 723 crore upon conversion of warrants over the next 18 months, of which around Rs 241 crore has been paid upfront. Upon conversion, Carlyle's shareholding will increase further to around ~73%.

 

The ratings were placed on 'Watch with Developing Implications' on February 19, 2026, following the announcement of the proposed transaction on February 10, 2026. The watch has now been resolved following receipt of the requisite regulatory and statutory approvals and completion of the first phase of the transaction.

 

Previously, Crisil Ratings assessed Nido as part of the Edelweiss group, reflecting the strong operational, managerial and financial linkages among the group entities.

 

Following the change in ownership, the ratings are now based on NIDO's standalone credit profile, while also factoring in the expectation of support from Carlyle (rated ‘A-/Stable by S&P Global), the company's largest shareholder, both on an ongoing basis and in the event of distress.

 

The expectation of support from Carlyle reflects its significant ownership stake, demonstrated capital commitment and strategic interest in Nido. Carlyle is a global investment firm with assets under management of USD 485 billion as of March 31, 2026. It has prior experience investing in the Indian housing finance sector and was also an investor in the EFSL group from 2011 to 2017. Crisil Ratings expects Carlyle's association to support Nido's growth plans while strengthening governance standards, risk management practices and strategic oversight over the medium term.

 

The ratings also reflect Nido's comfortable capitalisation and the financial flexibility arising from Carlyle's ownership, including the expected infusion of the balance Rs 723 crore over the next 18 months. The ratings are constrained by the company's modest scale of operations, limited portfolio seasoning and subdued earnings profile.

 

Having commenced lending operations in May 2008, Nido operates in the affordable housing and mid ticket housing finance segments, as well as the small- and mid-ticket loans against property (LAP) and construction finance segments., with a network of 68 branches across tier I and tier II towns, primarily in southern and western India. As on June 30, 2026 the company had assets under management (AUM) of Rs 4,503 crore. The portfolio comprised home loans (56%), loan against property (35%) and construction finance (9%).

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has evaluated the standalone credit risk profile of Nido and has factored in the likely support of the majority shareholder, Carlyle.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Expectation of support from the majority shareholder – Carlyle

The ratings factor in the strong institutional backing from Carlyle, the majority shareholder in Nido, with a ~ 60% stake as on September 25, 2026. Carlyle's shareholding is expected to increase to around 73% upon infusion of the balance Rs 723 crore of primary capital over the next 18 months, and it is expected to remain the controlling shareholder over the medium term. The association enhances Nido's financial flexibility through demonstrated capital support and access to additional capital, if required.

 

Carlyle is a global investment firm that deploys private capital across three business segments: Global Private Equity, Global Credit, and Carlyle AlpInvest with an AUM of USD 485 billion as of March 31, 2026.

 

India has a growing importance for Carlyle and the group has been active in the Indian financial services sector over the years. The investment philosophy centers on sectors it understands well, partnering with high-quality shareholder groups and management teams and establishing strong governance structures

 

Nido is a strategically important investment for Carlyle in India. With the induction of Carlyle as a majority shareholder, Nido is expected to receive strong financial, managerial and operational support from it on steady state basis. The benefits of this association shall also accrue in the form of further institutionalisation of governance standards, strengthening of risk management frameworks, support in talent acquisition and constant strategic guidance to support long-term growth of the company.

 

Since Carlyle has the right to appoint majority of board members at Nido, it has appointed four nominee directors on the board, who bring extensive experience in financial services. Beyond these, the board also includes existing directors from the EFSL group and new independent directors that further enhance the pedigree of the board with each having rich experience across audit, technology, financial services and organisational development.

 

Carlyle will nevertheless play a key role in strategic decision making, including fundraising if needed.

 

The various steps taken by the company, implemented with the support of the investor, will enable it to maintain a strong capital position for its business needs, comply with contractual and regulatory requirements, and meet its debt obligations on time.

 

Comfortable capitalisation, supported by capital infusion 

Capitalization remains comfortable with networth at Rs 849 crore as on June 30, 2026, compared to Rs 853 crore as on March 31, 2026 (Rs 828 crores as on March 31, 2025). Primary infusion of Rs 1446 crore from Carlyle out of which Rs 723 crore has been received, has further strengthened the capital. Remaining funds will be received within 18 months, upon exercise of share warrants.

 

Gearing was stable at 3.7 times as on June 30, 2026, and March 31, 2026.Overall capital adequacy ratio stood at 30.1% as on June 30, 2026, as compared to 28.8% as on March 31, 2026, well above the regulatory requirement, which will support increasing scale of operations.

 

Key rating drivers - Weaknesses 

Modest scale of operations with limited portfolio seasoning

The company operates at a modest scale compared to the overall industry, with AUM of Rs 4,503 crore[1] as on June 30, 2026, representing a flat book   compared to Rs 4,469 crore as of March 31, 2026. Gross loan book stood at Rs 3,458 crore as on June 30, 2026 as compared to Rs 3,505 crore as on March 31, 2026.

 

Disbursements have improved over the past two fiscals, increasing to Rs 1,856 crore in fiscal 2026 and Rs 1,415 crore in fiscal 2025 from Rs 1,119 crore and Rs 816 crore in fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023, respectively. Disbursements stood at Rs 182 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.

 

Despite the improvement in disbursements, AUM growth has remained subdued due to elevated balance transfers and prepayments, reflecting the company's high dependence on the direct selling agent (DSA) channel for sourcing.

 

Balance transfer outflows increased to Rs 353 crore in fiscal 2026 from Rs 260 crore in fiscal 2025. Going forward, the company intends to increase the share of self-sourced business to mitigate this risk and support sustainable growth.

 

Although Nido commenced operations in May 2008, the portfolio remains relatively unseasoned as disbursement volumes have accelerated only in recent years. Consequently, asset quality metrics are yet to fully stabilise. While delinquencies have witnessed some moderation, they remain at manageable levels, with gross non-performing assets (GNPA) and net non-performing assets (NNPA) at 2.9% and 2.4%, respectively, as on June 30, 2026, compared with 2.3% and 1.9% respectively as on March 31, 2026.

 

The company's ability to maintain asset quality while scaling up operations will remain a key monitorable.

 

Subdued earnings profile

The company has a subdued earnings profile with compressed net interest margins (NIMs), elevated operating expenses and some rise in credit costs.

 

NIM to average managed assets (AMA) was reported at 2.1% in fiscal 2026 as compared to 2.2% in fiscal 2025.

 

Operating expenses remain a drag on profitability at 4.0% of AMA for fiscal 2026 as compared to 3.7% for the previous fiscal mainly due to investment in technology and manpower addition. Credit costs have remained low but witnessed increase to 0.4% in fiscal 2026 from 0.2% in fiscal 2025, with portfolio seasoning.

 

Core profitability remains subdued, with reported profitability supported by other income, particularly income from direct assignment (DA) transactions, apart from fee income and profit on sale of investments. Other income to AMA was reported at 2.9% in fiscal 2026 as compared to 2.2% in fiscal 2025. During fiscal 2026, DA income was reported at Rs 62 crore against Rs 24 crore in fiscal 2025.

 

Accordingly, profit after tax (PAT) was modest at Rs 23 crore for fiscal 2026 with return on managed assets (RoMA) at 0.5%, compared to Rs 19 crore and 0.4%, respectively for the previous fiscal.

 

The company reported a loss of Rs 4.5 crore in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 as against a profit of Rs 3.6 crore in the corresponding period of the previous fiscal. This was due to lower total income at Rs 134 crore as compared to Rs 146 crore in the previous period on account of reduction in colending volumes as well as sell down/DA.

 

Further, credit cost saw an uptick to 0.5% (annualized of managed assets) from 0.3%. Nido expects to improve portfolio yields through increased focus on higher-yielding products such as small-ticket LAP, while continuing to focus on the affordable housing segment.

 

With the expected gradual scale-up in the loan book, improvement in incremental cost of borrowing and the company’s focus on maximizing branch level productivity, the earnings profile is expected to improve. Ability of the management to achieve this while maintaining asset quality will remain a monitorable.

[1] AUM including IndAS adjustments stood at Rs 4,900 crore as on June 30, 2026 and Rs 4,906 crore as on March 31, 2026

Liquidity Adequate

The asset liability management profile showed positive cumulative mismatches in the up-to-one-year bucket as on March 31, 2026 (including sanctioned and documented unutilised bank lines). As on September 28, 2026, the company had liquidity of cash and cash equivalents of Rs 832 crore and unutilised cash credit/working capital bank lines of Rs 7 crore. Against this, the company has repayments of Rs 194 crore over the next four  months till January 31, 2027.

Outlook Stable

Crisil Ratings expect Nido to maintain adequate capitalisation, increase growth momentum and benefit from support from its majority shareholder, Carlyle.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors

  • Substantial and sustained improvement in the market position while maintaining asset quality
  • Improvement in the earnings profile with core return on managed assets of over 1.5% on a steady state basis
  • Increase in benefits accruing from association with the Carlyle Group

 

Downward factors

  • Downgrade in the credit risk profile of Carlyle and/or material change in the shareholding or support philosophy, impacting the quantum and timing of expected support
  • Deterioration in asset quality with gross NPA increasing to above 5%, over an extended period, thereby also impacting profitability.

About the Company

Nido is a housing finance company registered with National Housing Bank (NHB) and was incorporated in May 30, 2008. The company offers home loans, LAP and construction finance loans.

 

As on June 30, 2026, the company has an AUM of Rs 4,503 crore with presence in Maharashtra (33% of AUM), Gujarat (16%), Tamil Nadu (12%), Telangana (9%), Karnataka (9%), Delhi (6%), Andhra Pradesh (4%), Madhya Pradesh (3%) and Chandigarh (2%).

 

The ticket size of loans varies from Rs 5 lakh to over Rs 10 crore for the company with tenures of upto 30 years

 

Nido reported a PAT of Rs 23 crore on a total income (net of interest expense) of Rs 245 crore in fiscal 2026 as against PAT of Rs 19 crore on a total income of Rs 200 crore in previous fiscal.  

 

In the quarter ended June 30, 2026, NHFL reported a loss of Rs 4.5 crore on a total income of Rs 50 crore as against a PAT of Rs 3.6 crore on a total income of Rs 60 crore in quarter ended June 30, 2025.
