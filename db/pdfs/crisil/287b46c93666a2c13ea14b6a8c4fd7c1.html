Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Provisional Crisil A+ (SO)’ rating to Series A1 pass-through certificates (PTC) issued by Felix 2W 2026 under a securitisation transaction originated by Manba Finance Limited (Manba), backed by a pool of two-wheeler loan receivables.

 

The ratings are based on the credit support available to the PTCs, the credit quality of the underlying pool receivables, origination and servicing capabilities of Manba, and soundness of the transaction’s legal structure.

 

The transaction has a Par with Excess Interest Spread (EIS) with trigger-based turbo structure. The EIS will be utilised to accelerate the principal redemption of Series A1 PTCs (till they are outstanding), if the portfolio at risk (PAR) 90+ of the pool exceeds 5.0% of the initial pool principal.

Payment structure

The transaction has a Par with monthly subordinated EIS structure. The trust settled by Catalyst Trusteeship Limited will issue Series A1 PTCs in exchange for purchase consideration equal to 93.0% of the pool principal at the time of securitisation. Series A1 PTC holders are promised timely interest payments on a monthly basis. Principal repayment, while expected on a monthly basis, is promised only on ultimate basis by the instrument’s final maturity date.

Adequacy of credit enhancement

The investor payouts for Series A1 PTC are supported by cash collateral and subordination of EIS. On a monthly basis, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised interest payments to Series A1 PTCs in case of shortfall in collection from the pool. On the Series A1 PTC final maturity date, the cash collateral can be used to make the promised principal repayment in case of shortfall in collection from the pool. All prepayment collections will be utilised for redemption of the Series A1 PTCs.

 

Credit enhancement available in the transaction structure to support promised PTC payouts:

 

  • External credit enhancement from cash collateral of Rs 2.57 crore (8.0% of the pool principal) which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank and lien-marked in favour of the trustee.
  • Scheduled cash flow subordination (internal credit enhancement assuming zero prepayment) aggregating Rs 5.96 crore (18.5% of pool principal) for Series A1 PTCs – including overcollateralisation tranche amounting to Rs 2.25 crore (7.0% of the pool principal).

 

Based on the Crisil Ratings assessment, the total credit enhancement available in the transaction (internal – in the form of EIS, overcollateral and unrated equity tranche; and external – in the form of cash collateral) provide loss absorption against stressed shortfalls in the pool, commensurate with the rating assigned to the PTCs.

Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description

Strengths:

Credit support available in the structure

  • External credit enhancement from cash collateral of Rs 2.57 crore (8.0% of the securitised pool principal) which is expected to be maintained as fixed deposits with a bank and lien-marked in favour of the trustee.
  • Scheduled cash flow subordination aggregating to Rs 5.96 crore (18.5% of the pool principal, assuming zero prepayment) for Series A1 PTCs – including subordination of over collateralisation principal of Rs 2.25 crore (7.0% of the pool principal).

 

Seasoning of contracts in the pool

  • The pool has a weighted average seasoning of 6.5 months and pre-securitisation amortisation of 19.5%.
  • All the contracts in the underlying loan pool are current as of the cut-off date (September 5, 2026).

 

Weaknesses:

  • High risk profile of the underlying asset class - The pool is backed by new two-wheeler loans, an asset class which has historically exhibited higher delinquency.
  • Exposure to geographical concentration risk as 34.8% of the pool principal comprises contracts originated from Gujarat.

Rating Sensitivity factors

Upward factor

  • Credit enhancement (based on both internal and external credit enhancements) available in the structure exceeding 1.9 times the estimated base case shortfall on the residual cash flow of the pool.

 

Downward factors

  • Credit collateral (internal and external combined) falling below 1.75 times the estimated base case loss
  • Sharp downgrade in the rating of the servicer/originator
  • Non-adherence to the key transaction terms envisaged at the time of the rating

Liquidity Strong
Liquidity is strong given that the credit enhancement available in the structure is sufficient to cover loss exceeding 1.5 times the base case shortfall in the pool.

Quality of the asset pool and strength of cashflows

The pool cash flow is securitised and comprises receivables from new two-wheeler loans originated by Manba. The pool has a weighted average net seasoning of 6.5 months, weighted average loan-to-value (LTV) of 79.6%, weighted average interest rate of 19.8% and Gujarat accounting for 34.8% of the pool principal. Average ticket size of the pool is Rs 0.84 lakh. All contracts in the pool are current as on the cut-off date.

Rating assumptions

Background:

  • To assess the base case shortfall for the transaction, Crisil Ratings has analysed moving portfolio delinquency and collection efficiency for the two-wheeler loan portfolio provided by Manba till June 2026. The 90+ days past due (dpd) and 0+ dpd on the portfolio were 3.6% and 9.5%, respectively, as of June 2026. Crisil Ratings has factored in pool-specific characteristics and estimated the base case shortfall in the pool by the maturity of the transaction in the range of 8.0% to 10.0% of the cash flow. Crisil Ratings has also factored in the following assumptions on the basis of the typical industry characteristics of the asset class and its criteria for rating asset-backed securitisations.
  • Based on the assessment of the short-term credit risk profile of Manba, Crisil Ratings has factored in the risk of commingling of cash flow.

 

Assumptions:

  • Base case shortfall: After making the adjustments on these factors, the base case shortfall in the pool by maturity of the transaction is in the range of 8.0% to 10.0% of the pool cash flow.
  • Prepayment rate: Monthly prepayment rate of 0.5–1.5% has also been applied to the pool cash flow.

About the company- Originator/Servicer profile

Manba, based in Mumbai is a non-banking finance company (NBFC) registered with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), which provides two-wheeler finance. The company started operations in 1996 as a direct selling agent (DSA) for ICICI Bank for two-wheeler loans till 2008, after which it started lending operations. Manba came out with an initial public offering (IPO) and is now a listed entity. Its shares got listed on September 30, 2024.

 

The originator operates in Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh, and has acquired the preferred financier tag for Suzuki, Yamaha, TVS, Piaggio and Hero MotoCorp.

A summary of key terms of servicer contract

As per indicative transaction terms, the key points on the role of the servicer to be covered as part of the transaction documents are as follows:

  • The trustee, acting for and on behalf of the investors shall appoint, the servicer for the purpose of collecting, receiving and managing payment of the Receivables into the Collection and Payment Account for the purpose of managing, collecting and receiving the receivables, holding the underlying security and carry out other roles and roles and responsibilities as specified under the transaction documents
  • The servicer shall receive servicing fees which shall be paid by the trustee in accordance with the Waterfall Mechanism as per the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall collect the receivables from the underlying borrowers and deposit the collected amounts in the collection and payment account in a timely manner as per the terms of the transaction documents.
  • The servicer shall submit to the trustee all the data and reports in the manner and as per the timelines as specified under the transaction documents.
  • The occurrence of certain events as per the terms of the transaction documents shall be construed as a Servicer Event of Default.

Provision for appointment of back-up servicer

The trustee (acting on the instructions of the investors), as per the terms of the servicer agreement and upon the occurrence of Servicer Event of Default, shall retain the right to appoint an alternate servicer.

Performance of outstanding rated transactions

Crisil Ratings has ratings outstanding on instruments issued under several securitisation transactions backed by two-wheeler receivables originated by Manba. The 90+ dpd in the underlying pools for these transactions have not gone beyond 2.0% of the initial pool principal.

For details on Crisil Ratings' use of 'PP-MLD' please refer to the notes to Rating scale for Debt Instruments and Structured Finance Instruments at the following link: https://www.crisilratings.com/en/home/our-business/ratings/credit-ratings-scale.html
