Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned its ‘Crisil AA+/Stable’ rating to Rs 700 crore non-convertible debentures of Godrej Finance Limited (GFL). Also, Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank facilities and existing debt instruments of GFL.

 

Crisil Ratings has also withdrawn its rating on Rs 200 crore of non-convertible debentures (See ‘Annexure: Details of rating withdrawn’ for details) as the outstanding against the same is nil and on receipt of confirmation from the debenture trustee. The withdrawal is in line with the policy of Crisil Ratings

 

Crisil Ratings has taken note of the proposed reorganisation within the Godrej Capital group, pursuant to which GFL is proposed to be amalgamated with Godrej Capital Limited (GCL). The proposed amalgamation remains subject to the receipt of all requisite statutory, regulatory and other necessary approvals.

 

To arrive at the ratings for GFL and Godrej Housing Finance Limited (GHFL), the existing analytical approach factors in the combined business and financial risk profiles of GCL and its subsidiaries, GHFL and GFL, and also factors in the strong support the Godrej Capital group is likely to receive from its parent, Godrej Industries Limited (GIL), on an ongoing basis and in case of distress.

 

Upon completion of the proposed amalgamation, GFL will cease to exist as a separate legal entity. Consequently, the analytical approach currently applied to GFL will be adopted for the assessment of GCL. However, the existing analytical approach for GHFL will be discontinued. The revised analytical approach will be based on the standalone credit risk profile of GHFL and will factor in the strong support GHFL is likely to receive from its parent, GCL, on an ongoing basis and in case of distress. Nevertheless, Crisil Ratings believes the consummation of the proposed transaction is unlikely to result in any change in the ratings.

 

The existing ratings for GHFL and GFL centrally factor in the expected strong support from its ultimate parent, GIL, both on an ongoing basis and in case of distress. This is on account of the strategic importance of lending businesses to GIL and the moral obligation of the latter to provide support, given the majority shareholding and common brand. The ratings also factor in the comfortable capitalisation and experienced management team of the Godrej capital group. These strengths are partially offset by nascent stage of operations and limited track record of the group in the financial services business.

 

While GHFL is a housing finance company (HFC) of the Godrej group and focuses on home loans, GFL is the non-banking financial company (NBFC) arm of the group and offers business loans, loans against property (LAP) and construction financing and has recently entered supply chain financing and gold loans. Both these entities are ultimately held by GIL through its wholly owned subsidiary, Godrej Investment Limited, which holds 91.1% stake in GCL.

 

GHFL caters to both mid-market and prime housing segments while GFL focuses on small and medium enterprise (SME) loans. GHFL had 63 branches/offices and is expanding its reach to other markets while GFL operated through 93 branches/offices as on June 30, 2026. GHFL and GFL derive synergies in terms of management, systems and treasury.

Analytical Approach

Crisil Ratings has combined the business and financial risk profiles of GCL and its subsidiaries, GHFL and GFL, collectively referred to as the Godrej Capital group, and has factored in the strong linkages with GIL in terms of operations, finance, management and infrastructure, along with a strong common brand. The ratings also factor in the strong support the Godrej Capital group is likely to receive from GIL, on an ongoing basis and in times of distress.

Key Rating Drivers - Strengths

Expectation of strong support from GIL and the Godrej group

The lending business of GIL took off with GHFL. GIL, through its wholly owned subsidiary, Godrej Investment Limited, holds 91.1% stake in GCL, which in turn holds 100% stake in GHFL and GFL. The lending business has been identified as a focus area for the group’s diversification plans.

 

GIL is the flagship company of the Godrej group (promoter and promoter group companies held 74.64% equity in GIL as on June 30, 2026). GIL is expected to maintain minimum shareholding of 51% and retain management control over the Godrej Capital group.

 

The Godrej Capital group benefits from the existing ecosystem of the Godrej group. It also receives managerial and operational support from the group. Pirojsha Godrej, who is on the board of directors in various group companies, is the non-executive director of GHFL and GFL. Furthermore, the common corporate identity and brand have benefited GHFL and GFL in terms of ability to raise funds from lenders at competitive interest rates.

 

Comfortable capitalisation

The Godrej Capital group has access to sufficient capital to scale up the business. The Godrej group had initially committed equity capital of around Rs 1,500 crore to the lending business, which was completely infused. Subsequently, the Godrej group infused equity of Rs 950 crore, Rs 793 crore and Rs 729 crore in fiscals 2024, 2025 and 2026, respectively. Moreover, in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, the Godrej Capital group received additional equity of Rs 283 crore. Resultantly, consolidated adjusted networth increased to Rs 4,422 crore as on June 30, 2026, with gearing of 5.7 times (Rs 4,052 crore and 5.8 times, respectively, as on March 31, 2026). Gearing is expected to remain at 6–6.5 times on a steady-state basis as the business scales up. However, strong capital support should aid growth and help mitigate asset-side risks.

 

Experienced management

The Godrej Capital group is led by Manish Shah (managing director and chief executive officer), who has over two decades of experience in the financial services sector. The leadership across other critical functions is well-qualified with each member having significant experience in retail lending, including housing finance. The management has focused on putting in place adequate systems, processes and policies for scaling up the business.

Key Rating Drivers - Weaknesses

Nascent stage of operations

GHFL and GFL commenced operations in November 2020 and March 2022, respectively, but the consolidated loan book[1] expanded rapidly to Rs 30,053 crore as on June 30, 2026 (Rs 27,832 crore as on March 31, 2026) from Rs 10,761 crore as on March 31, 2024, and Rs 5,253 crore as on March 31, 2023. The growth was primarily driven by GFL, which recorded a robust 3-year CAGR of 140% between March 31, 2023, and March 31, 2026. As on June 30, 2026, the assets under management (AUM) of GFL and GHFL stood at Rs 20,178 crore and Rs 9,875 crore, respectively. The Godrej Capital group is expected to continue witnessing strong growth in its loan book while expanding its geographical presence over the medium term.

 

The asset quality metrics remain controlled given the limited seasoning of the books. As on June 30, 2026, GHFL and GFL had reported gross non-performing assets (GNPAs) of Rs 13.4 crore (0.2%) and Rs 85.3 crore (0.4%), respectively.

 

Given the initial stage of operations and investments towards requisite infrastructure, operating expenses are high, thereby constraining earnings. However, with economies of scale kicking in, the profitability has been improving, as reflected in net profit of Rs 232 crore in fiscal 2026, compared with Rs 152 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 50 crore in fiscal 2024). On a standalone basis, GHFL and GFL reported net profit of Rs 72 crore and Rs 162 crore, respectively, in fiscal 2026, compared with Rs 51 crore and Rs 102 crore, respectively, in fiscal 2025. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, the group reported net profit of Rs 64 crore.

 

The group’s earnings are expected to remain modest over the near term and improve thereafter with improvement in operating efficiency due to continued ramp-up of operations. The ability to scale up business with healthy asset quality and profitability will remain monitorable.

[1] Total loan book includes off-book portfolio

Liquidity Strong

Liquidity of the Godrej Capital group was comfortable with adequate cash and equivalent (Rs 1,895 crore) and unutilised bank lines (Rs 7,930 crore) as on August 31, 2026. Against this, the group has debt obligation of Rs 5,781 crore during September 2026 to November 2026. Liquidity is supported by expectation of continued capital support from GIL as well as good financial flexibility, enhancing the ability to raise funds from diverse lenders at competitive interest rates.

Outlook Stable

The Godrej Capital group will continue to derive financial, managerial and operational support from GIL and maintain comfortable capitalisation.

Rating sensitivity factors

Upward factors:

  • Upward revision in credit view on the ultimate parent by Crisil Ratings
  • Attaining sizeable market share while maintaining healthy asset quality (gross non-performing assets [NPAs] less than 1%) and sustained improvement in earnings

 

Downward factors

  • Reduction in support to the Godrej Capital group from GIL
  • Downward revision in the credit view on the ultimate parent by Crisil Ratings
  • Deterioration in asset quality with gross NPAs exceeding 3%, over an extended period

About the Company

GIL, the flagship entity of the Godrej group, is one of India’s leading manufacturers of oleochemicals and surfactants. GIL, through its subsidiaries, is a leading player in the fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG), real estate and food and agri-sector businesses. GIL, through its wholly owned subsidiary, Godrej Investment Limited holds 91.1% stake in GCL, which in turn holds 100% stake in GHFL and GFL.

 

GHFL received the housing finance licence from the Reserve Bank of India in October 2020. The company is engaged in the mid and premium housing segments and offers home loans; it has recently diversified into the affordable segment. After commencing operations in November 2020, it had AUM of Rs 9,875 crore as on June 30, 2026. GHFL operated through 63 branches/offices as on June 30, 2026. In fiscal 2026, GHFL reported profit of Rs 72 crore on total income (net of interest expenses) of Rs 282 crore, compared with Rs 51 crore and Rs 164 crore, respectively, in fiscal 2025. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, GHFL reported profit of Rs 15 crore on total income (net of interest expenses) of Rs 78 crore, compared with Rs 9 crore and Rs 52 crore, respectively, during the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.

 

GFL, incorporated as Ensemble Holdings and Finance Limited in 1992, held the NBFC licence and was part of the Godrej group. Following a change in shareholding structure, it was brought under GCL with GIL being the ultimate holding parent. The company focuses on non-mortgage loans, including SME loans, LAP, construction financing and commenced lending in March 2022. Recently, it has forayed into supply chain financing and gold loans. The company operated through 93 branches/offices as on June 30, 2026. The AUM grew to Rs 20,178 crore as on June 30, 2026. In fiscal 2026, the company reported profit of Rs 162 crore on total income (net of interest expense) of Rs 846 crore, compared with Rs 102 crore and Rs 479 crore, respectively, in fiscal 2025. For the quarter ended June 30, 2026, GFL reported profit of Rs 50 crore on total income (net of interest expense) of Rs 268 crore, compared with Rs 10 crore and Rs 161 crore, respectively, during the corresponding period of the previous fiscal.
